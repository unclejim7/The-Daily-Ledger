Geopolitics · Diplomacy · Wednesday Edition

Trump Threatens to "Drive Iran Into Hell" at the UN, Then Holds a "Very Good" Three-Hour Meeting With Its Foreign Minister the Same Day

President Trump delivered his most incendiary UN remarks yet Tuesday, asking world leaders whether he should "annihilate the Islamic Republic and do it quickly" or "drive them into hell with no chance of survival." All but one member of Iran's delegation walked out in response; Cuba's delegation followed suit minutes later after Trump separately called the country an "absolutely failed state." It was, by most accounts, one of the more chaotic UN addresses in recent memory.

Yet the same day produced the war's most substantive diplomatic contact to date: U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi for a three-hour session at UN headquarters, with Qatar's prime minister present for most of it. Trump himself called it a "very good meeting," and Iran reportedly used the session to present a road map for a regionwide ceasefire and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Trump predicted publicly that any actual deal wouldn't materialize until "right after the election" in November — a familiar hedge, but one now paired with an unusually concrete description of what a deal might contain.

The whiplash continues today: Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian addresses the UN General Assembly for the first time since the war began, giving Tehran its formal answer to Trump's rhetoric before the same body. Simultaneously, Treasury's promised worldwide shutdown of Iranian airlines takes effect, alongside new sanctions on three foreign banks — the Dubai branch of Banque Misr, Turkey's Golden Global Yatirim Bankasi, and Russia's VTB Bank — as Secretary Bessent warns remaining enablers of an "extinction-level event" for their businesses.

Markets pulled back modestly from Tuesday's records, with defense and chipmaker names leading the slide after Trump said he wouldn't allow defense contractors to pay dividends or buy back stock; the Nasdaq held closest to its high while the Dow and S&P eased further. Bitcoin held near $86,000 as its recent short-squeeze rally cooled, gold stayed roughly steady near $4,340, and the 30-year mortgage rate remained at 6.95% ahead of Thursday's fresh reading.

A Concrete Ask, Finally

A Hormuz Road Map Is More Specific Than Anything Offered Before

Seven months of vague talk about "conditions" and "de-escalation" gives way to something with actual shape — a regionwide ceasefire tied explicitly to reopening Hormuz is the kind of proposal negotiators can actually work from, whether or not it ultimately succeeds.

Iran's Turn at the Microphone

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Concrete Proposals Signal a Conflict Nearing Its Negotiated Phase

Armstrong Economics has argued that wars historically shift from open-ended threats to specific, actionable proposals only once both sides privately conclude further escalation offers diminishing returns. A detailed Hormuz-reopening road map, delivered the same day as the war's most extreme public rhetoric, fits a pattern the site's War Cycle model associates with conflicts entering their eventual negotiated resolution phase — even when public theater suggests otherwise.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Rare TORCH Infections in Pregnancy Linked to Higher Autism and Disability Risk

A major Swedish study of 3.7 million people found that rare TORCH infections — a group of pathogens including toxoplasmosis, rubella, cytomegalovirus, and herpes — passed from a pregnant woman to the fetus are linked to significantly higher risks of autism and intellectual disability later in life. Researchers say the findings reinforce the importance of prenatal screening and early intervention for these infections, even though they remain relatively uncommon in the general population.
