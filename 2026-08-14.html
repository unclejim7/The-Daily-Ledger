Geopolitics · Economy · Friday Edition

Iran Kills, Wounds Tanker Crew in Third ADNOC Attack This Week as Bessent Vows Economic Measures “Never Seen in History”

The UAE said Thursday evening that Iran struck two more tankers affiliated with the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company in "unprovoked attacks" as they transited the Strait of Hormuz, describing the strikes as acts of piracy by Iran's Revolutionary Guard. It's the third such attack on ADNOC shipping this week alone, part of a pattern that has now hit 15 of the company's vessels since the war began — a toll that, across those incidents, has killed one crew member and injured 20 others. Qatar, Kuwait, Egypt, and Bahrain all issued statements Friday condemning the attacks and citing U.N. Security Council Resolution 2817, adopted in March, which explicitly demanded Iran halt strikes on Gulf shipping.

The escalation lands as Washington signals a pivot toward economic rather than purely military pressure. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Newsmax Thursday the U.S. is preparing to "apply measures like have never been seen in the history of economic isolation on a country," with details expected next week as part of what he called a "one-two punch": sweeping new sanctions layered on top of the continued naval blockade. Trump, for his part, dismissed Iran Thursday as "all talk and no action, the bully of the Middle East no longer," while Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi shot back on social media that "worse than fake news is fake intelligence," warning Washington to "be careful" after Trump's repeated claims of "total control" over the strait.

All of it unfolds four days before the 60-day Oman-Iran memorandum of understanding expires Sunday, August 18, with essentially no visible progress this week on the underlying dispute the MoU was meant to help resolve. Markets, still trading on a separate track entirely, closed Thursday with the S&P 500, Dow, and Nasdaq all at records, though Friday's futures were mixed as traders weighed the fresh tanker attacks against the week's run of favorable inflation data. Bitcoin extended its slide to a fourth straight lower session, dropping below $63,000 to around $62,829, while oil pushed higher again on the renewed attack risk premium.

Armstrong Economics has argued for weeks that the war's economic dimension would eventually overtake its purely geopolitical one, and this week supplies two separate confirmations: a 40-year-low U.S. crude stockpile reading Wednesday, and now Bessent's promise of unprecedented economic isolation measures. Whether "never seen in history" proves to be more than rhetoric will be one of next week's central questions, alongside whatever happens — or doesn't — when Sunday's deadline actually arrives.

FDA Approves New mRNA Flu Vaccine for Older Adults Ahead of Flu Season

The FDA has approved a new mRNA-based flu vaccine specifically for older adults ahead of the 2026-2027 flu season, adding an mRNA option to a vaccine category that has historically relied on egg-based and cell-based production methods. Public health officials say the approval could offer a faster-to-update option in years when circulating flu strains shift significantly from what a given season's vaccine was designed to target, though real-world effectiveness data will accumulate over the coming season.
