Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Morning Naps Linked to Higher Mortality Risk Than Afternoon Naps in Older Adults

Researchers from Mass General Brigham and Rush University Medical Center tracked 1,338 older adults for up to nineteen years using wrist-worn activity monitors, publishing their findings in JAMA Network Open. They found that napping in the morning was associated with roughly 30% higher all-cause mortality risk compared with napping in the early afternoon, and that each additional hour of total daily napping was linked to about a 13% higher mortality risk, with each additional daily nap adding roughly 7% more risk. The study is observational and cannot prove naps directly cause earlier death; the researchers suggest certain napping patterns may instead serve as an early signal of underlying illness rather than a cause of harm in their own right. The sample skewed older, white, and Midwestern, so the findings may not generalize to all populations.

Random Fact

Wrist-worn actigraphy devices, used in today's featured napping study, were first developed for sleep research in the 1970s and work by detecting patterns of movement to infer periods of rest versus activity, without requiring a brain-wave sensor.

Word of the Day
actigraphy

(noun, from Latin actus "a doing" + Greek graphein "to write") — a method of monitoring rest and activity cycles using a wearable motion sensor. Today's health item relied on nearly two decades of actigraphy data to track napping patterns in older adults.
