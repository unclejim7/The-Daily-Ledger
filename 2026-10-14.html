Markets · Inflation · Iran · Wednesday Edition

Hot Inflation Report Rattles Markets as Iran's New Hormuz Surveillance Push Sends Oil Flows Into Reverse

The inflation report this edition has flagged for days finally landed Wednesday morning, and it ran hotter than Wall Street wanted. September's Consumer Price Index rose 3.7% year-over-year and 0.6% month-over-month, while core CPI — stripping out food and energy — climbed 2.6% year-over-year and 0.3% month-over-month. Both figures came in above the roughly 3.6% headline and 2.5% core consensus economists had expected, reviving worries that the Federal Reserve has less room to cut rates than markets had been pricing in.

The data landed alongside a notable shift in the Hormuz standoff. Iran's navy announced new surveillance units covering waters "east of the Strait of Hormuz and across the Sea of Oman and the Arabian Sea," saying the goal is "real time surveillance of enemy activities and maritime traffic," and separately warned vessels against switching off their transponders while transiting the region. The announcement follows directly from Tuesday's revelation of the "US-UAE oil shuttle" system, and the pressure appears to be working: Kpler counted 11 tankers struck in or near the strait between September 28 and October 5, UKMTO has logged roughly 9 attacks so far this month — already about half September's full-month total — and Al Jazeera reports that September's oil-flow rebound to 12.8 million barrels per day has begun reversing as the renewed attacks on feeder vessels take a toll. Analysts remain divided on exactly how much total Hormuz-area shipments have fallen as a result.

Markets sold off on the inflation print: the S&P 500 fell 0.84% to 7,742.18, the Dow dropped 0.60% to 51,538.83, and the Nasdaq led declines with a 1.30% slide to 26,892.50. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped to a fresh high near 5.38% as traders priced in a slower pace of Fed easing. Brent crude, meanwhile, jumped toward $109 a barrel, with the hot inflation data and the renewed Hormuz disruption reinforcing each other in a way traders have grown wary of this fall.

Bitcoin slid to roughly $81,800 as the cooling rate-cut outlook weighed on risk assets broadly. The 30-year mortgage rate's last reading holds at 7.40%, with Freddie Mac's next weekly survey due Thursday — a reading that will now have to digest both today's inflation surprise and the fresh jump in long-term yields.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Heart Failure Deaths Among Younger Americans Have Climbed Sharply Since 2001

A new report from the Heart Failure Society of America, published in the Journal of Cardiac Failure, finds that crude heart failure mortality rose 121% among Americans ages 25 to 44 and 96% among those ages 45 to 64 between 2001 and 2020. Hospitalization rates for heart failure have also risen among adults under 65 even as they declined among those 65 and older from 2010 to 2022, and age-adjusted heart failure mortality overall climbed roughly 37% from 2010 to 2023. Heart failure contributed to 452,573 deaths in the US in 2023 alone, underscoring what researchers describe as a shift in the disease's burden toward younger adults.

Random Fact

Core CPI strips out food and energy prices specifically because they are considered too volatile month-to-month to reflect underlying inflation trends — which is exactly why today's core beat, even a modest one, carries more weight with the Fed than headline CPI alone.

Word of the Day
disinflation

(noun, dis- "reversal of" + inflation) — a slowing in the rate of price increases, as distinct from deflation (falling prices outright). Markets had been pricing in continued disinflation; today's print suggests that process has stalled, at least for now.
