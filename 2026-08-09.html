Geopolitics · Sunday Edition

Iran Hardens Hormuz Demands as the U.S. Must “Correct Its Behavior” — Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan Sign a Defense Pact Iranian Clerics Call a Target for “The Roar of Our Missiles”

Iran's Supreme National Security Council said Sunday the Strait of Hormuz will not fully reopen until the United States "corrects its behavior," a notably harder line than the shipping-logistics language Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi used just a day earlier when he called talks with Oman "approaching the final stages." The council tied any full reopening to the broader list of demands Iranian officials have voiced for weeks: an end to the war, a U.S. troop withdrawal from the region, and compensation for what Tehran calls American violations of a June agreement. Araqchi himself was careful to caution that the near-final Oman text "is not a sign of the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz."

The demand landed the same weekend a very different regional realignment took shape. Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement Friday, a trilateral pact under which an armed attack on any one signatory is to be treated as an attack on all three, building on the bilateral Saudi-Pakistan defense agreement from last September. Turkish officials insisted the pact "does not identify Iran, or any other country, as a common threat," but Iranian clerics mocked the signing and promised "the roar of our missiles" would reach its signatories — a threat that reads differently given Pakistan's status as a nuclear-armed state.

Markets are closed for the weekend, leaving Friday's record S&P 500 close and its best week since April as the freshest read on sentiment heading into a new week still dominated by rate-cut optimism. Bitcoin, which trades around the clock, kept climbing regardless — around $65,228 Sunday, a sixth consecutive daily gain that puts the coin within roughly $72 of the $65,300 level that has capped every rally attempt since mid-July.

Armstrong Economics reads both threads as confirmation of a single underlying dynamic: financial markets pricing in resolution even as the region's political structure keeps fracturing into new alignments, with Sunni powers consolidating around Saudi Arabia even as Iran refuses to soften its position. It's a pattern Armstrong's Economic Confidence Model has forecast intensifying through the 2027 Panic Cycle regardless of how any single Hormuz shipping text ultimately reads.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Meeting the Standard Exercise Guideline Offers Only Modest Heart Protection

A large new study finds that the widely recommended 150 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous exercise per week offers only modest protection against heart attack and stroke, with substantially greater cardiovascular benefits requiring something closer to 560 to 610 minutes a week — roughly four times the standard guideline. Researchers cautioned the finding isn't an argument against the current guidelines, which remain a meaningful floor, but a reminder that the dose-response curve for cardiovascular benefit keeps climbing well past the minimum most public health messaging emphasizes.

Random Fact

The Mecca Joint Defence Agreement is the first formal trilateral mutual-defense pact among Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan, but bilateral defense arrangements between Gulf and South Asian states go back decades — Pakistan has quietly guaranteed elements of Saudi security since the 1960s, making this pact less a new relationship than an old one finally put in writing and expanded to include Ankara.

Word of the Day
precondition

(noun, from Latin "prae-," before, + "condicio," agreement) — a condition that must be fulfilled before something else can happen. Iran's Supreme National Security Council spent Sunday converting what had sounded like closing logistics into a fresh list of these, a reminder that "almost done" negotiations can grow new preconditions faster than they resolve old ones.
