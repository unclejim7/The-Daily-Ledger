Geopolitics · Saudi Arabia · Saturday Edition

Houthi Strikes Hit Saudi Defense Ministry and Aramco Facilities as Oil Settles Above $116, Its Highest Since April

The conflict's geography widened dramatically this week. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed four strikes within 48 hours: Saudi Arabia's Defense Ministry headquarters in Riyadh, King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, an Aramco facility in Yanbu, and Abha International Airport, using a combination of ballistic missiles and drones. Saudi aviation authorities confirmed two people were killed in the Dammam airport strike. Saree warned of “major options” if Saudi forces struck Yemeni tribal or civilian gatherings in response.

Saudi Aramco ordered staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters and work remotely following the Dammam attack, and Saudi Arabia's benchmark stock index fell 2.5% on the news. Brent crude settled at $116.72 a barrel Friday, up 3.1% and its highest close since April, with an intraday peak above $117 — a sharp escalation from Thursday's already-elevated $113.17 close, as the conflict's spread beyond the strait itself gave oil markets a second, independent source of upward pressure.

President Trump dismissed Iran's own tanker and mine claims from earlier in the week as fabrication, writing: “They are even making up fake hits, mines, and everything else, because more Oil is coming through the Hormuz Strait now than it did even prior to the beginning of the Conflict.” The remark echoes this edition's earlier reporting on Iranian officials privately describing a strategy of prolonging the crisis to keep oil prices elevated through the November 3 midterms.

Markets are closed for the weekend, leaving Friday's second straight losing session to stand. Fed rate-hike odds for the October 27–28 meeting eased further to roughly 10%, even as oil climbed again — a sign markets still read this week's escalation as a geopolitical shock rather than grounds to revisit the Fed's path. Bitcoin held near $81,500 over the weekend, and the 30-year mortgage holds at 7.38%.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Personalized Brain-Stimulation Treatment Cuts PTSD Symptoms by 52%

A clinical trial combining personalized brain mapping with targeted magnetic stimulation reported a 52% reduction in PTSD symptoms and a 68% remission rate among participants, a notably strong result for a condition that has long resisted consistent treatment outcomes. Researchers say tailoring the stimulation pattern to each patient's individual brain activity, rather than using a one-size-fits-all protocol, appears central to the approach's effectiveness, though larger follow-up trials will be needed to confirm the findings.
