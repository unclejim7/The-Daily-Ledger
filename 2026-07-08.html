Geopolitics · Markets · Wednesday Edition

Trump Declares Iran Ceasefire “Over” After U.S. Strikes 80+ Targets Overnight — Oil Surges 6%, Wall Street Braces for a Red Open

The fragile U.S.-Iran interim agreement effectively collapsed overnight. Speaking Wednesday on the sidelines of the NATO summit in Ankara, President Trump declared the ceasefire “over,” telling reporters “I think it’s over. I don’t want to deal with them anymore,” and calling Iran’s leadership “scum” and “sick people.” The remarks followed a fast-moving 48 hours: Iran’s Revolutionary Guard struck three commercial tankers in the Strait of Hormuz Monday and Tuesday — a Qatari LNG carrier and a Saudi-flagged supertanker among them — prompting the U.S. Treasury to revoke the sanctions waiver that had allowed Iranian oil sales, with any remaining trade required to wind down by July 17. CENTCOM then launched what the Pentagon described as strikes against more than 80 Iranian targets overnight, including dozens of Revolutionary Guard patrol boats near the strait. Iran responded Wednesday morning with ballistic missiles and drone attacks on U.S.-linked sites in Kuwait and Bahrain; Kuwaiti defense officials said all projectiles were intercepted with no casualties or damage.

Trump said he would still allow negotiators to continue talking — envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner remain in contact with Doha-based mediators — but described further engagement as “a waste of time.” Iran’s chief negotiator, parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, fired back that Washington, not Tehran, broke the June 17 memorandum of understanding by reimposing oil sanctions, declaring “the era of bullying and extortion is over. We don’t fold.” NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, standing alongside Trump in Ankara, called the U.S. response “absolutely necessary” and urged a full reopening of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of global oil production normally flows. At least four tankers reportedly reversed course rather than risk the strait Wednesday, and the UN’s shipping agency condemned the tanker attacks as reckless endangerment of “innocent seafarers.”

Markets moved immediately. Oil jumped more than 6% in the biggest single-session spike since June’s worst fighting: WTI crude near $74.80 a barrel and Brent near $78.80, both up sharply from Tuesday’s close as traders rebuild the geopolitical risk premium that had nearly vanished over the past two weeks. U.S. equity futures fell hard — S&P 500 futures down about 0.6%, the Dow off roughly 0.9%, and the Nasdaq down close to 0.8% — unwinding a chunk of Monday’s record close above 53,000. Gold, oddly, slipped rather than rallied, down about 1.2% to roughly $4,078 an ounce as a stronger dollar and higher Treasury yields offset the usual safe-haven bid. The VIX jumped 5% to around 16.4, a real move but still far below the levels seen during June’s worst days of fighting.

All of this unfolds as Iran observes the fifth and sixth days of Ali Khamenei’s state funeral, with negotiations formally paused until mourning concludes Thursday. Today the late Supreme Leader’s body travels to Iraq’s holy cities of Najaf and Karbala — the most symbolically loaded leg of the six-day procession — ahead of Thursday’s burial at the Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad. Markets will get a second major catalyst this afternoon: the Fed releases minutes from its June 16–17 meeting at 2 PM ET, the committee’s only on-record account of a 9-9 split over whether to hike rates in 2026.

Tankers & Sanctions

Three Vessels Struck in Two Days as Treasury Reimposes Iran Oil Sanctions

A Qatari-owned LNG tanker and a Saudi-flagged supertanker were both struck by projectiles Monday in the Strait of Hormuz, with a third commercial vessel hit Tuesday by the Revolutionary Guard. The Treasury Department responded by revoking the waiver that had permitted Iranian oil sales since June’s ceasefire, effective immediately, with any production or delivery of Iranian crude required to wind down by July 17 — nearly six weeks earlier than the original August 21 deadline. Several LNG carriers bound for Qatar’s Ras Laffan facility reversed course rather than risk the strait Wednesday.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

FOMC Minutes — 2 PM ET Today

June Minutes Are the Fed’s Only Real Signal on a 2026 Hike, Because Warsh Won’t Give One

The Fed releases minutes from its June 16–17 meeting at 2 PM ET Wednesday, and they carry unusual weight: Chair Kevin Warsh withheld his own dot from the Summary of Economic Projections, the first chair to do so since the dot plot launched in 2012, leaving the committee transcript as the only substantive on-record account of internal thinking on a possible September hike. Of 18 officials who submitted projections, nine penciled in at least one 2026 hike, eight saw no change, and one projected a cut — effectively a 9-9 split. Warsh’s June 17 statement ran roughly 130 words, about half the length of prior statements, and dropped the “easing bias” language that had persisted through three meetings. At the ECB’s Sintra forum July 1 he again declined to project a path: “I’m not going to make a judgment now.” CME FedWatch currently prices roughly a 25% chance of a July hike and closer to 50% for September, a probability that could move meaningfully depending on how hawkish today’s transcript reads — and how the reimposed Iran oil sanctions feed into the inflation outlook the committee will debate at its July 28–29 meeting.

NATO Summit — Ankara

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, appearing alongside Trump, defended the overnight strikes as a necessary response to a ceasefire violation and pressed for unimpeded freedom of navigation through Hormuz, calling it “crucial for all 32 allies.” The EU’s foreign affairs chief, Kaja Kallas, separately called Iran’s attacks on Bahrain and Kuwait “unacceptable” and said Tehran had violated the memorandum it signed in June.

30-Year Rate Holds Near 6.54% as Freddie Mac’s Weekly Survey Sits at a Seven-Week Low of 6.43%

Bankrate’s daily 30-year fixed mortgage rate stood at 6.54% Wednesday, unchanged over the past week, while Optimal Blue data pegged the conforming 30-year at 6.474% and Zillow-sourced figures for the prior day ran higher, near 6.635% — the usual spread reflecting differing methodologies across surveys. Most housing economists still expect the 30-year fixed to hold above 6% through year-end regardless of the Fed’s next move.

Tax & Accounting

Two Days Left: COVID-Era Penalty Refund Claims Under the Kwong Decision Are Due Friday, July 10

The window to claim refunds or abatements of penalties and interest the IRS assessed during the roughly 3.5-year COVID-19 disaster period narrows to two days. Individuals, small businesses, estates, and trusts must file Form 843, Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement, by July 10 to preserve their rights following Kwong v. United States, which challenges the IRS’s authority to assess those charges. Practitioners continue advising clients with any uncertainty about eligibility to file protective claims now rather than risk missing Friday’s cutoff.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

As Heat and Humidity Climb Back In, Doctors Flag Extra Risk for Anyone on GLP-1 Medications

With Chicagoland's humidity building again this week after a brief break, public health officials are renewing warnings about heat-related illness risk for people taking GLP-1 medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide. These drugs can blunt thirst signals and, through appetite suppression, lead some users to under-hydrate without realizing it — a combination that raises the risk of dehydration and heat exhaustion on hot, humid days. The guidance is straightforward: set a regular water-drinking schedule rather than relying on thirst cues, watch for early warning signs like dizziness or unusually dark urine, and move strenuous outdoor activity to the cooler morning or evening hours as the mercury climbs into the high 80s over the coming days.

Random Fact

The Strait of Hormuz is only 21 miles wide at its narrowest point, with shipping lanes on each side just two miles across — separated by a two-mile buffer zone. Through that sliver of water passes roughly 20% of the world's oil and a comparable share of global liquefied natural gas, making it, by tanker volume, the single most consequential chokepoint in the global energy system. No alternate route exists that doesn't add thousands of miles and weeks of transit time, which is exactly why a handful of tanker strikes can move oil prices 6% before breakfast.

Word of the Day
rescind

(verb, 17th century, from Latin rescindere, “to cut off, abrogate”) — to formally cancel or revoke an agreement, law, or order. The U.S. Treasury rescinded its waiver on Iranian oil sales Tuesday, undoing in a single afternoon a permission it had granted only weeks earlier — a reminder that in this conflict, concessions have proven far easier to rescind than to rebuild.
