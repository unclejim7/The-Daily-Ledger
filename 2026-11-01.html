Markets · Midterms · Sunday Edition

Two Days From the Midterms, Markets Enter the Final Weekend Still Reeling From Friday's Selloff

Markets are closed for the weekend after Friday's brutal session left the Nasdaq down 2.86% on the week and the S&P and Dow both solidly lower, with Intel's historic 29% plunge still the single biggest driver. With the midterms now just two days away, JPMorgan strategists argue the election itself is unlikely to be the market's main risk factor this week, writing that the midterm effect on markets is "likely to be relatively limited" given expectations of continued gridlock either way — AI-related volatility, in their view, remains the bigger threat heading into November.

The Strait of Hormuz situation hasn't budged over the weekend: Brent crude is holding near $117 a barrel, with no fresh incident reported since Friday's fourth-tanker claim but also no de-escalation. Saudi Arabia has now gone six days without confirming or denying the original Houthi claim of a strike on the Ras Tanura refinery, a silence that has outlasted nearly every other thread of this week's news cycle.

Bitcoin edged up slightly to roughly $77,300, holding just above Friday's six-week low as the crypto market continues to digest the Binance hack. The 30-year mortgage holds at 7.34%, with Thursday's Freddie Mac reading now the next scheduled data point of the week.

On the lighter side: the Bears head to Seattle for Monday Night Football, with the Seahawks favored by 4.5 points, and Milwaukee still hasn't set a date for its long-awaited championship celebration.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Certain Hormonal Birth Control Methods Linked to a Small Increase in Brain Tumor Risk

A large Danish case-control study published in JAMA Network Open, covering roughly 3 million women, found that use of certain progestogen-based hormonal contraceptives was associated with a higher risk of meningioma, a usually benign brain tumor. The strongest association was with medroxyprogesterone injections; low-dose levonorgestrel IUDs showed no elevated risk. Researchers note the absolute risk increase remains small in real-world terms, and the findings are not a reason for most women to change their contraceptive choice without first discussing it with a doctor.
