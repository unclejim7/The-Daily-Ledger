Markets · Federal Reserve · Sunday Edition

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh Faces His First Make-or-Break Rate Decision as Markets Brace for a Pivotal Week

Markets remain closed on Sunday, leaving Friday's close to stand — the S&P 500 at 7,612.47 and the Nasdaq at 26,078.60, both logging Wall Street's worst week since late June, with the Dow at 51,168.63 still up 0.7% for the week. The numbers now sit in the rearview mirror of a far bigger question: whether Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, installed in May after Jerome Powell's term ended, will preside over a rate hike at his first major decision on October 27–28. Odds have moved to roughly a coin flip since Wednesday's hotter-than-expected inflation report, a genuinely open outcome that would have been unthinkable as recently as two weeks ago.

The Hormuz situation holds at the same elevated plateau it's occupied for days: no single new incident was reported Sunday, but oil remains near its highest levels in months following a stretch that saw at least 18 attacks on shipping in and around the strait since the start of October, per maritime trackers. Iran's forces have continued ordering inbound tankers to turn back near the strait's narrowest points, with vessels generally complying rather than testing the warning.

Bitcoin, which trades through the weekend, ticked up again to roughly $80,600, extending Saturday's modest stabilization. The 30-year mortgage rate holds at 7.46% heading into Thursday's next weekly reading. In Chicago, another mild, mostly sunny fall day continues the weekend's run of pleasant weather, a quiet backdrop for what's shaping up to be a loud afternoon in sports.

The Chicago Bears kick off at Atlanta at 1 p.m. ET today, looking to bounce back from last week's lopsided loss at Green Bay. A few hours later, if Saturday's Game 4 in Cleveland didn't settle the ALCS outright, a decisive Game 5 follows at 5 p.m. ET with a World Series berth against the Milwaukee Brewers on the line — Milwaukee having already swept the Dodgers in the NLCS to reach its first pennant since 1982.

Researchers Study New Concussion Treatment Options

Researchers at the University of Regina are examining new approaches to treating concussions, part of a broader push in sports medicine to move beyond rest-and-wait protocols toward more active intervention in the days following a head injury. Full details of the study's methodology and findings have not been widely published yet, but researchers say the work reflects a growing consensus that earlier, targeted treatment may improve recovery outcomes compared to traditional approaches.
