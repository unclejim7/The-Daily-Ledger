Real Estate · Markets · Thursday Edition

Freddie Mac's New Reading Arrives as Analysts Warn an Uncalled House Complicates the Post-Midterm Rally Thesis

The 30-year mortgage ticked down to 7.31% in today's new Freddie Mac weekly reading, a modest move consistent with the broader market's recent drift. More notable is a point several analysts have raised this week: the historical tendency for stocks to rally strongly in the year following a midterm election has typically depended on a clear outcome being known relatively quickly — a condition this cycle hasn't met, with House control still undetermined two days after polls closed.

Markets edged higher again regardless, with the S&P 500 rising 0.1% to 7,655.65, the Dow adding a similar amount, and the Nasdaq gaining 0.2%. Brent crude ticked up toward $118 a barrel after Houthi forces claimed retaliatory strikes following Wednesday's Saudi counterstrikes in Yemen, extending a conflict that has now shifted into a visible back-and-forth exchange rather than one-sided claims.

Bitcoin extended its climb to roughly $79,100, a sixth consecutive day of gains. Tonight, the Blackhawks host Florida at the United Center, and the Bears, sitting at 6-2, prepare to host Tampa Bay on Sunday at Soldier Field.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Heavy Drinking Linked to Sharply Higher Mortality, With a Different Pattern for Wine

A UK Biobank study of more than 340,000 adults followed for roughly 13 years found heavy drinkers had 24% higher all-cause mortality, 36% higher cancer mortality, and 14% higher cardiovascular mortality than rare or non-drinkers, regardless of what they drank. At low-to-moderate intake, though, the pattern diverged by beverage: spirits, beer, and cider were linked to somewhat higher mortality risk, while moderate wine consumption was associated with lower cardiovascular death risk, possibly tied to polyphenols or other lifestyle factors common among moderate wine drinkers. Researchers caution the study is observational and relied on self-reported baseline drinking habits.
