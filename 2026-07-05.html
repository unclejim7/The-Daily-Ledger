Bitcoin · Geopolitics · Sunday Edition

Bitcoin Reclaims $63,000 as Khamenei's Funeral Enters a Second Day and Wall Street Waits Out a Quiet Weekend

Bitcoin pushed back above $63,000 overnight, its highest level in more than a month, as a stretch of thin holiday-weekend trading lets a modest but meaningful shift in flows do the talking. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs snapped a brutal ten-day, $4.5 billion outflow streak on Thursday with a $221.7 million net inflow, and CryptoQuant data shows whales have accumulated more than $16.7 billion of Bitcoin over the past two weeks even as retail sentiment stayed stuck in Extreme Fear. The move caps a wild stretch that took Bitcoin from over $93,000 at the start of the year to a 21-month low near $58,278 on Wednesday, and now back above $63,000 in barely 96 hours — a reminder that in a market this thin, conviction from a small number of large holders can move price further and faster than headlines alone would suggest.

Wall Street itself is dark until Monday, leaving Thursday's record Dow close of 52,900.07 as the last print anyone can trade against. The Dow's third record close of the week came alongside a soft June jobs report — just 57,000 positions added against 113,000 expected — that gave markets a reason to keep betting the Fed stays patient, even as Chair Kevin Warsh warned attendees at the European Central Bank's Sintra forum that "we've seen that prices are too high." September hike odds sit in the 65-70% range heading into a data-light three weeks before the July 28-29 FOMC meeting.

In Tehran, the second day of Ali Khamenei's six-day state funeral drew large crowds back to the Grand Mosalla on Sunday for funeral prayers for the assassinated Supreme Leader and four family members killed alongside him in February's opening strike of the Iran war. Mourners chanted vows of unity and revenge as the procession schedule moves toward Monday's main march through central Tehran and, eventually, burial in Mashhad on July 9. Indirect U.S.-Iran talks in Doha remain paused for funeral week, with mediators saying discussions will resume "at the earliest possible time" once the ceremonies conclude.

Locally, the holiday weekend's rain-soaked sports slate reaches its rubber games today: the Cubs try to avoid a series sweep against the Cardinals at Wrigley, and the White Sox head into Cleveland's Star-Spangled Sunday finale in a virtual tie for the AL Central lead.

Technical Picture — Sunday

From a 21-Month Low to a Month-High in Four Days — Whale Accumulation Meets a Seasonally Strong July

Bitcoin's round trip this week has been remarkable even by its own standards: a 21-month low near $58,278 on Wednesday, followed by a climb back above $63,000 by Sunday morning, a gain of roughly 8% in under a week. CryptoQuant data shows whales accumulating more than $16.7 billion of Bitcoin over the past two weeks even as retail-driven ETF flows stayed negative until Thursday's reversal — a divergence that has historically preceded local bottoms. July has averaged a 7.25% gain over the past 13 years. Citigroup's cut of its 12-month Bitcoin target to $82,000 from $112,000 last week is a reminder that sentiment remains fragile heading into the July 28-29 FOMC meeting, and Peter Schiff continues to warn that a break below the low-$50,000s would open the door to a deeper capitulation.

Geopolitics

Iran — State Funeral, Day Two

Funeral Prayers Held for Khamenei and Slain Family Members as Tehran's Streets Fill for a Second Day

Thousands of mourners dressed in black returned to Tehran's Imam Khomeini Grand Mosalla on Sunday for the second day of public ceremonies honoring assassinated Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, with funeral prayers offered for him and four family members killed in the same February 28 strike. "This is by itself the revenge," one mourner, Sara, 45, told CNN, describing the scale of the crowds as its own form of defiance. Monday brings the funeral's main event: a roughly six-mile procession from Imam Hossein Square to Azadi Square beginning at 6 a.m. local time, before the body continues on to Qom, Najaf, Karbala, and finally burial at the Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad on July 9. Khamenei's son and successor, Mojtaba, elected Supreme Leader on March 8, has yet to make a public appearance since being wounded in the same strike that killed his father.

Iran — Doha Track

U.S.-Iran Talks Stay Paused for Funeral Week After Mediators Reported "Positive Progress"

Qatari and Pakistani mediators say this week's indirect U.S.-Iran discussions in Doha made positive progress on issues tied to the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding before both delegations agreed to pause until funeral proceedings conclude. Unresolved disputes include Iran's reported plan to impose tolls on vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz starting in mid-August and the unfreezing of Iranian assets abroad. President Trump said at Mount Rushmore Friday that Iran is "dying to settle," adding of the funeral pause: "We gave them a week off for a funeral, because we're nice."

Sunday Rate Check

30-Year Rate Holds Near 6.39% APR as Freddie Mac's Weekly Survey Sits at a Seven-Week Low

NerdWallet's daily 30-year fixed rate held essentially steady at 6.39% APR Sunday morning, little changed from Saturday and still 14 basis points above where it stood a week ago. Freddie Mac's more closely watched weekly survey, released Thursday, put the 30-year fixed at 6.43% for the week ending July 2 — a seven-week low, down from 6.49% the prior week. "With rates at a seven-week low and purchase demand continuing to edge higher, it's an encouraging sign as prospective homebuyers respond to modest improvements in affordability," Freddie Mac said in its release. Bankrate's separate survey runs somewhat higher, at 6.54%, reflecting methodology differences across the major rate trackers.

Tax & Accounting

Five Days Left: COVID-Era Penalty Refund Claims Under the Kwong Decision Are Due July 10

The National Taxpayer Advocate's deadline for claiming refunds or abatements of penalties and interest the IRS assessed during the roughly 3.5-year COVID-19 disaster period is now five days away. Relief is not automatic: individuals, small businesses, estates, and trusts must file Form 843, Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement, by July 10 to preserve their rights following the Kwong v. United States decision, which calls into question the IRS's authority to assess those charges. Taxpayers with unresolved uncertainty are encouraged to file protective claims in the meantime.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Chicago Caught a Break This Weekend — Much of the Country Didn't, and the Danger Isn't Over

While Chicagoland's heat backed off into the low 80s this weekend, a stubborn heat dome continues to bake more than 200 million Americans elsewhere, with overnight lows failing to drop below 75-80°F in many dense urban areas — a pattern physicians say is often more dangerous than the daytime peak itself, since the body never gets a real chance to recover before the next day's heat begins building on top of it. Cumulative heat stress of this kind is linked to elevated rates of heart attack, stroke, and kidney injury, particularly among older adults, outdoor workers, and anyone without reliable air conditioning. The practical guidance holds regardless of geography: hydrate on a schedule rather than waiting for thirst, move strenuous activity to the earliest or latest hours of the day, and treat a first evening of poor sleep in the heat as a warning sign rather than something to push through. Cooling centers remain open in many of the hardest-hit cities for anyone who needs a few hours of real relief.

Random Fact

Today's "Star-Spangled Sunday" — all 15 MLB games starting within minutes of each other on July 5 — is a tradition MLB began in 2022 to create a single, day-long national baseball event around the Fourth of July weekend. Every team plays, every game is available nationally on some combination of NBC, Peacock, and NBCSN, and this year's slate includes the White Sox and Guardians playing for a share of first place in the AL Central at the same moment the Cubs and Cardinals decide their own series at Wrigley.

Word of the Day
quiescence

(noun, from Latin quiescere, "to become quiet") — a state of stillness, dormancy, or inactivity, often understood as temporary rather than permanent. Today's markets, closed until Monday, and Iran's paused diplomacy, deliberately suspended for a funeral, are both examples of quiescence rather than resolution — a pause in motion, not an end to it.
