Trump Rejects Iran's Seven-Day Ceasefire Plan, WSJ Reports, and Expects to Resume Bombing After the Midterms

President Trump has rejected Iran's proposal for a seven-day ceasefire and told aides he expects to resume bombing after November's midterm elections, the Wall Street Journal reported Friday, citing U.S. officials. Iran's proposal would have reopened the Strait of Hormuz and restarted nuclear talks in exchange for Washington lifting its naval blockade of Iranian ports — the same terms Foreign Minister Araghchi had presented to envoy Steve Witkoff earlier in the week, alongside demands for the release of frozen Iranian assets and an end to fighting across all "resistance" fronts.

Trump's skepticism is reportedly rooted less in the ceasefire terms themselves than in doubts Iran would actually honor promised limits on its nuclear program; the report also notes Washington wants to preserve its dwindling munitions stockpile for other potential contingencies rather than commit further resources to this theater. The Journal notes his position could still change in the coming weeks, leaving the door only partially closed.

Iran's own internal signals remain genuinely mixed: President Pezeshkian said Friday that Tehran was prepared to negotiate limits on its nuclear program within the NPT framework, with inspection agreements reachable through talks — a real concession on paper. But a hardline Iranian lawmaker pushed back immediately, calling 60% uranium enrichment a "fundamental national right" that Tehran would not abandon under any circumstances, underscoring how far apart Iran's own factions remain on what's actually negotiable.

Markets shrugged off the geopolitical uncertainty entirely, closing out a volatile week with a strong Friday session: the Dow surged 478.64 points (+0.93%) to 51,828.62, the S&P 500 gained 0.51% to 7,743.41, and the Nasdaq added 0.5% to 27,068.72, ending the week higher despite the sharp Treasury yield spike. Bitcoin pulled back to around $81,250 from Thursday's levels while gold held near $4,321, and the 30-year mortgage rate remained at 7.03% with the next Freddie Mac reading due Thursday.

Reading Between the Lines

Preserving Munitions Is Its Own Kind of Signal

Washington's stated desire to conserve munitions for "other contingencies" suggests strategic planning well beyond this single theater, and implicitly acknowledges that a full resumption of major combat operations carries real opportunity costs elsewhere.

The Same Sticking Point, Again

Enrichment Remains the Conflict's Most Durable Obstacle

Nearly every other issue in this war — shipping, sanctions, prisoner and asset questions — has shown at least some movement over seven months; uranium enrichment has not, and today's hardliner pushback shows why that particular line may be the hardest to ever cross.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: A Post-Midterm Timeline Confirms Both Sides Are Playing for Position, Not Resolution

Armstrong Economics has argued that explicitly tying military and diplomatic decisions to an election calendar, as both Washington and Tehran are now doing, reveals that near-term posturing rather than genuine urgency is driving current decision-making on both sides. The site's War Cycle model suggests real movement toward resolution is unlikely before November, with the intervening weeks likely to be dominated by exactly this kind of proposal-and-rejection cycle rather than any breakthrough.

Brain Scans Show Long COVID May Damage Dopamine-Releasing Neurons

New brain-imaging research found evidence that long COVID may damage neurons responsible for releasing dopamine, potentially explaining persistent symptoms like fatigue, low motivation, slowed movement, and memory difficulties that many long-COVID patients report. Researchers say the findings offer one of the first concrete biological mechanisms linking long COVID to its most common and stubborn symptoms, opening the door to more targeted treatment approaches going forward.
