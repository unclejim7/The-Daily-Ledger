Geopolitics · Markets · Monday Edition

Tehran's Biggest Day Yet: Khamenei's Main Funeral Procession Draws Immense Crowds as Wall Street Reopens to a Tech Rebound

The largest single day of Ali Khamenei's six-day state funeral is unfolding in Tehran this morning, as the flag-draped coffins of the assassinated Supreme Leader and four family members killed alongside him move by truck from Imam Hossein Square toward Azadi Square, trailed by crowds that state broadcaster IRIB and independent observers describe as larger than the estimated one million who turned out for Qassem Soleimani's 2020 procession. Mourners dressed in black have chanted for vengeance against President Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu throughout the morning, with at least one effigy of Trump hanged along the route. Authorities, wary of repeating the deadly crowd crush that marred Ayatollah Khomeini's 1989 funeral, have shut down streets, airspace, and daily life across the capital. The procession is the centerpiece of a route that continues to Qom on Tuesday and Iraq's holy cities of Najaf and Karbala on Wednesday, before Khamenei is buried at the Imam Reza shrine in his native Mashhad on Thursday.

Indirect U.S.-Iran talks in Doha remain paused for funeral week after mediators reported "positive progress" on issues tied to the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, including Iran's proposed toll on Hormuz shipping traffic and the unfreezing of Iranian assets abroad. Both sides have agreed to resume once the ceremonies conclude later this week.

Wall Street, meanwhile, reopens this morning after a holiday weekend with tech stocks doing the early heavy lifting: Nasdaq 100 futures were up roughly 1% and S&P 500 futures gained 0.4%, while Dow futures sat little changed just above 53,100 following last Thursday's record close of 52,900.07. That Thursday rally came on the back of a surprisingly soft June jobs report — just 57,000 positions added against 113,000 expected, though the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.2% — which gave markets room to bet the Fed stays patient even after Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish "prices are too high" remarks at last week's Sintra conference. South Korea's SK Hynix is set to launch a roughly $28 billion Nasdaq depositary-receipt listing today, one of the largest AI-era share offerings on record, a fresh sign that chip-sector capital raising hasn't slowed despite last month's volatility.

Bitcoin has eased back to around $62,900 this morning after briefly topping $63,000 over the weekend, still a marked improvement from Wednesday's 21-month low near $58,278. Locally, both the Cubs and White Sox catch a breather today after Sunday's marathon Star-Spangled slate, with the Cubs avoiding a series sweep against St. Louis and the White Sox splitting their showdown with Cleveland.

Chips & Listings

SK Hynix Launches ~$28B Nasdaq Depositary Listing Today, One of the Largest AI-Era Share Sales Ever

South Korea's SK Hynix is proceeding with a roughly $28 billion U.S. depositary-receipt listing today, offering 17.79 million new shares as it looks to capitalize on hyperscaler demand for high-bandwidth memory. The listing follows a volatile stretch for Korean chipmakers — both SK Hynix and Samsung fell sharply on reports of trillion-dollar joint investment plans just over a week ago — and lands as chip stocks broadly rebound to start the new trading week.

Jobs & the Fed

June's Soft Jobs Report Still Setting the Tone: 57,000 Added Against 113,000 Expected

Thursday's June employment report showed just 57,000 jobs added, roughly half what economists had modeled, though the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.2% from 4.3%. Economists broadly read the miss as buying the Fed "wait-and-see" room on rates, a read that helped drive the Dow to its third record close of the holiday-shortened week even as chip stocks slid for a second straight session on AI-valuation jitters.

Corporate Moves

Meta's Cloud Pivot and Rivian's Raised Guidance Highlight a Quiet Pre-Holiday Session

Meta Platforms shares jumped last week after Bloomberg reported the company plans to enter the cloud business and sell excess AI computing capacity — a signal, some analysts said, that its capital spending had run ahead of near-term needs. Separately, Rivian raised its 2026 delivery guidance on strong EV demand and reports second-quarter results July 30, while Nike shares slipped despite a fiscal Q4 beat on continued weakness in Greater China sales.

First Half in Review

Dow +8.9%, S&P +9.6%, Nasdaq +12.8%, Russell 2000 +22% — A Historic First Half for U.S. Equities

The major averages closed the first half of 2026 with their strongest returns in years: the Dow's 8.9% gain is its best first half since 2021, the S&P rose 9.6%, the Nasdaq climbed 12.8%, and the small-cap Russell 2000 surged nearly 22% for its best half since 1991, powered by rotation into domestically-focused names as AI-linked semiconductors cooled from spring highs.

Technical Picture — Monday

Bitcoin Slips Back Below Its Own Daily Pivot as the Rally Off Wednesday's Low Pauses to Consolidate

Bitcoin is trading near $62,900 Monday, just under its daily pivot point of roughly $63,240, with support at the S1 level of about $62,565 and a more critical floor at the Bollinger lower band near $58,360 — a level that would matter again if the recent bounce fails. Price remains comfortably below the 50-day EMA near $65,740 and well under the 200-day EMA above $76,000, keeping the broader trend structurally bearish even as the Fear & Greed Index's move off its recent lows suggests some of the panic has drained out of the market. Whale wallets have added more than $16.7 billion of Bitcoin over the past two weeks per CryptoQuant data, and U.S. spot ETFs booked their first inflow day in weeks on Thursday after a brutal ten-day, $4.5 billion outflow stretch. Analysts continue to flag the setup as one that rewards patience: a confirmed MACD bullish cross would be the next technical signal worth watching before treating this as more than a corrective bounce.

Geopolitics

Iran — State Funeral, Day Three

Tehran's Streets Fill for the Funeral's Largest Procession as Mourners Call for Revenge

The main funeral procession for Ali Khamenei is moving through Tehran Monday, with helicopter footage aired on state television showing crowds stretching for miles down Azadi Street. The turnout is being described by state media as exceeding the roughly one million who attended the 2020 procession for Quds Force commander Qassem Soleimani. Former president Mahmoud Ahmadinejad was seen among the mourners, and signs and effigies along the route have targeted both President Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The procession continues to Qom on Tuesday and Iraq's holy cities of Najaf and Karbala on Wednesday, ahead of burial at the Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad on Thursday. New Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, who was wounded in the same February strike that killed his father, has yet to make a public appearance during the ceremonies.

Iran — Doha Track

U.S.-Iran Talks Stay Paused Through Funeral Week After Mediators Cite Progress on the Islamabad MOU

Qatari and Pakistani mediators say last week's indirect U.S.-Iran discussions in Doha made positive progress before both delegations agreed to pause proceedings until Khamenei's funeral concludes. Unresolved issues include Iran's proposed toll on vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz beginning in mid-August and the unfreezing of Iranian assets held abroad; both sides say talks are expected to resume once the mourning period ends later this week.

Real Estate, Mortgage & Tax

Monday Rate Check

30-Year Rate Holds Near 6.39% APR as Freddie Mac's Weekly Survey Sits at a Seven-Week Low

NerdWallet's daily 30-year fixed rate held essentially steady at 6.39% APR Monday morning, unchanged from the weekend and still about 15 basis points above where it stood a week ago. Freddie Mac's more closely watched weekly survey, released last Thursday, put the 30-year fixed at 6.43% for the week ending July 2 — a seven-week low, down from 6.49% the prior week. Fannie Mae's June 2026 housing forecast projects rates holding around 6.4% for the remainder of the year, a stable but still-elevated environment for buyers weighing affordability against a modest pickup in purchase demand.

Tax & Accounting

Four Days Left: COVID-Era Penalty Refund Claims Under the Kwong Decision Are Due July 10

The National Taxpayer Advocate's deadline for claiming refunds or abatements of penalties and interest the IRS assessed during the roughly 3.5-year COVID-19 disaster period is now four days away. Relief is not automatic: individuals, small businesses, estates, and trusts must file Form 843, Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement, by July 10 to preserve their rights following the Kwong v. United States decision, which calls into question the IRS's authority to assess those charges. Taxpayers with unresolved uncertainty are encouraged to file protective claims before the window closes.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

What Makes It to 100? Researchers Find a Distinct Chemical Fingerprint in Centenarians' Blood

A study published last week found that people who live past 100 in remarkably good health carry a distinct chemical signature in their blood that sets them apart from typical aging, including unusual patterns of bile acids and steroid compounds linked to longevity. The researchers say the finding points toward biological pathways — rather than simple luck or lifestyle alone — that may help explain why some people age with unusual resilience, and could eventually inform ways to identify or support healthy aging earlier in life. It's an early-stage finding, drawn from a relatively small population of exceptionally long-lived people, but it adds to a growing body of research suggesting that healthy longevity has a measurable biological signature rather than being purely a matter of chance.

Random Fact

Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini's 1989 funeral procession — the closest historical precedent to this week's ceremonies for his successor — drew an estimated 10 million mourners to Tehran and ended in tragedy when crowd surges killed more than 10 people and injured over 10,000 more. Iranian authorities have spent the past week rerouting more than 11,000 buses and adjusting rail schedules specifically to avoid a repeat of that chaos during Khamenei's six-day funeral, which by Monday's main procession was already being described by state media as larger than any single-day gathering since Soleimani's 2020 funeral.

Word of the Day
cortege

(noun, from French, ultimately from Italian corteggio, "retinue") — a solemn procession, especially one accompanying a body to a funeral or burial. Today's main cortege for Ali Khamenei, moving by truck through Tehran toward Azadi Square under the watch of huge crowds, is the central event of a funeral that has already stretched across three days and will continue for three more before burial in Mashhad.
