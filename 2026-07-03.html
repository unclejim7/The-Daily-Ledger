Markets · Geopolitics · Holiday Edition

Wall Street Goes Dark for the Fourth as Dow Caps a Third Record Close This Week; Doha Talks Pause Ahead of Khamenei Funeral

The NYSE, Nasdaq, and bond markets are closed today in observance of Independence Day, giving Wall Street a chance to exhale after a holiday-shortened week that delivered a soft jobs report, a third Dow record close, and the official start of the third quarter. Thursday's session capped it: the Dow Jones Industrial Average surged 594.83 points, or 1.14%, to a record 52,900.07, touching an intraday all-time high of 52,903.85 as traditional, less AI-exposed sectors caught a bid. The S&P 500 finished essentially flat at 7,483.24, while the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.80% to 25,832.67 as semiconductor names extended a second straight day of profit-taking — Micron, Applied Materials, and AMD all posted sharp declines. For the holiday-shortened week, the S&P gained 1.8%, the Nasdaq added 2.1%, and the Dow rose 2%, a clean finish to a quarter that was Wall Street's best since 2020.

The catalyst for Thursday's rotation was Thursday's own jobs report: June nonfarm payrolls rose just 57,000 against a 115,000 estimate, pulling the 2-year Treasury yield down more than 5 basis points to 4.108% as traders further discounted the odds of a July Fed hike. That combination — softer data plus a still-hawkish Chair Kevin Warsh, who told the ECB's Sintra forum this week that "we've seen that prices are too high" — leaves the July 28–29 FOMC meeting as a genuinely two-sided debate rather than a hawkish foregone conclusion. Markets are now pricing roughly a 65% chance of at least a quarter-point hike by September, according to CME FedWatch data, a probability that will move again once the holiday-shortened week's data is fully digested.

Overseas, the geopolitical calendar quieted rather than accelerated. The technical-level U.S.-Iran talks in Doha, which produced what Qatari and Pakistani mediators described as "positive progress" on implementing the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding earlier this week, are now on pause. Qatar's foreign ministry said the next round will be scheduled only after the conclusion of funeral processions for Iran's former Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei — killed in a U.S.-Israeli strike on the first day of June's war — which are set to begin Saturday and run six days across Iran and Iraq. The pause leaves the interim ceasefire and the still-unresolved release of roughly $6 billion in frozen Iranian assets in a holding pattern over the holiday weekend.

With markets dark, Bitcoin is the one major asset still trading today, and it has quietly put together its best stretch since June's rout: BTC changed hands near $61,680 early Friday, up more than 2.7% over the past several sessions and well off the 21-month low near $58,000 it touched Wednesday. The next scheduled catalyst for both stocks and crypto is the July 28–29 FOMC meeting; in the meantime, Wall Street returns Monday to a holiday-shortened week with earnings season and the Doha track both still to be resolved.

Technical Picture — Friday

Bitcoin's Best Stretch Since the June Rout as Whale Accumulation Meets a Historically Strong Month

Bitcoin is up roughly 2.7% since the calendar turned to July, clawing back from Wednesday's 21-month low near $58,000 and June's brutal 20.5% monthly decline — the worst month of the current cycle and one that drove spot Bitcoin ETFs to record net outflows near $4.5 billion. CryptoQuant data shows whales have accumulated more than 270,000 BTC over the past two weeks even as retail sentiment remains stuck in Extreme Fear, a divergence that has historically preceded local bottoms. July has been a seasonally strong month for Bitcoin, averaging a 7.25% gain over the last 13 years, but Peter Schiff's warning stands: a sustained break below $58,000 support would open the door toward $53,800 and, in a capitulation scenario, the high-$40Ks. With equity markets closed today, crypto is the only major asset actively trading through the holiday, and its next real catalyst arrives with the July 28–29 FOMC meeting.

Iran — Doha

Next Doha Session on Hold as Six-Day Funeral Processions for Khamenei Begin Saturday

Wednesday's technical-level talks in Doha produced what Qatari and Pakistani mediators jointly called "positive progress" on implementing the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, including discussion of the roughly $6 billion in frozen Iranian assets still awaiting release. But Qatar's foreign ministry said the next round of talks will only be scheduled after the conclusion of funeral processions for former Iranian Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, killed in a U.S.-Israeli strike on the first day of June's war, which begin Saturday and are expected to run six days across Iran and Iraq. Iran's technical delegation, led by Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi, did not include the country's top negotiators, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi or Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf. The pause effectively freezes the diplomatic track over the U.S. holiday weekend.

Real Estate, Mortgage & Tax

Friday Rate Check

Freddie Mac's Weekly Rate Falls to a Seven-Week Low of 6.43% as Purchase Demand Edges Higher

Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey, released Thursday, put the 30-year fixed-rate mortgage at 6.43% for the week ending July 2, down from 6.49% the week prior and the lowest reading in seven weeks. The 15-year fixed averaged 5.79%, also down slightly. "With rates at a seven-week low and purchase demand continuing to edge higher, it's an encouraging sign as prospective homebuyers respond to modest improvements in affordability," Freddie Mac said in its release. NerdWallet's APR-based daily rate held steady at 6.38% Friday morning, nine basis points above where it stood a week ago but 15 basis points below year-ago levels. Fannie Mae's June housing forecast projects 30-year rates holding near 6.4% through the balance of 2026 regardless of whether the Fed moves at its July meeting.

Tax & Accounting

One Week Left: COVID-Era Penalty Refund Claims Are Due July 10

The National Taxpayer Advocate's July 10 deadline for claiming refunds or abatements of penalties and interest the IRS assessed during the roughly 3.5-year COVID-19 federal disaster period is now one week away. Relief is not automatic — individuals, small businesses, estates, and trusts affected must file Form 843, Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement, by the deadline to preserve their rights, following a court decision in the Kwong case that calls into question the IRS's authority to assess those charges. Taxpayers whose situations are less clear-cut are encouraged to file protective claims, which don't require an exact dollar figure but must describe the contingencies involved. With the July 4 holiday falling in the middle of the final week, filers with open questions should not wait until the last business day to act.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

The Heat Wave Is Breaking, But Storm Season Isn't — A Quick Refresher on Lightning Safety

With today's front finally ending Chicagoland's stretch of 90-degree heat, the tradeoff is an elevated risk of afternoon and evening thunderstorms right as many households head outdoors for holiday-weekend cookouts and Wrigley Field baseball. The National Weather Service's standard guidance remains the simplest and most effective: if you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck by lightning, and the only reliably safe locations are a substantial building or a hard-topped vehicle with the windows up. The common instinct to keep playing through a "light" rain shower before a storm fully arrives is one of the leading contributors to weather-related injuries every summer, precisely because lightning can strike more than 10 miles from the nearest rainfall. If outdoor plans are on the calendar for this afternoon or evening, checking the radar before heading out — and having a real indoor fallback in mind — is worth the two minutes it takes.

Random Fact

The Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey being referenced in today's real estate section has run continuously since April 1971 — making this week's 6.43% reading part of an unbroken 55-year weekly record. The survey's methodology has been updated over the decades, most recently in November 2022, when Freddie Mac shifted from a lender phone survey to data drawn directly from loan applications submitted through its Loan Product Advisor system, a change that made the weekly number more responsive to real-time market conditions.

Word of the Day
interregnum

(noun, from Latin inter- "between" + regnum "reign") — a pause or gap in continuity, historically referring to the period between the reign of one ruler and the accession of the next. It fits today's news cycle unusually well: a market interregnum with U.S. exchanges closed, and a diplomatic interregnum in Doha, where the next round of U.S.-Iran talks waits on the six-day mourning period for a fallen head of state before the process can resume.
