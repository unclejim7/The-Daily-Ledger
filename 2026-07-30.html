Geopolitics · Markets · Thursday Edition

U.S. Completes a “Heavy Wave” of Retaliatory Strikes on Iran as Tehran Vows to “Punish the Aggressor Today”

The U.S. military said it had "successfully completed a heavy wave of strikes against Iran" late Wednesday night, delivering on President Trump's promise of a forceful response to Tuesday's Iranian missile attack on American forces in Jordan. Trump had told reporters Wednesday it was "our turn" after CENTCOM confirmed the intercepted Iranian barrage, and by 10 p.m. ET the retaliatory campaign was underway. Iranian state media reported three civilians killed in the strikes, while Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said three of its own personnel died. The IRGC responded Thursday morning with a pointed warning that Iran "will punish the aggressor today," adding that any country assisting the United States "would also receive a harsh response"—a direct threat aimed at Saudi Arabia and Jordan, both of which have joined U.S. operations in recent days.

The overnight strikes confirm what Wednesday's missile attack had already signaled: the four-day pause that briefly reshaped the mood around this war is over, and the conflict is if anything widening rather than narrowing. Yemen's Houthi rebels remain locked in their own escalating fight with Saudi Arabia after this month's collapsed truce, Hezbollah has kept up missile fire on Israel since resuming attacks in March, and Saudi forces are now directly engaged against Iran-backed militias in Iraq alongside U.S. troops. Riyadh is effectively facing pressure from three directions at once—Houthi missiles from the south, militia drone attacks from Iraq, and now Tehran's explicit threats over its cooperation with Washington.

Markets are still digesting Wednesday's reaction to all of this layered atop the Fed's decision. Stocks were routed: the Dow tumbled 2.19% to 51,594.14, the S&P 500 dropped 1.52% to 7,316.15, and the Nasdaq fell 1.74% to 24,442.94, as long-dated Treasury yields rose sharply on signs the bond market thinks the Fed, in holding rates steady, may be falling behind the inflation fight rather than getting ahead of it. Oil extended its own volatile week, with Brent crude surging more than 7% to $90.66 as Trump's retaliation promise firmed up overnight.

Thursday brings its own reasons for markets to move again: Core PCE inflation data, the Fed's preferred gauge and the last major inflation read before September's meeting, lands at 8:30 a.m. ET, while Apple and Amazon report earnings after the close following Wednesday night's strong Meta beat ($60.80 billion in revenue, up 27% year-over-year, ahead of the $60.22 billion expected). Futures were pointing to a rebound early Thursday—Dow futures up 0.51%, S&P 500 futures up 0.4%, Nasdaq futures up a sharp 1.86%—though with a fresh Iran retaliation threat hanging over the day, how much of that holds into the close is very much an open question.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

The Core PCE index landing Thursday actually reports data for May 2026—a two-month lag baked into how the Bureau of Economic Analysis compiles personal consumption data, which means the "freshest" inflation gauge the Fed relies on for its 2% target is, by the time anyone reads it, already describing an economy two months in the past. That lag is precisely why bond traders were already reacting to inflation expectations Wednesday night rather than waiting for Thursday's official print.
