Geopolitics · Markets · Friday Edition

Iranian Cleric Calls a Cease-Fire “a Deception” as the U.S. and Israel Weigh a Land Blockade — Wall Street Rallies on Cooler Inflation Before Apple and Amazon Split Investors

A day after CENTCOM's "heavy wave" of retaliatory strikes on Iran, a senior Iranian cleric moved to close the door on any diplomatic off-ramp. Ahmad Alamolhoda, the supreme leader's representative in Mashhad, told Friday prayer-goers that any cease-fire offer from Washington would be "a deception" designed to buy the U.S. time to redeploy forces before launching a new attack—a striking public rejection of even the possibility of de-escalation, delivered from one of Iran's most influential religious pulpits. Separately, the Telegraph reported that U.S. and Israeli officials have begun discussing the possibility of a land blockade of Iran, aimed at tightening economic pressure on Tehran beyond the naval blockade already in place. Neither government has confirmed the reporting, but if accurate, it would mark a significant expansion of the economic dimension of a war that has already reshaped gas prices at pumps across the United States.

Markets, for their part, spent Thursday looking past the war entirely. The Dow surged 613.92 points, or 1.2%, to 52,208.06, the S&P 500 climbed 1.7% to 7,437.64, and the Nasdaq jumped 2.8% to 25,122.18—a sharp rebound from Wednesday's rout, powered by Microsoft, semiconductor strength, and a friendlier-than-fought inflation report. Core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, came in at 3.3% year-over-year for June, matching forecasts and down from May, while the headline PCE rate eased to 3.7% from 4.1%—exactly the kind of data that undercuts Wednesday's bond-market worry that the Fed's hold left it behind the curve.

Big Tech earnings, though, delivered a split verdict Friday morning. Amazon obliterated expectations, posting $200.61 billion in second-quarter revenue against $196.46 billion expected and earnings of $5.75 per share versus a $1.82 estimate, sending shares up 12% in premarket trading. Apple beat on revenue, earnings, and iPhone sales, but its guidance landed with a thud: citing "supply constraints," the company projected 9-11% revenue growth for the current quarter against Street expectations of 12%, and shares fell 7% premarket. The divergence is being read on trading desks as investors actively picking winners and losers within the AI and consumer-tech trade rather than treating Big Tech as a single basket.

Elsewhere, gold held near $4,086 as the dollar slid on suspicion of fresh Japanese currency intervention, oil stayed range-bound in the mid-$80s after this week's whiplash, and Bitcoin drifted slightly lower to around $63,875. Freddie Mac's weekly survey, released Thursday, showed the 30-year fixed mortgage jumping to 6.66%, a fresh one-year high, up from 6.58% the week before—a reminder that a friendlier inflation print doesn't necessarily translate into cheaper borrowing right away.

The Beat

Amazon Blows Past Estimates on Both Revenue and Earnings

Amazon's $200.61 billion in second-quarter revenue and $5.75-per-share earnings crushed Wall Street's expectations of $196.46 billion and $1.82 per share respectively, sending shares up 12% premarket as investors reward the clearest sign yet that its cloud and retail businesses are both firing.

The Miss

Apple's Guidance Stumbles on “Supply Constraints”

Apple topped estimates on revenue, earnings, and iPhone sales, but guided current-quarter revenue growth to 9-11%, short of the 12% Wall Street wanted, citing supply constraints—enough to send shares down 7% premarket despite the otherwise solid quarter.

The Inflation Read

Core PCE Cools to 3.3%, Undercutting Wednesday's Bond-Market Fears

June's Core PCE reading matched forecasts at 3.3% year-over-year, down from May, while the headline rate eased to 3.7% from 4.1%—a friendlier print than the bond market's Wednesday-night reaction to the Fed's hold seemed to anticipate.

The Dollar

Gold Holds Near $4,100 as the Dollar Slides on Suspected Japanese Intervention

Gold's modest pullback to around $4,086 masks a currency story underneath: the dollar weakened broadly Friday on suspicion Japan intervened again to prop up the yen, a cross-current that's kept gold historically resilient even on a technically down day.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

The Data Catches Up

Cooler Core PCE Complicates the “Fed Is Behind” Narrative

Thursday's Core PCE report—3.3% year-over-year in June, matching forecasts and down from May, with the headline rate easing to 3.7% from 4.1%—lands as a partial rebuttal to Wednesday night's bond-market selloff, which had priced in fears the Fed's hold left it behind the inflation curve. The data doesn't resolve the debate around Wednesday's reported committee dissents, but it does give Chair Warsh's "wait and see" posture a firmer data-driven footing heading into September, when the next meeting will have a fresh jobs report and another PCE reading to weigh alongside whatever the Iran conflict does to energy prices between now and then.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Limiting Sugar in the First Two Years of Life May Lower Dementia Risk Decades Later

New research finds that restricting added sugar intake through age two is associated with a reduced risk of dementia much later in life, adding to growing evidence that early-childhood nutrition can shape long-term brain health in ways that persist for decades. Researchers caution the findings are associational and more work is needed to understand the mechanism, but the result lands alongside a separate reminder this week that vigilance matters at every life stage: the FDA issued a nationwide Class II recall of the thyroid medication levothyroxine over potency concerns, underscoring that both what we consume early and what we're prescribed later deserve the same level of scrutiny.

Random Fact

Mashhad, where Friday's "cease-fire would be a deception" sermon was delivered, is Iran's second-largest city and home to the Imam Reza shrine, one of the holiest sites in Shia Islam and a pilgrimage destination for an estimated 20 million visitors a year—meaning the cleric's remarks reached one of the largest and most religiously significant Friday prayer audiences anywhere in the country.

Word of the Day
blockade

(noun, from the verb "to block" plus the military suffix "-ade") — the isolation of a place, typically by military force, to prevent goods, people, or supplies from entering or leaving. Blockades come in variants: naval (the sea route already restricted around Iran), and now reportedly land, which would close overland trade and supply corridors as well—together approaching what strategists sometimes call a "full-spectrum" blockade, cutting off a target from nearly every direction at once.
