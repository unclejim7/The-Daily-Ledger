Markets · Federal Reserve · Wednesday Edition

Markets Increasingly Bet the Fed Will Hold, Not Hike, as Bitcoin Finally Reclaims $84,200

The genuine uncertainty that's hung over the Federal Reserve's October 27–28 decision for weeks has tilted sharply toward clarity. Prediction markets and CME FedWatch pricing now put the odds of another rate hike at roughly 15%, down from near a coin flip just last week, with Goldman Sachs telling clients to expect the Fed to skip an October move entirely and defer any further tightening to December. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, speaking at an IMF event in Bangkok, again declined to tip his hand — “I don't prejudge meetings” — but did call October and December “the key decision points” left this year, while reiterating that inflation remains “uncomfortably elevated.”

Bitcoin finally reclaimed the $84,200 level this edition has flagged for weeks as the threshold needed to reclaim momentum, rallying to roughly $84,700 on a broader tech rally that lifted risk assets generally. It's the first time the asset has traded above that mark since the hot inflation report and subsequent selloff knocked it lower earlier this month.

Stocks were mixed: the Dow rose 0.5% to 51,630.38 on a strong earnings beat from 3M, which also raised its full-year guidance, while the S&P 500 gained 0.3% to 7,650.37. The Nasdaq slipped 0.2% to 26,050.74 as chip-sector weakness persisted even as the broader market found its footing. Brent crude eased to roughly $109 a barrel, still near its highest levels since May even with a modest pullback from Tuesday's peak.

The 30-year mortgage rate holds at 7.46% ahead of tomorrow's Freddie Mac reading. In sports, the World Series opens Friday at Milwaukee, with the Brewers' Jacob Misiorowski facing the Rays' Drew Rasmussen in Game 1 and Milwaukee favored at home.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Medical Groups Issue Independent Fall Vaccine Guidance as CDC Recommendations Lag

The American Medical Association and a university-based vaccine integrity project published independent recommendations for fall flu, COVID, and RSV vaccination this week, stepping in where they say federal guidance has gone quiet. The CDC has not issued updated 2026-27 recommendations for RSV or COVID vaccines, and its flu guidance still points to an older schedule that doesn't address a newly approved mRNA flu vaccine for older adults, prompting medical societies to fill the gap directly for clinicians and patients.
