Geopolitics · Markets · Friday Edition

Khamenei Laid to Rest in Mashhad as the Ceasefire He Died Over Stays Broken — Wall Street Looks Past It Toward SK Hynix

Iran's slain Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was buried before dawn Friday at the Shrine of Imam Reza in Mashhad, state media reported, closing out a six-day state funeral that drew crowds Iranian officials put at more than 15 million and possibly double that. A helicopter lifted his coffin over the crush of mourners for the final approach to the shrine; Khamenei was buried alongside four family members killed alongside him in the February 28 US-Israeli strike that opened the war. His son and declared successor, Mojtaba Khamenei, was absent from every stage of the funeral, a person familiar with the matter told CNN, citing security concerns after Israel's threats against him specifically. Mourners in Mashhad chanted for revenge against President Trump throughout the procession, underscoring how far the diplomatic track has unraveled since the memorandum both governments signed in Versailles less than a month ago.

That track remains broken. Trump declared the ceasefire "over" Wednesday after Iran struck three tankers near the Strait of Hormuz, and the US answered with two consecutive nights of strikes on more than 80 targets, including sites near a nuclear power plant and a fishing pier in Bushehr province Thursday that Washington has not confirmed carrying out. A US official told CNN the strategy is deliberate: strike, then pause, to avoid full-scale escalation while keeping a target list in reserve as leverage. Pakistan and Qatar are working back channels to bring both sides back to the table, the same official said, while Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu spoke by phone Thursday evening to coordinate next steps. Traffic through Hormuz, which had rebounded during the ceasefire, thinned again this week after the tanker strikes, though MarineTraffic data showed at least 13 vessels still transited the strait in the past 24 hours.

Markets, for a second straight session, chose not to flinch. The S&P 500 rose 0.81% Thursday to 7,543.64, the Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.30% to 26,206.89, and the Dow added 0.27% to close at 52,487.41, with chipmakers doing most of the lifting as investors positioned for Friday's main event: SK Hynix's roughly $26.5 billion Nasdaq listing, the largest-ever US share offering by a foreign company and a proxy vote on whether the AI trade still has legs. Reported demand ran seven times the shares available. Friday's premarket was more mixed than euphoric — Dow futures added about 0.2%, S&P futures were roughly flat, and Nasdaq 100 futures slipped as some chip names cooled ahead of the debut — while oil gave back only part of Wednesday's spike, with WTI near $72 and Brent above $77 as traders weighed whether this week's escalation has staying power or is another round of the strike-and-pause pattern that has repeated for weeks.

Layered underneath both stories is Wednesday's FOMC minutes, which showed the rate-setting committee split exactly 9-9 on a 2026 hike and Chair Kevin Warsh withholding his own dot-plot projection for the first time in the tool's history. CME FedWatch now prices roughly a 79% chance the Fed holds rates steady at the July 28-29 meeting, up from earlier in the week, as this week's renewed oil-price pressure complicates the disinflation story markets had been counting on. The 10-year yield eased to roughly 4.55% Friday morning, still elevated enough to keep mortgage rates and borrowing costs from providing much relief.

Chip IPO

SK Hynix Prices ~$26.5B Nasdaq Debut Friday — Largest-Ever US Listing by a Foreign Company

The South Korean memory giant and key Nvidia supplier priced its US depositary-receipt offering Friday after reported demand ran roughly seven times the shares available, making the listing the largest ever on a US exchange by a foreign issuer. The debut is widely read as a referendum on whether the AI infrastructure trade still has room to run after a volatile stretch for chip stocks; Micron and Sandisk both gained sharply Thursday in anticipation.

Earnings

Delta Beats on Both Lines, Reaffirms Full-Year Guidance Despite Its Highest-Ever Quarterly Fuel Bill

Delta reported adjusted Q2 EPS of $1.56 against a $1.48 estimate on revenue of $17.7 billion, up 14% year-over-year, even as it absorbed the highest quarterly fuel expense in company history — $3.93 a gallon, up 75% from a year ago. CEO Ed Bastian said fare increases covered roughly 60% of that jump, and the airline guided Q3 EPS to $2.00-$2.50 while reaffirming its full-year forecast of $6.50-$7.50 and announcing a 15% dividend increase starting next quarter.

Oil & Hormuz

Crude Holds Most of Wednesday's Spike as Strait Traffic Thins Again on Renewed Strikes

WTI settled Wednesday up more than 4% near $73.52 and Brent jumped 5.4% to $78.19 after the tanker strikes and reimposed Treasury sanctions on Iranian oil sales, which give buyers until July 17 to complete purchases nearly six weeks ahead of the original deadline. Friday morning prices eased modestly — WTI near $72, Brent above $77 — as traders weighed reports that roughly 13 vessels still transited Hormuz in the past day despite the renewed hostilities.

Rates

10-Year Eases to ~4.55% Friday as Markets Digest a Split Fed and a Firmer Oil Backdrop

The 10-year Treasury yield, which climbed toward 4.6% earlier in the week on the hawkish FOMC minutes, eased modestly Friday morning even as the two-year held near 4.17-4.21%. CME FedWatch now prices roughly a 79% probability the Fed holds at the July 28-29 meeting, up from earlier this week, though several strategists note that a sustained oil rally from this week's strikes could revive hike odds quickly if it persists into August.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

Reading the Committee

A 9-9 Split, a Silent Chair, and an Oil Shock That Landed Two Days After the Minutes Did

Wednesday's FOMC minutes showed the committee split exactly down the middle on a 2026 rate hike, with Chair Kevin Warsh withholding his own dot-plot projection for the first time in the tool's 14-year history. What the minutes could not capture is what has happened since: a fresh round of tanker strikes and US-Iran escalation that pushed oil more than 5% higher Wednesday, complicating the disinflation narrative the committee was debating just three weeks earlier. CME FedWatch's 79% probability of a July hold suggests markets still expect Warsh to look through this week's volatility, but the July 28-29 meeting will land with a materially different oil and geopolitical backdrop than the one the committee debated in mid-June. Whether this week's spike proves durable or fades as quickly as June's did will do more to shape that meeting than anything written in Wednesday's transcript.

State Media Confirms Khamenei's Burial as Six-Day Funeral Closes; Successor Still Unseen

Armstrong's framework continues to treat 2026 as the Directional Change, 2027 as the Panic Cycle escalation, and 2028 as the potential systemic break — with this week's events, in his reading, simply the latest data point confirming the pattern rather than a departure from it.

Real Estate, Mortgage & Tax

Friday Rate Check

Freddie Mac's 30-Year Climbs to 6.49% for the Week as Daily Trackers Show Rates Mostly Higher

Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey, released Thursday, put the 30-year fixed at 6.49% for the week ending July 9, up from 6.43% the week before though still below 6.72% a year ago; the 15-year rose to 5.82% from 5.79%. Daily trackers show similar drift: Zillow's average 30-year rose 12 basis points to 6.47% Friday, Fortune's Mortgage Resource Center figure put the rate at 6.608%, and Mortgage News Daily's real-time index topped 6.7% Thursday. Freddie Mac chief economist Sam Khater attributed the stability-with-a-slight-uptick to the apparent end of the Middle East ceasefire reviving inflation concerns; the MBA and Fannie Mae both continue to forecast 30-year rates holding in the mid-6% range through year-end regardless of the Fed's next move. The Freddie Mac survey itself turns 55 this year, having launched in April 1971.

Tax & Accounting — Deadline Today

Today Is the Final Day to File Form 843 Kwong-Related Protective Refund Claims

Taxpayers who paid failure-to-file or failure-to-pay penalties on a 2019-2022 Form 1040 during the COVID disaster period must file Form 843 by the end of today to preserve their right to a refund under Kwong v. United States, now on appeal. Individual filers with an IRS Online Account can submit electronically for fully paid interest and penalties; everyone else must mail a paper form to the Ogden, UT service center marked "Kwong vs. United States" across the top, and a separate form is required for each tax year in question. National Taxpayer Advocate Erin Collins has publicly criticized the compressed timeline as unfair to otherwise-eligible taxpayers who may not have heard about the claim window in time.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

How Fast You Walk May Predict How Sharp You Stay — New Study Links Gait Speed to Cognitive Decline

New research highlighted this week found that older adults who maintain a faster walking pace show roughly a 50% lower risk of cognitive decline compared to slower walkers, adding to a growing body of evidence that gait speed functions as an accessible, low-cost marker of brain health. Researchers note the relationship likely runs in both directions — cardiovascular fitness supports brain function, while early cognitive changes can themselves slow movement — but the practical takeaway is straightforward: regular brisk walking, even in short daily sessions, remains one of the best-supported low-effort interventions for long-term cognitive health. With a break in the weather before next week's heat returns, today is a reasonable day to get outside for one.

Random Fact

Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey, which set this week's 6.49% benchmark rate, has been running continuously since April 1971 — making it 55 years old this year. Since Freddie Mac began collecting the data, the median 30-year fixed rate has been 7.23%, meaning today's 6.49% actually sits below the historical middle, even though it feels elevated after the ultra-low-rate years of 2020-2021, when the 30-year briefly touched a record 2.65%.

Word of the Day
recrudescence

(noun, from Latin recrudescere, "to become raw again") — the breaking out afresh of something that had been dormant or in remission, especially a disease or a conflict. Coined for medicine, the word fits this week's Iran story with uncomfortable precision: a ceasefire that had gone quiet, only to reopen along the same fault lines — Hormuz, tankers, retaliatory strikes — that produced it in the first place.
