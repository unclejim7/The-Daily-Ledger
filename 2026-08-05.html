Geopolitics · Markets · Wednesday Edition

“The Deal Is Now Complete”: U.S. and Iran Reach Framework Agreement to End the War

After more than five months of strikes, pauses, denials, and reversals, the United States and Iran announced a framework deal Wednesday to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. "The Deal with the Islamic Republic of Iran is now complete," President Trump said, a declarative statement that stands in sharp contrast to the claim-and-denial pattern that has defined nearly every prior diplomatic development in this conflict. Iranian officials confirmed the framework as well, a rare moment of alignment between the two governments' public statements after weeks of contradiction.

The memorandum of understanding under discussion runs to 14 points and reportedly includes an end to hostilities not just between the U.S. and Iran directly but in the broader theater the war expanded into, including Lebanon, along with a withdrawal of U.S. forces from the region. On the Strait of Hormuz specifically, the deal establishes a 60-day temporary arrangement between Oman and Iran governing safe passage, with room to extend if it holds. U.S. Central Command said Wednesday that the strait's southern route, through Omani territorial waters, "remains free and open."

The scale of the reversal is difficult to overstate against the backdrop of just the past seven days: this time last Wednesday, Iran was firing ballistic missiles at U.S. forces in Jordan, shattering an earlier pause and prompting a "heavy wave" of American retaliatory strikes. In the days since, a planned energy-infrastructure bombing campaign was drawn up and then called off, and diplomatic signals flipped from "no talks scheduled" to "framework complete" within roughly 72 hours. Whether this settlement holds where so many prior pauses didn't is the question hanging over everything else in today's edition.

Markets, which had already been rallying hard on the mere prospect of de-escalation, now have to price the real thing. Tuesday's close was already a landmark session on its own: the Dow closed above 54,000 for the first time in its history, gaining 907 points, or 1.71%, while the S&P 500 jumped 1.79% to a record 7,736.52 and the Nasdaq surged 2.59% to 26,584.99. Oil, which had already shed roughly 5% Tuesday on de-escalation hopes, held in the mid-$70s Wednesday as traders weighed how a genuine end to the conflict changes the supply picture going forward. With no Fed meeting until September, this week's JOLTS report and Friday's jobs data now carry the added complication of a potentially disinflationary oil environment layered on top of an already-hawkish rate-hike repricing.

Armstrong Economics's 2026 Directional Change framework has never argued the war itself was the point—the war has been one visible symptom of a broader instability the site has tracked toward the 2027 Panic Cycle escalation all year. Health, Wellness & Fitness

Once-Weekly HIV Pill Matches Daily Regimen in International Study

A once-weekly HIV pill combining the antiretroviral drugs islatravir and lenacapavir proved as effective as a standard once-daily regimen in a new international study, a potentially significant quality-of-life improvement for patients managing lifelong treatment. Reducing pill burden from daily to weekly has repeatedly shown, across other chronic conditions, meaningful improvements in long-term adherence, which researchers say could translate directly into better real-world outcomes even without any change in the drugs' underlying efficacy.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

Memoranda of understanding like the reported 14-point Iran framework are, by design, less legally binding than formal treaties—a deliberate diplomatic choice that lets both sides claim a deal without either government needing legislative ratification, which is exactly why so many of this war's prior "agreements" proved fragile: an MOU can be walked back with a press statement in a way a ratified treaty cannot.

Word of the Day
framework

(noun, from Old English "fram," forward, plus "weorc," work) — a basic structure underlying a system or concept, providing shape without specifying every detail. In diplomacy, a "framework deal" signals broad agreement on principles while leaving implementation details to be worked out later—useful for building momentum quickly, but also the exact gap where previous Iran-U.S. agreements have unraveled.
