Federal Reserve · Markets · Monday Edition

Markets Open FOMC Week Cautiously as Oil Holds Near Multi-Month Highs and Aramco's Evacuation Continues

The Federal Reserve's two-day policy meeting begins tomorrow, with Wednesday's 2 p.m. ET decision now the week's central event. Markets opened the week with modest, tentative gains: the S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,559.52, the Dow added 0.2% to 51,485.25, and the Nasdaq gained 0.4% to 25,398.42. The moves read less like conviction than positioning — Chair Kevin Warsh has given traders nothing to work with beyond his now-familiar refusal to preview the outcome.

Oil held near its recent multi-month highs, with Brent easing slightly to roughly $115.50 a barrel after Friday's $116.72 settle, as no new incident was reported overnight in either the Strait of Hormuz or the broader Saudi theater. Aramco's evacuation of its Dhahran headquarters enters its second week with still no timeline for staff to return — an open-ended posture that continues to read as more significant than any single new headline.

Bitcoin ticked up to roughly $81,200, continuing its stabilization after a volatile weekend that saw it touch a low near $79,200 before recovering. The 30-year mortgage rate holds at 7.38%, with no new weekly reading until Thursday.

Tonight, the World Series shifts to Tampa Bay for Game 3, with the Brewers' Robert Gasser on the mound as Milwaukee looks to extend its 2-0 series lead. First pitch is scheduled for 7 p.m. Central at Tropicana Field.

Positioning, Not Conviction

Monday's Modest Gains Look Like Traders Hedging Their Bets, Not Making Them

Gains under half a percent across the board, with no clear catalyst and a major Fed decision 48 hours out, read more like portfolio adjustments ahead of uncertainty than any genuine vote of confidence in a particular outcome.

A New Baseline, Quietly Confirmed

Oil Holding Near $115 Without a Fresh Incident Is Itself the Story

A day without a new headline used to mean falling prices; now it means oil simply holds near its recent highs, further evidence that the market has stopped waiting for de-escalation and started treating elevated prices as the default.

A Second Week, Still Open-Ended

Aramco's Evacuation Has Now Outlasted Most Single Incidents in This Conflict

Most disruptions tied to specific strikes resolve within days; a corporate evacuation stretching into a second week without a stated return date suggests the company's own risk assessment remains unsettled, not just cautious.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Markets Often Misread a Quiet Stretch as the Beginning of the End

Armstrong Economics has repeatedly cautioned that a lull following a dramatic escalation is frequently mistaken for the start of de-escalation, when in reality it's often just the natural pause between phases of a prolonged conflict. The site's War Cycle model continues to treat this week's calm as provisional rather than conclusive, particularly with a major Fed decision also pending.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

GLP-1 Medications May Be Linked to Higher Risk of Osteoporosis, Gout, and Osteomalacia

New research suggests that GLP-1 medications, widely used for diabetes and weight management, may be associated with an increased risk of osteoporosis, gout, and osteomalacia among people with type 2 diabetes and obesity. The findings add a note of caution to a drug class that has otherwise generated almost entirely positive headlines in recent months, and researchers say patients and physicians should weigh these potential risks alongside the medications' well-documented benefits.
