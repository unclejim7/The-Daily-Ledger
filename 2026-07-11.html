Geopolitics · Markets · Weekend Edition

Trump Says the Ceasefire Is “Over” but Iran Talks Will Continue — Wall Street Closes the Week Near Records on SK Hynix’s Blockbuster Debut

President Trump used a Friday Truth Social post to hold two contradictory ideas at once: the U.S.-Iran ceasefire remains “OVER,” he wrote, even as he confirmed Washington has agreed to Tehran’s request to keep talking. “The Islamic Republic of Iran has asked us to continue ‘talks.’ We have agreed to do so,” Trump wrote, adding that the U.S. had nonetheless made clear the ceasefire itself is finished. Iran’s top negotiator, parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, struck a matching note of hostility from the sidelines of the stalled Bürgenstock process, telling reporters Friday that Iran remains “distrustful of the Americans” and recalling that he told Vice President JD Vance directly, “we have no trust in you.” Pakistan and Qatar continue working back channels to bring both sides to the table, regional sources told CNN, while U.S. Deputy UN Ambassador Tammy Bruce told the Security Council Friday that Washington still prefers a diplomatic resolution but “stands ready to hold Iran to account.”

A CNN analysis published Friday, conducted with the Institute for Science and International Security, added a new and uncomfortable wrinkle: satellite imagery from June 22 and July 7 shows apparent repair and reconstruction work at Parchin’s Taleghan 2 site, where experts believe explosive material for nuclear weapons is stored, alongside vehicle activity at the suspected underground Pickaxe Mountain facility during the period the memorandum was supposedly in effect. Neither government has responded on the record. Separately, the Strait of Hormuz remains the practical fault line — Iran continues to assert control over parts of the waterway despite the memorandum’s ambiguous language on the subject, and roughly 13 to 15 commercial vessels transited the strait in the past 24 hours, a fraction of pre-war traffic, according to MarineTraffic data.

Wall Street, for the second straight week, treated the diplomatic deterioration as background noise. Friday’s main event was South Korean chipmaker SK Hynix’s Nasdaq debut: the American depositary receipts, priced at $149, opened at $170 and briefly touched $175 — a pop of roughly 17% — before settling to close up about 13%, capping the largest-ever U.S. listing by a foreign company at $26.5 billion raised. The S&P 500 gained 0.42% to 7,575.39, the Nasdaq added 0.29% to 26,281.61, and the Dow rose 0.29% to 52,637.01; both the S&P and Nasdaq notched weekly gains despite the week’s renewed Iran strikes, while the Dow slipped about 0.6% on the week. Meta jumped roughly 6% on a Bank of America note flagging lower-than-feared AI infrastructure costs, and Micron fell 1.4% as investors weighed SK Hynix as a genuine second pure-play option in memory chips. Oil eased modestly — WTI settled near $71.59, off almost 1% — even with Hormuz traffic still thin, a sign traders are betting the current strike-and-pause pattern holds rather than escalates further.

The coming week carries its own weight: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh testifies before the House Financial Services Committee, June CPI lands Tuesday, and the FOMC’s July 28–29 meeting looms behind both. Locally, the White Sox’s 14-1 demolition of the Athletics vaulted them into first place in the AL Central, the Cubs were shut out by a dominant Hunter Greene, and Chicagoland gets one more mild, sunny weekend before next week’s heat wave arrives in earnest.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

Ahead of the Testimony

Warsh Heads to Capitol Hill Still Refusing to Hint at July, While His “New Course” for the Fed Takes Shape

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh will testify before the House Financial Services Committee next week, his first appearance before lawmakers since taking the gavel in May, and he arrives having spent the past several weeks laying out an unusually candid overhaul of how the Fed makes decisions. At the ECB’s Sintra forum on July 1, Warsh reiterated that “we’ve all looked around, and we’ve seen that prices are too high,” while describing five new task forces — on communications, balance sheet policy, data sources, productivity, and the inflation framework itself — that he wants delivering findings by year-end. He has also said price risks and inflation expectations have “come down” over the past month, even as he continues withholding his own dot-plot projection, a break from 14 years of Fed practice. Futures markets have priced in a greater-than 75% chance of at least one hike before year-end, up sharply from 58% at the start of June, with most traders still expecting a hold at the July meeting and the real decision point pushed to September. Tuesday’s June CPI print, arriving before Warsh takes questions from lawmakers, could move that math in either direction.

Freddie Mac’s 30-Year Settles at 6.49% for the Week as Daily Trackers Show Rates Holding Near the High End

Freddie Mac’s weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed at 6.49% for the week ending July 9, up from 6.43% the week before, though still below 6.72% a year ago; the 15-year rose to 5.82% from 5.79%. Daily trackers diverge slightly — NerdWallet had the 30-year at 6.44% APR Friday, Bankrate’s Saturday average sits at 6.58%, and Mortgage Daily’s Optimal Blue-based figure eased three basis points to 6.54%. Freddie Mac chief economist Sam Khater pointed to the apparent end of the Middle East ceasefire as the driver behind the week’s modest uptick, and the Mortgage Bankers Association and Fannie Mae both continue to forecast rates holding in the mid-6% range through year-end. Tuesday’s June CPI print is the next data point with the clearest ability to move the needle either way.

Tax & Accounting

Kwong-Related Form 843 Refund Window Closed Thursday — What Comes Next for Taxpayers Who Missed It

Thursday, July 10 was the final day for most taxpayers to file Form 843 protective refund claims tied to COVID-era penalties under the Kwong v. United States decision, which remains on appeal. National Taxpayer Advocate Erin Collins had publicly criticized the compressed timeline as unfair to otherwise-eligible filers who may not have learned about the window in time; taxpayers who missed Thursday’s deadline will not be eligible for relief even if Kwong is ultimately upheld. Separately, small businesses still weighing retroactive research-and-experimentation expensing elections under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act should note that the July 6 election deadline has also now passed for most taxpayers, though normal refund-claim deadlines under IRC § 6511 may still apply in some cases — a point worth confirming with a preparer before assuming the window is fully shut.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

New Research Links Heat Waves to a Rise in Mental-Health Hospitalizations — Worth Knowing Before Next Week’s Heat Arrives

A multicountry study published this week in Nature Health, led by researchers at Monash University, examined more than 2.6 million warm-season hospitalizations across Brazil, Canada, Chile, and New Zealand and found that sustained exposure to heat waves was associated with a measurably increased risk of hospitalization for mental and behavioral disorders. The effect was more pronounced the longer a heat wave persisted, according to lead author Yuming Guo. With Chicagoland’s next heat wave building for Monday and Tuesday, the practical takeaway lines up with standard heat-safety guidance: stay hydrated well before you feel thirsty, seek air conditioning during peak afternoon hours, and check in on older relatives or neighbors who may be more vulnerable — heat stress affects the mind as much as the body.

Word of the Day
rapprochement

(noun, from French rapprocher, “to bring together”) — the establishment or resumption of cordial relations between parties, especially nations, after a period of estrangement or hostility. It’s the word diplomats reach for when two sides agree to keep talking without agreeing on much else — precisely the odd posture Washington and Tehran struck this week, continuing negotiations while each insists the other broke the peace.
