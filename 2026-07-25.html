Geopolitics · Markets · Saturday Edition

Drones Strike Northern Iraq and Sirens Sound in Bahrain After a 13th Night of U.S. Strikes on Iran — Wall Street Closes a Volatile Week Little Changed as Oil Pulls Back From $100

Explosive-laden drones targeted Irbil, the northern Iraqi city that hosts American military advisers, early Friday, with U.S.-led forces shooting down five of them and black smoke seen rising near the airport. The attack came hours after U.S. Central Command said it had finished a 13th consecutive night of strikes on Iran, this round hitting an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps naval base in the north, positions on Qeshm Island, and additional sites in Isfahan and southwestern Khuzestan province. Sirens also sounded in Bahrain and Jordan, both of which said they intercepted aerial attacks Friday, as the war that began with the February 28 assassination of Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei ground into its fifth month with no resolution in sight.

The diplomatic track took a step backward this week. Iranian and Iraqi officials confirmed Thursday that Tehran had rejected a ceasefire proposal President Trump delivered through Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi, and Trump told reporters Friday he is weighing a stronger response, though he also said he believes Iran is “getting more serious” about eventually reaching a deal and that he is “willing to listen.” Trump separately warned that sanctioned Iranian funds now in U.S. possession will be used to pay for repairing ships damaged in the Strait of Hormuz fighting, though the legal mechanism for that remains unclear. Saudi Arabia said it struck Yemen's Houthi rebels after the group hit another Saudi-linked vessel in the Red Sea, underscoring how the war's second front, opened this week with a Houthi blockade of Saudi ports, continues to widen the conflict's geographic footprint.

Wall Street closed a genuinely volatile week almost exactly where it started Friday, with the S&P 500 edging up 0.05% to 7,411.98 and the Dow adding 0.46% to 51,947.25 on a 3.5% jump in Apple, while the Nasdaq slipped 0.64% to 24,975.82 as chip and megacap tech names stayed under pressure following Thursday's rout. All three major indexes still finished the week lower, and Bank of America's Bull & Bear sentiment indicator — a contrarian gauge of investor positioning — hit its highest, most bullish reading since 2021, a level that has historically preceded selloffs. Stocks briefly extended gains at midday after Reuters reported, citing Pakistani sources, that Islamabad is exploring a China-initiated path toward renewed U.S.-Iran negotiations, though obstacles to any near-term breakthrough remain significant.

Oil did most of the week's work on sentiment. Brent crude, which briefly broke above $100 a barrel Thursday for the first time since May, pulled back roughly 4% Friday to trade below $96, while WTI settled near $89.31, down about 3%. The retreat came even as fighting around the Strait of Hormuz and the new Red Sea front continued, and it helped take some of the edge off a week in which Fed rate-hike odds for the July 29 meeting tripled from roughly 10.7% to close to 38%. S&P Global's flash PMI, released Friday, showed U.S. business activity expanding at its fastest pace in eight months, a reading some economists attributed partly to World Cup-related spending, while the White House exempted select energy products from a new round of tariffs as it tries to manage the inflationary fallout from elevated oil prices.

Irbil Attack

Explosive Drones Target Northern Iraqi City Hosting U.S. Advisers — No Casualties Reported

U.S.-led forces intercepted and shot down five explosive-laden drones over Irbil early Friday, with black smoke visible near the city's airport but no casualties or damage reported, according to security officials in Iraq's semiautonomous Kurdish region. The strike came hours after CENTCOM wrapped a 13th consecutive night of strikes on Iran, which hit a Revolutionary Guard naval base in the north, drone-boat facilities on Qeshm Island, and sites in Isfahan and Khuzestan provinces. The conflict, which began with the February 28 assassination of Khamenei, has increasingly devolved into a fight over control of the Strait of Hormuz rather than the original stated goals of regime change or eliminating Iran's nuclear program.

Sentiment Warning

BofA's Bull & Bear Indicator Hits Its Most Bullish Reading Since 2021 — A Signal That Has Preceded Selloffs

Bank of America's proprietary Bull & Bear Indicator, which tracks fund flows, hedge fund positioning, and market breadth, climbed to its highest and most bullish reading since 2021 this week, a threshold the bank's own research flags as a strong sell signal historically associated with subsequent market pullbacks. The reading arrives even as the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both logged their second consecutive weekly loss, illustrating a market that remains broadly positioned for a soft landing even as war-driven oil volatility and a live Fed decision sit one week out.

Earnings Wrap

Apple Jumps 3.5% to Anchor the Dow as Intel Slides Despite an Earnings Beat

Apple led Dow gainers Friday with a 3.5% advance, while Salesforce and IBM also contributed to the index's 235-point gain. Intel shares fell as much as 6.5-8% even after beating quarterly estimates, with investors instead focused on the chipmaker's heavy capital-spending plans. Sandisk dropped nearly 11% alongside other memory names, and Space Exploration Technologies slipped ahead of an upcoming test flight, underscoring how the second-quarter earnings season has repeatedly punished capex guidance even when the underlying results beat expectations.

Diplomacy Watch

Reuters: Pakistan Weighs a China-Initiated Path Toward Renewed U.S.-Iran Talks

Reuters reported Friday, citing three Pakistani sources, that Islamabad is considering a path toward establishing new peace negotiations between Washington and Tehran, with the initiative originating from Beijing. Stocks and oil both moved on the headline intraday before giving back some of the gain as reporters noted significant obstacles remain to any near-term breakthrough. The report follows Tehran's rejection this week of an Iraqi-brokered ceasefire proposal, leaving the diplomatic track fragile heading into the weekend.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

Countdown to July 29

Hike Odds Hold Near 38% Heading Into the Weekend as Warsh's First Live Decision Approaches

With four days to go before the July 28–29 FOMC meeting, CME FedWatch continues to price roughly a one-in-three-to-38% chance of a rate hike, up from just 10.7% on July 15 — a repricing driven almost entirely by oil's spike toward $100 a barrel this week. Economists surveyed by FactSet still expect the Fed to hold at 3.5–3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting, and Friday's pullback in Brent and WTI, if it holds into next week, would ease some of the pressure that built through Thursday. Fed Governor Lisa Cook has flagged inflation running at 3.7%, well above target, while Vice Chair Philip Jefferson and Governor Christopher Waller have both warned policy may need reconsidering if price pressures don't cool. Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference Wednesday at 2:30 PM ET, delivered with deliberately reduced forward guidance, is expected to do most of the market-moving work regardless of the decision itself, given investors currently have limited insight into his reaction function.

Geopolitics

Strait of Hormuz

Oil Retreats From $100 as Markets Wait to See Whether the Pullback Holds Into Next Week

Brent crude's brief run above $100 a barrel Thursday — its highest since May — gave way to a roughly 4% Friday retreat to below $96, with WTI settling near $89.31. The relief came even as fighting around the Strait of Hormuz and the Houthi blockade of Saudi ports continued, suggesting Friday's move reflected profit-taking and the Pakistan-mediation headline as much as any change in the underlying supply picture. Whether the pullback holds through the weekend will matter directly for the Fed's July 29 decision, given how directly this week's spike in hike odds tracked the oil price.

Second Front

Saudi Arabia Strikes Houthi Targets in Yemen After Rebels Hit Another Saudi-Linked Vessel

Saudi Arabia launched strikes against Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen after the group attacked another Saudi vessel in the Red Sea, the latest exchange in a maritime front that opened earlier this week alongside the Houthis' announced blockade of Saudi ports. Reuters has separately reported that Iran flew IRGC commanders and missile-related equipment into Yemen this month to support the campaign, a detail that reinforces U.S. officials' argument that Tehran bears direct responsibility for the Houthi escalation even as the main U.S.-Iran fight continues along the Gulf.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: The Job Market's Statistics No Longer Resemble Reality

Armstrong Economics flagged this week that the number of unemployed Americans has, for the first time since April 2021, outnumbered the number of available job openings — 7.24 million unemployed against 7.18 million openings as of the latest July data. Armstrong's read is structural rather than cyclical: companies are outsourcing work to lower-cost labor markets and automated systems rather than expanding, discouraged by rising wages, regulation, and a tax and interest-rate environment that makes debt-financed growth unattractive even as headline rates get discussed for cuts. A companion piece argues that every recovery eventually reaches a point where the official statistics no longer resemble reality — a recurring Armstrong theme heading into what his Economic Confidence Model still marks as 2026's Directional Change year, with the 2027 Panic Cycle escalation the next structural marker on the clock.

Saturday Rate Check

Freddie Mac's 30-Year Holds at a One-Year High of 6.58% as Daily Trackers Show Rates Easing Slightly

Freddie Mac's weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey, released Thursday, put the 30-year fixed-rate mortgage at 6.58% for the week ending July 23, its highest level since August 2025 and the third straight weekly increase, as Middle East tensions and inflation concerns keep pushing rates higher. Zillow's daily lender marketplace, by contrast, showed the average 30-year rate easing to 6.455% Friday, down nearly 6 basis points on the day, illustrating the usual lag between the application-weighted weekly benchmark and real-time daily quotes. The Mortgage Bankers Association expects 30-year rates to hold between 6.4% and 6.5% through the rest of 2026, while Fannie Mae's forecast sits at roughly 6.4%. With Friday's oil pullback and next week's Fed decision both live variables, the path for rates into August remains genuinely unsettled.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Scientists Identify the Molecular “Switch” That Explains Why Exercise Reverses Muscle Aging

Researchers have identified a gene called DEAF1 whose activity rises in aging muscle and appears to block the body's natural repair processes. Regular physical activity was found to reduce DEAF1 levels, effectively re-opening the pathway that allows older muscle tissue to clear out cellular damage and rebuild itself — a finding that helps explain, at the molecular level, why lifelong exercisers tend to retain more muscle function into old age than sedentary peers. The researchers stress the effect appears to require ongoing activity rather than a single bout of exercise; the switch, in other words, has to be flipped and kept flipped. It's a useful frame heading into a stretch of genuinely pleasant Chicago weather that won't last much past Saturday.

Random Fact

Irbil, the northern Iraqi city targeted by drones Friday, sits atop one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited settlements — the Erbil Citadel, a fortified mound occupied for roughly 6,000 years, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014. The city has hosted a U.S. and coalition military presence, on and off, since the 2003 invasion of Iraq, making it a recurring flashpoint whenever regional tensions escalate, as they did again this week.

Word of the Day
attrition

(noun, from Latin attritio, "a rubbing against") — the gradual wearing down of an opposing side's strength or resolve through sustained pressure, rather than a single decisive blow. Originally a term for physical erosion, it became military vocabulary during World War I's trench warfare and now describes wars, like this one, where the cumulative effect of many small strikes matters more than any single engagement. Thirteen consecutive nights in, that's precisely the phase this conflict is in.
