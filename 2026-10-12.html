Geopolitics · Iran · Monday Edition

Rubio Says the Strait of Hormuz Is Open and Nearly Back to Prewar Flows, Directly Contradicting Sunday's Collapse in Transit Data

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Monday that the Strait of Hormuz is open, that Iran has "lost control" of it, and that oil is flowing through the waterway at levels nearly matching those before the war. The claim lands one day after this edition reported the IMF's PortWatch recording just four transits through the strait on October 4, against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 a day — arguably the starkest version yet of this war's recurring pattern of flatly incompatible claims about the same stretch of water, now coming from the two sides within 48 hours of each other.

Iran's own position remains unchanged from Rubio's characterization: an IRGC adviser said the strait remains closed and that Tehran will soon move to block what it calls "illegal routes," while military adviser Yahya Rahim Safavi reiterated that Iran's actual aim is to restrict traffic rather than shut the waterway entirely. Tanker-strike counts are now reportedly approaching 20 vessels since the pattern of attacks began, and Saturday's reported naval mine strike remains uncorroborated outside Iranian state media.

Markets capped a volatile week with gains Friday, posting a fourth straight weekly advance for the Nasdaq: the S&P 500 rose 1.2% for the week, the Dow 0.9%, and the Nasdaq 0.6%, even as long-dated bond yields and oil prices swung sharply before easing into the close. Traders are now turning their attention to Wednesday's U.S. inflation report for the next signal on the Fed's path.

Bitcoin climbed back above $84,000 Monday as ETF selling eased and short positions unwound, though it remains down roughly 3.3% for the week as high Treasury yields and soft spot-ETF demand continue to weigh on crypto broadly. The 30-year mortgage rate's last reading holds at 7.40%, with Freddie Mac's next weekly survey due Thursday.

The Sharpest Contradiction Yet

"Nearly Prewar Flows" and "4 Transits" Cannot Both Describe the Same Strait

Two claims this incompatible, arriving within 48 hours of each other from an independent tracker and a sitting cabinet secretary, leave essentially no room for both to be good-faith readings of the same underlying reality.

A Claim Built for a Domestic Audience

"Lost Control" Is a Message About Resolve, Not Necessarily About Traffic

Framing the strait as functionally open serves a specific political purpose — demonstrating the blockade and pressure campaign are working — regardless of how closely that framing tracks the actual, measurable flow of ships through the waterway.

A Position Unchanged by the Dispute

Iran's Restriction-Not-Closure Framing Has Held Steady for Days

Tehran repeating the same "restrict, don't close" position regardless of what Washington claims suggests Iran's messaging strategy isn't reactive to American statements, but rather a fixed talking point it intends to hold regardless of the back-and-forth.

A Milestone With No Celebration

Approaching 20 Tanker Strikes Barely Registers as News Anymore

A running tally that would have been a shocking headline months ago now gets folded into a single ticker line, a reminder of how thoroughly sustained conflict can normalize numbers that would otherwise demand their own dedicated coverage.

Whose Numbers Do You Trust

Neither Side Has an Incentive to Report Accurately Here

Washington benefits from claiming success, Tehran benefits from claiming continued leverage, and the IMF's independent transit count is the only figure in this dispute without an obvious interest in which answer turns out to be true.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: When Official Claims Diverge This Sharply From Independent Data, Trust the Data

Armstrong Economics has long argued that in protracted conflicts, government statements from either side should be weighted far less heavily than data collected by parties with no stake in the outcome, since official claims reliably drift toward whatever narrative best serves each government's domestic audience. The site's War Cycle model treats the PortWatch transit figures as the more reliable signal in this specific dispute, by a wide margin.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Statins Linked to Lower Glaucoma Risk and Less Need for Treatment

A new study found that patients taking statins, typically prescribed to lower cholesterol, showed a reduced risk of developing glaucoma and a lower likelihood of needing treatment for the condition once diagnosed. Researchers say the findings add glaucoma to a growing list of conditions where statins appear to offer benefits beyond their primary cardiovascular use, though they caution that statins aren't currently prescribed specifically for eye health and more research is needed before that could change.
