Chicagoland Forecast — Monday, July 27
Tornado Warnings and 100 MPH Gusts Rake the Region Before a Cold Front Clears the Air for a Cooler, Drier Week

The severe weather the region had been bracing for all weekend arrived in force Monday: the National Weather Service issued a run of tornado warnings across the collar counties, from Glendale Heights and Downers Grove in the west to Orland Park, Tinley Park, and New Lenox in the south suburbs, as a line of tornado-capable storms tracked through between late morning and early afternoon. Hail up to 5 inches was reported near Lemont, wind gusts topped 100 mph in spots, and more than 200,000 ComEd customers lost power, with downed trees, a roof torn off a home in Lansing, and flight delays at both O'Hare and Midway. The good news: this was the trigger for the cold front the forecast had been pointing to, and it clears the humidity out behind it. Expect a cooler, drier stretch through Thursday with highs settling into the low-to-mid 80s—a welcome break for anyone still cleaning up storm debris, and good ballpark weather once the Cubs and White Sox are both back in action.

Geopolitics · Markets · Monday Edition

Iran and the U.S. Hold Fire for a Third Day as Trump Says “Good Things Can Happen” — Wall Street Steadies Into a Week of Fed and Big Tech Earnings

The pause that opened over the weekend held through a third consecutive day Monday, with no new U.S. strikes reported on Iran and Tehran's army confirming its own retaliatory operations remain suspended in kind. President Trump, talking to reporters aboard Air Force One, said Iran had reached out "through their surrogates and directly, and we're meeting," adding "we're talking with Iran right now" and that "good things can happen." Iran's Foreign Ministry pushed back within hours, a spokesman insisting Tehran has not requested a resumption of formal negotiations and that "mediators may convey messages to us from the American side," but that no direct talks are underway. The gap between Washington's framing and Tehran's is itself a familiar pattern from this war: both sides have repeatedly described the same lull in incompatible terms, even as the guns, for a third straight day, stayed quiet.

The diplomatic track running through Oman that surfaced over the weekend appears to be the substance behind the rhetoric, with regional sources describing continued shuttle efforts aimed at reopening the Strait of Hormuz and de-escalating the broader campaign. Whether "good things" means a durable ceasefire or another temporary hold remains the open question heading into a week that was already going to be consequential on its own terms: Wednesday brings the Fed's rate decision, and the biggest earnings reports of the year—Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon all report within days of each other, with capital-expenditure guidance likely to matter as much as headline profit.

Markets spent Monday digesting both threads at once. The Dow added 0.51% to 52,210.08 and the S&P 500 ticked up a scant 0.02% to 7,413.18, while the Nasdaq slipped 0.18% to 24,932.08 as semiconductor names sold off broadly—the VanEck Semiconductor ETF lost more than 2%, with AMD down 5% and Teradyne off 4%—and SanDisk plunged 11% on memory-market weakness. Gold climbed roughly 1% toward $4,100 an ounce, and oil eased further as the Iran pause continued to drain the risk premium that had been building through last week's spike toward $100 a barrel.

That oil retreat is doing real work on Fed expectations: CME FedWatch now puts the odds of a hold at Wednesday's meeting at 65.7%, with a quarter-point hike priced at roughly 35%—down from the nearly-38% hike odds that had built up last week on the back of the oil shock. A fifth consecutive hold at 3.50–3.75% remains the base case, but Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference will be read closely for how much the ceasefire dynamics, if they hold, factor into the committee's thinking. Back home, Monday's other headline story arrived from the sky rather than the wire services: a line of tornado-warned storms tore through the Chicago area, a reminder that the week's biggest immediate disruption for a lot of readers was more local than geopolitical.

Technical Picture — Monday

Bitcoin Opens Higher on Cease-Fire Optimism, Then Gives It All Back by Evening

Bitcoin jumped 1.6% at Monday's open to $65,333, tracking the weekend's Iran de-escalation headlines into the broader risk-on move across markets. The optimism didn't hold: by mid-morning the coin had slipped to around $64,974, and by evening it was trading near $63,754, a roughly 2.4% intraday round trip that left Bitcoin still inside the same range it has held for much of the past two weeks. Monday Market Check

June Existing-Home Sales Slip 2.4% as Rising Inventory Reshapes a “Rebalancing Year”

Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed rate remains at its one-year high of 6.58% as of the July 23 survey, with the next update due Thursday. The National Association of Realtors' June report showed existing-home sales at a 4.09 million annualized pace, down 2.4% from May but up 2.8% year-over-year, with the median sales price at $440,600 and 4.6 months of inventory on the market—more supply than a year ago, which analysts say is nudging 2026 toward a "rebalancing year" of better affordability even as rates stay elevated. Mortgage applications, meanwhile, have stayed unusually strong, up 7.8% year-over-year for the week ending July 10, suggesting buyers are adjusting to the new normal rather than waiting it out.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Shorter Daily Eating Window Linked to Better Planning and Problem-Solving in Older Women

A small six-month trial found that older women who limited their daily eating to an 8-to-9-hour window performed better on tests of planning and problem-solving than those who ate on a more typical schedule, adding to a growing body of research suggesting time-restricted eating may help protect certain cognitive skills as people age. Researchers caution the study was small and the mechanism isn't fully understood, but the results track with earlier work linking meal-timing changes to metabolic and brain-health benefits—worth filing away for anyone whose storm-disrupted Monday already threw their usual routine off schedule.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

The 5-inch hailstones reported near Lemont on Monday don't grow that large by accident: it takes an updraft powerful enough to hold a chunk of ice aloft for several minutes while it cycles through a storm's supercooled layer, adding a new layer of ice each pass, roughly the same physics that let a strong thunderstorm produce the 100 mph wind gusts reported the same afternoon. Hail that size needs updraft speeds well north of 100 mph just to stay suspended—meaning the same storm structure that shattered car windshields in the south suburbs was also the one flattening trees a few miles away.

Word of the Day
détente

(noun, from French "détente," a relaxing or loosening) — an easing of strained or hostile relations between parties, especially nations, typically achieved through diplomacy rather than resolution of the underlying dispute. A détente does not require either side to agree on what caused the thaw, or even on whether it is happening—only that, for now, both have stopped escalating. Monday's dueling U.S. and Iranian accounts of their own contacts were a near-perfect illustration: two governments in apparent détente who can't agree on what to call it.
