Geopolitics · Markets · Thursday Edition

Hormuz Deal “On the Verge of Being Finalised,” Iran Says — But Tolls Remain the Sticking Point

A day after Trump declared the broader Iran deal "complete," Thursday's details suggest the Strait of Hormuz piece specifically is close but not yet done. Iran said its agreement with Oman on reopening the strait is "on the verge of being finalised," with both countries confirming they've agreed on coordinates for inbound and outbound shipping routes after more than three weeks of negotiation. The talks have also produced progress on technical arrangements, questions of Iranian and Omani security and sovereignty, and a proposed joint coordination center to manage maritime traffic through the corridor.

The remaining gap is a real one. U.S. officials say the Trump administration wants total freedom of commercial navigation through Hormuz—no Iranian tolls, and no requirement that ships obtain Tehran's approval before transiting. Iran, for its part, has signaled it intends to introduce some form of fee system for vessels using the route. Iranian officials confirmed Thursday that the deal is close but insisted the U.S. must meet its own commitments before the strait fully reopens, a reminder that "close" and "finished" remain two different things in this negotiation, even after Wednesday's more sweeping announcement.

Trump, for his part, kept the tone upbeat, saying "a lot of progress has been made" on reopening the strait and suggesting a fuller announcement could come soon. CNN's read on the emerging arrangement was more pointed: the agreement taking shape is "not one Trump wants," a reference to the toll question that the administration has treated as close to a red line. Whether that gap gets resolved with a face-saving compromise or becomes the next flashpoint is very much still open.

Markets, having already priced in a great deal of good news this week, took a breather Wednesday: the Dow notched yet another record, closing at 54,349 on a 263-point gain, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both dipped modestly, with traders described as banking profits after a "massive rally" rather than reacting to any negative catalyst. Gold told a more interesting story, climbing toward $4,300 an ounce for a fourth consecutive gain and its best week in months—up nearly 6%—even as the Hormuz progress that's supposed to be disinflationary would typically argue against a safe-haven rally. Oil, more predictably, extended its slide to $74.79 as traders continue betting the strait genuinely reopens in some form.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

Transit tolls on international straits are far from unprecedented—Egypt has charged tolls on the Suez Canal since it opened in 1869, and Panama does the same for the canal bearing its name—but the Strait of Hormuz has never operated under a toll system precisely because it's classified as an international strait under maritime law, where the right of "transit passage" is generally understood to preclude the kind of fees Iran is now reportedly proposing.

Word of the Day
finalise

(verb, British spelling variant of "finalize," from Latin "finalis," of or relating to an end) — to complete the last details of something, making it official and settled. Iran's choice of "on the verge of being finalised" rather than simply "finalised" is doing real diplomatic work: it signals genuine progress while explicitly preserving room to walk back or renegotiate before the last signature is actually in place.
