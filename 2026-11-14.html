Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Labeled Drug Ads Shown to AI Chatbots Biased Their Medication Recommendations in Controlled Test

A Mount Sinai study published in The Lancet Digital Health tested twelve consumer-facing AI models — including systems from several major developers — against 33 physician-written clinical cases. Researchers found that placing a clearly labeled pharmaceutical advertisement before a clinical question raised the share of AI responses recommending the advertised drug from 35% to 47.6%. Notably, the ads did not appear to push clearly unsupported or suboptimal medications, and the bias did not reduce the AI systems' overall clinical accuracy — but it did measurably skew which appropriate drug got recommended. The researchers note they did not test whether this effect would translate into real-world prescribing or patient outcomes, but say it raises real questions as consumer-facing AI tools increasingly field health-related questions alongside advertising.

Word of the Day
confound

(verb, from Latin confundere "to pour together, mix up") — in research, a variable that obscures or distorts the true relationship between two other variables being studied. Today's AI drug-ad study was carefully designed to isolate advertising's effect while controlling for this kind of confound.
