Geopolitics · Markets · Thursday Edition

Trump Predicts the War Ends "Immediately" After the Midterms as Markets Post a Third Straight Losing Session and Brent Tops $101

President Trump offered his most specific public timeline yet for ending the war, telling supporters at the Republican midterm convention in Dallas Wednesday, "I think the war's going to end immediately after the election because they can't hold out any longer." In the same remarks, Trump floated renaming the Strait of Hormuz after himself: "We should call the Hormuz strait — we should call it the Trump — I should get something out of it — the Trump Strait." The comments landed as confidently upbeat as any the president has made about the conflict's trajectory since it began.

They also landed in direct tension with the reported assessment of his own intelligence community. Recent intelligence assessments reportedly indicate Tehran is prepared to continue fighting for months despite the severe economic pressure documented in recent editions — a collapsed export sector, near-70% inflation, and now oil prices that are rising rather than falling as the war grinds on. Whether "they can't hold out any longer" reflects genuine private intelligence or simply campaign-season optimism, the gap between the president's public timeline and the reported private assessment is itself notable.

The fighting gave little indication Wednesday of nearing any endpoint. Iran fired 20 ballistic missiles at Jordan's Al-Azraq air base, home to both the Royal Jordanian Air Force and U.S. personnel; Jordan's military said it intercepted 18, with the remaining two landing in unpopulated areas and no casualties reported. The IRGC said the strike destroyed hangars used by U.S. fighter jets. The salvo came within hours of the U.S. striking five more Iranian oil tankers, continuing the tanker-for-tanker exchange that has now drawn in a third country's territory as a battlefield.

Markets, for their part, are not yet pricing in Trump's optimism. Wednesday marked a third consecutive losing session for all three major indexes: the Dow fell 405.41 points (-0.77%) to 52,380.66, the S&P 500 dropped 0.48% to 7,636.36, and the Nasdaq eased 0.64% to 26,253.34, as Treasury yields climbed to fresh 52-week highs and Brent crude officially crossed $101 a barrel for the first time this war. Gold, notably, climbed back above $4,400 an ounce, ending its own three-session slide as a weakening dollar drew buyers back in, while Bitcoin ticked up toward $79,200. Thursday's producer-price data and Friday's CPI report now carry outsized weight heading into next week's Fed decision, arriving in the middle of a week that's delivered a presidential prediction, a cross-border missile strike, and a fresh oil-price milestone all at once.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Political Timelines and Battlefield Realities Rarely Move Together

Armstrong Economics has consistently warned against reading political predictions about a war's end as reliable forecasts, arguing that domestic electoral incentives and the actual conditions driving a conflict operate on entirely separate timelines. A presidential prediction tied explicitly to an election date, delivered the same week oil crosses a genuine new threshold, reads on the site as exactly the kind of mismatch the Panic Cycle framework treats as a red flag rather than a reassurance.
