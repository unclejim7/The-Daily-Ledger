Geopolitics · Markets · Monday Edition

US Strikes Iranian Mine-Laying Rockets on Larak Island as Hormuz Fighting Resumes and Warsh's Hawkish Jackson Hole Turn Sends Rate-Hike Odds to 57%

The fragile momentum behind Iran and Oman's proposed Hormuz shipping corridor collided head-on with renewed violence over the weekend. U.S. forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island Sunday — the first American strikes in over a month — after observing Iranian units preparing to launch rockets carrying sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said the "US-Zionist enemy" attack caused casualties among both forces and civilians and vowed retaliation; Tehran followed through overnight, striking U.S. allies elsewhere in the region.

The strikes reopen a dispute that the corridor talks had, for a few days, mostly papered over: who actually controls the strait. Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Saturday, before the U.S. strikes, that "the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, and any vessel passing through the strait does so in coordination with Iran." U.S. Central Command flatly rejects that framing, stating "Iran does not control the Strait of Hormuz" while confirming its own forces finished clearing sea mines from the waterway's international shipping routes just last week — the same claim Trump made a week ago that Iran's IRGC had disputed as premature.

All of this lands just three days after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's own market-moving turn at Jackson Hole. Warsh told the symposium Friday that "while this summer's PCE and CPI readings were better than expected, they do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved," a comment markets read as meaningfully hawkish; bets on a September rate hike spiked to 57%, and the 10-year Treasury yield has held near 4.716% ever since. Stocks fell Friday on the news (S&P 500 −0.25% to 7,711.76, Dow −0.02% to 53,559.99, Nasdaq −0.52% to 26,402.42), even as the S&P still closed out a winning week overall.

Monday's session opened lower across the board as investors weighed the resumed Hormuz fighting against Warsh's hawkish turn: oil jumped sharply on the strikes (WTI +3%, Brent +2%), while both Bitcoin and gold, this month's twin "debasement trade" beneficiaries, gave ground instead — Bitcoin slipping to around $78,000 and gold futures down 0.5% to about $4,510 — as higher rate expectations make holding non-yielding assets look less attractive, at least for now.

Diplomacy, Interrupted

A Week of Progress Undone in a Single Weekend

Iran and Oman spent last week building toward an actual corridor framework; a single set of U.S. strikes on rocket-launching sites is enough to reset the entire conversation back toward active hostilities, underscoring how fragile this war's diplomatic gains remain.

Preemption, Not Retaliation

The U.S. Struck Before the Mines Were Laid

Hitting the launchers while Iranian forces were still preparing to fire, rather than responding after mines were already in the water, is a meaningfully different posture — a preemptive strike rather than a retaliatory one, with its own distinct escalation risks.

The Core Dispute, Again

Two Governments, Two Incompatible Claims About the Strait

Gharibabadi's "remains closed" and CENTCOM's "does not control" statements can't both be true — the same basic factual disagreement that undercut last week's mine-clearance plan is now back at the center of an actual shooting exchange.

A Hawkish Handoff

Warsh's Jackson Hole Turn Still Setting the Tone Monday

Friday's hawkish surprise from the Fed chair is still the dominant force behind this week's rate expectations, even with a genuine military escalation now competing for markets' attention — a reminder that Fed policy signals can outlast a single geopolitical news cycle.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Long-Term Melatonin Use Linked to a 90% Higher Heart Failure Risk

A large preliminary study of people with chronic insomnia found that those who used melatonin supplements long term had a roughly 90% higher risk of developing heart failure over five years compared with non-users. Researchers caution the study is observational and can't prove melatonin itself causes the increased risk, since chronic insomnia patients may differ from non-users in other health-relevant ways, but say the finding warrants closer scrutiny of a supplement many people take for extended periods without much oversight.

Random Fact

Larak Island sits at one of the narrowest points of the Strait of Hormuz, just a few miles from the shipping lanes that carry roughly a fifth of the world's oil — making it a natural staging ground for exactly the kind of sea-mine dispersal operation U.S. forces say they interrupted Sunday.

Word of the Day
preemptive

(adjective, from Latin "praeemere," to buy before) — taken in advance to deter or prevent an anticipated action, rather than in response to one already carried out. Striking rocket launchers before mines were actually dispersed is the defining feature of a preemptive, rather than retaliatory, military action.
