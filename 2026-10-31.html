Markets · Geopolitics · Saturday Edition

Tech Selloff Deepens as Intel Craters 29% and Iran Claims a Fourth Tanker Struck a Mine in Hormuz

Friday's selloff didn't reverse Thursday's Alphabet-driven losses — it deepened them. The Nasdaq fell 2.86% to 25,437.00, the S&P 500 dropped 1.82% to 7,580.07, and the Dow shed 572 points (-1.09%) to 51,770.31, after Intel's disappointing earnings sent its stock down roughly 29%, the chipmaker's worst single-day drop since 1974, with Nvidia falling another 4.2% in sympathy. October closes as the Nasdaq's worst month since March 2025, down 5.2%, even as the Dow managed a 1.7% gain for the month on the back of its more diversified mix.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard escalated its Hormuz mine claims overnight, with the Iranian Army adding a fourth tanker to the three the IRGC Navy had already claimed were struck. U.S. Central Command again called the reports false, repeating that shipping lanes remain clear. IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebi fired back directly at that denial: "Prove the claim that the Strait of Hormuz has been cleared of mines with independent evidence, not a statement." Saudi Arabia, meanwhile, has now gone five days without confirming or denying the original Houthi claim of a strike on the Ras Tanura refinery.

Brent crude climbed toward $117 a barrel on the renewed tension, extending the week's gains. Bitcoin, which hit a fresh six-week low near $76,400 Friday after Monday's $6.3 billion Binance hack, found a fragile footing Saturday near $77,100; Tether and Circle have frozen funds tied to the stolen assets, and Yahoo Finance reports North Korean hackers are suspected in the breach. The 30-year mortgage holds at 7.34%, with no new Freddie Mac reading until next Thursday.

With the midterms three days away, nearly every thread in today's edition — markets, oil, Hormuz — runs through that same pre-election window. Milwaukee, for its part, still hasn't announced a date for its World Series victory celebration.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

CDC: Lifetime ADHD Diagnoses Among U.S. School-Age Children Rose to 13.8%

A new CDC report found that lifetime ADHD diagnoses among U.S. school-age children rose from 9.9% in 2019 to 13.8% in 2025, a substantial jump over six years. Researchers have not pinned down a single explanation for the increase, which likely reflects some combination of greater awareness, broader diagnostic criteria, and improved access to evaluation, alongside any genuine change in underlying prevalence.
