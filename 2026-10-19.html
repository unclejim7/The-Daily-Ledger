Geopolitics · Iran · Monday Edition

CENTCOM Says 97% of Iran's Hormuz Attacks Fail, as Tehran Claims Two More Tankers Hit Mines

The dueling-narrative pattern that's defined this conflict for weeks sharpened again Monday. CENTCOM spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins said “the vast majority of Iran's attempted attacks on innocent civilian mariners transiting an international waterway, being the Strait of Hormuz, they are unsuccessful,” putting the failure rate at roughly 97%. CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper added that roughly 400 merchant ships transited the strait in the preceding seven days alone — a volume meant to illustrate that the waterway remains functionally open despite the ongoing attacks.

Iran's account of the same week reads very differently. Tehran's Revolutionary Guard navy said two oil tankers, each reportedly carrying around 2 million barrels of crude, caught fire Sunday after striking naval mines in the strait, blaming the vessels for attempting an “illegal” transit route with GPS and radar disabled. The claim has not been independently verified by Western sources, continuing a pattern in which Iran's own account of specific incidents routinely goes unconfirmed even as the broader attack campaign itself is well documented.

Markets, meanwhile, attempted a rebound Monday after last week's worst stretch since late June, with tech stocks leading a recovery bid and premarket futures pointing sharply higher after the prior week's AI-valuation selloff. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, speaking at the IMF and World Bank meetings in Bangkok, offered no hints on his intentions ahead of the October 27–28 decision: “I don't believe in saying now what I might do at the meetings that we have in the balance of this year.” That deliberate silence has left rate-hike odds scattered wildly across different models, ranging from the low teens to roughly even depending on the source.

Bitcoin rebounded to roughly $82,700 as risk appetite broadly returned Monday morning. The 30-year mortgage rate holds at 7.46% ahead of Thursday's reading. In Chicago sports, the Bears routed the Falcons behind a returning Caleb Williams, while the Rays took a 3-2 ALCS lead over the Guardians with tonight's Game 6 able to send Tampa Bay to the World Series against Milwaukee.

Geopolitics

Two Audiences, Two Messages

CENTCOM Is Talking to Markets; Iran Is Talking to Its Own Public

The "97% success" framing is designed to reassure shippers and investors that the strait functions; Iran's granular mine-strike claims are designed to project ongoing capability and resolve to a domestic audience — both messages can be true to their intended purpose without directly contradicting each other's facts.

A Volume Argument

400 Ships in a Week Is CENTCOM's Strongest Rebuttal Yet

Raw transit volume is a harder number for Iran to dispute than percentage framing alone, and citing it directly suggests US Central Command is increasingly leaning on concrete throughput data rather than qualitative claims about the strait being "open."

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Competing Statistics Are Themselves a War Cycle Tactic

Armstrong Economics has long observed that belligerents in a prolonged conflict eventually shift from military claims to dueling statistics, each selected to support a preferred narrative to a specific audience. The site's War Cycle model treats this week's CENTCOM-versus-Iran numbers exchange as evidence the conflict has entered exactly that kind of information-war phase.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Regular Late-Night Eating Linked to a 20–30% Higher Risk of Fatty Liver Disease

Researchers tracking close to 200,000 people found that those who regularly ate close to bedtime had a 20 to 30 percent higher risk of developing fatty liver disease compared with those who finished eating earlier in the evening. The researchers say the finding adds to a growing body of evidence that meal timing, not just overall diet quality, plays a meaningful role in metabolic and liver health, though they caution the study cannot establish causation on its own.

Random Fact

A 97% success rate sounds nearly total until applied to volume: against roughly 400 weekly transits, even a 3% interruption rate implies more than ten disrupted or attacked passages a week, a figure that sounds far less reassuring than the headline percentage alone.

Word of the Day
throughput

(noun, from through + put) — the amount of material or traffic passing through a system in a given time. CENTCOM's 400-ships-per-week figure is a throughput statistic, designed to describe overall flow rather than any single incident.
