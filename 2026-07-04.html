National Affairs · Geopolitics · Independence Day Edition

America Turns 250: A Record-Shattering Fireworks Show and Historic Heat Mark the Semiquincentennial as Iran Pauses for a Funeral

The United States marks 250 years of independence today with the largest fireworks display in world history, a punishing heat wave gripping much of the country, and a global backdrop unusually quiet given the two active fronts — Wall Street and Tehran — that have driven headlines all year. Tonight's "Salute to America" show on the National Mall will attempt to launch roughly 850,000 shells across ten sites in just 40 minutes, a bid to eclipse the current Guinness World Record of 810,904 fireworks set at a 2016 New Year's countdown celebration in the Philippines. New York's Macy's show is also going bigger than usual, planning 85,000 shells in 30 colors for its own commemoration of the nation's Semiquincentennial.

The celebration is unfolding under genuinely dangerous heat. Washington, DC has an extreme heat alert in effect through July 5, with a forecast high of 101°F that would make today the hottest Fourth of July on record for the capital, surpassing the 100°F mark set in 1919. President Trump, who is expected to deliver lengthy remarks at the Mall event, dismissed concerns about the conditions earlier this week: "On July Fourth, it's going to be approximately 107 degrees out, and I'm going to go, and I'm going to make a really long speech just to show that I can do anything." Event organizers have added misting stations and complimentary water refills, and the U.S. Capitol Police pushed back gate times for related concert events to account for the heat. Meteorologists also warn that the combination of record heat and history-making pyrotechnics could produce hazardous short-term air quality around the Mall, with ground-level ozone and fireworks particulate matter both expected to spike into Sunday morning.

The milestone lands at an unusually still moment on two of the year's dominant storylines. Wall Street is closed for the weekend after Thursday's record Dow close, leaving traders nothing to react to until Monday. And in Tehran, the multi-day state funeral for assassinated Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei began in earnest today at the Grand Mosalla, with organizers expecting turnout as high as 35 million people over six days of processions across Iran and Iraq before his burial in Mashhad on July 9. The parallel pause in U.S.-Iran diplomacy at Doha means both of the year's defining forces — markets and the war — are effectively holding their breath at the same time the country pauses to celebrate.

Locally, the holiday brings its own contrasts: a Cubs team coming off a 17-1 blowout loss hosts the Cardinals at Wrigley tonight, the White Sox fight for first place in Cleveland, and a real chance of evening thunderstorms threatens to complicate fireworks plans across Chicagoland just as it does in the nation's capital.

Geopolitics

Iran — State Funeral

Khamenei's Six-Day Funeral Begins in Tehran as Diplomacy Pauses and Iran Warns Against "Any Miscalculation"

Funeral processions for assassinated Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, killed in the U.S.-Israeli strike that opened the Iran war on February 28, are underway across Tehran, Qom, and the Iraqi cities of Najaf and Karbala, ahead of his burial in Mashhad on July 9. Officials expect turnout as high as 35 million people, which would make it the largest state funeral in Iranian history; mourners gathered under water-mist fans at the Grand Mosalla in Tehran on Saturday as temperatures reached roughly 97°F. It remains unclear whether Khamenei's son and successor, Mojtaba, will make his first public appearance since being wounded in the same strike that killed his father. Iran's military has warned the U.S. and Israel against any attack during the processions, and CENTCOM has confirmed a second Marine Expeditionary Unit's ships are en route to the region as a precaution.

Iran — Doha Track

U.S.-Iran Talks Paused for the Funeral After "Positive Progress" in Two Days of Doha Meetings

Qatari and Pakistani mediators say indirect U.S.-Iran discussions held this week in Doha made "positive progress" on issues tied to the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, with both delegations agreeing to reconvene "at the earliest possible time" once funeral proceedings conclude. Key disputes remain unresolved, including Iran's reported intent to impose tolls on vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz starting in mid-August and the unfreezing of Iranian assets abroad. Speaking at Mount Rushmore Friday as part of America's 250th commemorations, President Trump said of Iran: "They're dying to settle, they want to settle so badly."

Tax & Accounting

Six Days Left: COVID-Era Penalty Refund Claims Under the Kwong Decision Are Due July 10

The National Taxpayer Advocate's deadline for claiming refunds or abatements of penalties and interest the IRS assessed during the roughly 3.5-year COVID-19 disaster period is now six days away. Relief is not automatic: individuals, small businesses, estates, and trusts must file Form 843, Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement, by July 10 to preserve their rights following the Kwong v. United States decision, which calls into question the IRS's authority to assess those charges. Taxpayers with unresolved uncertainty are encouraged to file protective claims in the meantime, which don't require an exact dollar figure but must describe the relevant contingencies.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Fireworks and Record Heat Are a Dangerous Combination Today — A Few Basics Go a Long Way

Emergency rooms typically see roughly double their average daily traffic on the Fourth of July, and this year's combination of record-setting heat and unusually large fireworks displays raises the stakes further. Doctors recommend hydrating well before symptoms of heat stress appear, since thirst is already a lagging indicator, and treating alcohol as something that accelerates dehydration rather than relieves it. On fireworks specifically, the safest approach is watching a professional display from at least 500 feet away rather than lighting personal fireworks, which the Consumer Product Safety Commission notes caused roughly 10,200 injuries and 11 deaths in a recent year, with hands, fingers, and eyes the most commonly affected. Anyone who does use legal fireworks should keep a hose or bucket of water within reach, never relight a dud, and fully soak spent fireworks before disposal to prevent smoldering debris fires. If you're grilling, keep raw and cooked food on separate plates and hold hot food above 140°F until served.

Random Fact

Despite its name, Independence Day doesn't mark the day the Declaration of Independence was actually signed. The Continental Congress voted for independence on July 2, 1776, and adopted the final text of the Declaration on July 4 — but most delegates didn't put pen to paper until the engrossed copy was ready on August 2, 1776, with a few signatures trickling in even later. John Adams was so convinced July 2 would be the date future generations celebrated that he wrote to his wife Abigail predicting fireworks and parades — he just had the date wrong by two days.

Word of the Day
semiquincentennial

(noun/adjective, from Latin semi- "half" + quincentennial "500th anniversary") — a 250th anniversary, marking the halfway point to a quincentennial. Today marks the Semiquincentennial of American independence, the occasion behind tonight's record-attempt fireworks display on the National Mall and celebrations across the country.
