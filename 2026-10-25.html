Federal Reserve · Geopolitics · Sunday Edition

Two Days From the Fed's Verdict, Markets Hold Steady as Aramco's Evacuation Drags On With No Return Date

The Federal Reserve's Open Market Committee meets Tuesday and Wednesday, with a decision due Wednesday at 2 p.m. ET under Chair Kevin Warsh. With two days to go, Warsh has offered no fresh signal beyond his now-familiar line: “I don't prejudge meetings.” Markets remain closed for the weekend, leaving Friday's second straight losing session to stand.

In Saudi Arabia, Aramco's evacuation of its Dhahran headquarters — ordered Thursday after the strike on Dammam's airport — remains in effect, with no timeline yet for when staff will return. Houthi claims of striking Saudi Arabia's Defense Ministry headquarters in Riyadh remain unconfirmed by Saudi authorities; the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted four drones over Riyadh and Madinah without confirming the specific targets the Houthis claimed to have hit, continuing a now-familiar gap between dramatic claims and verified outcomes on all sides of this conflict.

Bitcoin dipped to a weekly low near $79,200 Saturday before recovering to trade around $80,800 Sunday morning, as the asset continues to churn in a wide range ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision. The 30-year mortgage holds at 7.38%, with no new weekly reading until Thursday.

Chicago had plenty to celebrate this weekend regardless: the Brewers beat the Rays 5-4 Saturday night to take a 2-0 World Series lead in the first Fall Classic game played in Milwaukee in 44 years, with Game 3 following Monday at Tampa Bay. And the Bears, idle this weekend, enter their bye on a high note after beating the Patriots 30-24 Thursday behind Caleb Williams's 308-yard, two-touchdown night, improving to 5-2 ahead of a November 2 trip to Seattle.

The Silence Has a Purpose

Warsh's Discipline Is Itself Becoming the Story

Most incoming Fed chairs face pressure to offer at least directional hints before a contested meeting; Warsh's consistent refusal, repeated across multiple public appearances now, suggests a deliberate strategy to keep markets from front-running Wednesday's outcome.

An Open-Ended Evacuation

"No Timeline Yet" Is a Different Kind of Signal Than a Quick Return

Companies typically want to normalize operations as fast as possible after a security scare; Aramco's open-ended timeline suggests the threat assessment inside the company remains unresolved nearly a week after the initial strike.

Interception Without Confirmation

Four Drones Downed Doesn't Tell Us What They Were Aimed At

The coalition's intercept count establishes that an attack was attempted, but declining to confirm the Houthis' specific target claims leaves genuine ambiguity about whether the Defense Ministry was actually in the crosshairs or whether that detail is itself part of the information war.

A Good Weekend for Chicago, Despite Everything Else

Two Winning Storylines Arrived Right When the City Needed Them

Between the Bears' bounce-back win and the Brewers' statement series lead just up the road, this was a weekend where sports offered the kind of uncomplicated good news that's been scarce in this edition's other sections lately.

A Lull That Isn't Resolution

No New Strikes Overnight Doesn't Mean the Situation Has Calmed

A quiet Saturday night follows one of the most dramatic weeks of this conflict yet; given the pattern established over months, a pause is far more likely to be a breath before the next development than a genuine de-escalation.

Verification Remains the Scarce Resource

Every Side's Claims Keep Outrunning What Anyone Can Independently Confirm

Between Houthi strike claims, Iranian mine assertions, and competing shipping-traffic statistics, the gap between what's claimed and what's verified has become the conflict's defining feature — arguably more consequential at this point than any single incident.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Markets Often Price Calm Before a Big Decision as Confidence, Not Caution

Armstrong Economics has observed that markets heading into a major policy decision often mistake quiet trading for settled expectations, when it just as often reflects participants waiting on the sidelines rather than genuine consensus. The site's model treats this weekend's calm, ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision and amid an unresolved Saudi security crisis, as exactly that kind of fragile lull.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

GLP-1 Drug Use Linked to Fewer Acute Psychiatric Care Visits Among Schizophrenia Patients

A cohort study tracking more than 70,000 people with schizophrenia found that those using GLP-1 agonist medications had fewer acute psychiatric care visits compared with those not taking the drugs. Researchers say the finding adds to growing interest in GLP-1 medications' effects beyond weight management and diabetes, though they caution that the observational design cannot establish that the drugs directly caused the reduction, and further controlled research will be needed to confirm any causal relationship.
