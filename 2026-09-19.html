Geopolitics · National Security · Saturday Edition

Trump Says He's at a "Major Crossroads" as Satellite Images Show Iran Reinforcing Its Buried Nuclear Site Ahead of Tuesday's Gulf Summit

President Trump told Axios he's approaching a "major crossroads" in the Iran war over whether to restart large-scale strikes in an effort to force the conflict to a close, warning Tehran not to "get cute" as new satellite imagery emerged showing fresh construction and hardened defenses at Pickaxe Mountain, Iran's deeply buried tunnel complex near the Natanz enrichment site. Analysts at CSIS and the Institute for Science and International Security say 2026 has brought more construction activity to the site than at any point in its history, including additional earthen cover over tunnel portals meant to harden them against exactly the kind of bunker-buster strike Trump has repeatedly threatened.

The timing lands the same week Washington approved a potential $24.3 billion sale of 48 F-35 stealth fighters and associated engines to Saudi Arabia, a deal the administration is advancing as the kingdom absorbs intensifying Houthi attacks, including the group's claimed downing of a Saudi F-15 over Marib. Congress now has 30 days to scrutinize the sale, with some lawmakers citing concerns over sensitive technology transfer and Riyadh's ties to China; Israel, currently the only regional operator of the F-35, has separately voiced unease about the jet reaching a neighbor.

Diplomatically, China is positioning itself as a potential bridge between the two sides: Foreign Minister Wang Yi told his Iranian counterpart, Abbas Araghchi, in Beijing this week that China stands ready to play a "constructive role" in resolving the conflict, even as Beijing continues buying the bulk of Iran's sanctioned oil exports. Whether that dual posture — economic lifeline and diplomatic mediator at once — gives China real leverage over Tuesday's outcome, or simply reflects its own competing interests, remains an open question heading into next week's summit.

Markets closed out a volatile, Fed-hike-driven week on a mixed note Friday: the Dow slipped 95.40 points (-0.18%) to 51,682.64 while the S&P 500 edged up 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq gained 0.39% to 26,522.55, as traders digested elevated Treasury yields and oil prices alongside the week's rate decision. Bitcoin pushed back toward $79,000, gold held near $4,375 heading into the weekend close, and the 30-year mortgage rate remained at 6.95% with no new weekly reading due until Thursday.

A Site Built to Survive

Pickaxe Mountain Was Designed for Exactly This Moment

Built after 2020 sabotage at Natanz specifically to house replacement centrifuge production underground, Pickaxe Mountain's granite construction and estimated 100-meter burial depth were engineered from the start to resist the kind of strike now being publicly threatened against it.

A Familiar Middle East Pattern

Selling Advanced Jets to One Side Rarely Stays Contained

Israel's objection to the Saudi F-35 sale echoes a decades-old regional dynamic in which any shift in the balance of advanced weaponry among neighbors, even ostensible partners, tends to ripple well beyond the original transaction.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: China's Iran Diplomacy Reflects Great-Power Positioning, Not Peacemaking

Armstrong Economics has argued that Beijing's engagement with Tehran is best read through the lens of great-power competition rather than genuine mediation, noting that China's deep economic interdependence with Iran — including buying roughly ninety percent of its oil exports, settled in renminbi — gives it every incentive to keep the relationship stable without ever committing to a costly resolution. The site's analysis sees no indication Beijing would deploy troops or take on real risk; its "constructive role" is more likely to mean managed ambiguity than an actual settlement.
