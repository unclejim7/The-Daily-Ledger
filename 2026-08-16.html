Geopolitics · Sunday Edition

Israel Kills at Least 11 in Deadliest Lebanon Strikes Since June Truce as Iran's Military Vows “Complete Defeat”

Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon killed at least 11 people, including three children, in what Lebanese officials described as the deadliest raids since the deals that eased Israel-Hezbollah hostilities in June. Israel's military said the strikes targeted Hezbollah, including one hitting a commander it accused of using his family as "human shields," in retaliation for an explosive-drone attack that seriously injured three Israeli troops, including an officer, inside a security zone in southern Lebanon. Lebanon's health ministry separately reported four more people killed and 17 wounded, including a child and 11 women, in a strike on Deir Zahrani. The bombardment sent hundreds of families fleeing north, clogging roads out of the border region.

The flare-up landed the same day Iran's Armed Forces General Staff issued its own hardline statement, marking the anniversary of Iranian POWs' return from the 1980s Iran-Iraq war by vowing "we will not back down from the legitimate demands of the nation and the Leader until the US-Israeli enemies are fully defeated in the region." It's a notably more absolute framing than the negotiating-table language Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has used around the Oman shipping talks, which he said Sunday continue but still depend on the U.S. upholding June's memorandum of understanding.

All of it lands two days before that same MoU's 60-day window expires Tuesday, August 18. Maritime trackers now count 56 separate reports of vessel damage in and near the Strait of Hormuz since the war began February 28, while separately, Yemeni government forces say they carried out 181 strikes against the Iran-backed Houthis in the past 24 hours — a reminder that this conflict now runs across at least three active fronts simultaneously: the strait itself, Lebanon, and Yemen.

Markets remain closed for the weekend, leaving Friday's close (S&P 500 at 7,785.76) as the most recent read on sentiment; Bitcoin held roughly steady overnight near $62,978. Armstrong Economics has argued for weeks that this war's defining feature is its tendency to expand into new theaters rather than resolve in any one of them, and Saturday's Lebanon escalation — arriving during the same week as record U.S. crude stockpile lows, an unprecedented-sanctions preview, and a territorial claim over Hormuz itself — reads as confirmation rather than surprise under that framework.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Gene Discovery Explains How a Single Mutation Unravels Heart DNA Structure

Researchers have found that TBX5, a gene already linked to congenital heart disease, acts like an architect for heart cells' DNA, and that losing just one copy of it causes the genome's carefully folded three-dimensional structure to unravel, disrupting the genes needed to build a healthy heart. The finding helps explain why even a single defective copy of certain genes can produce serious congenital heart defects, and researchers say it could point toward new diagnostic or therapeutic approaches focused on genome architecture rather than individual genes alone.
