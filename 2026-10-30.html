Bitcoin · Markets · Geopolitics · Friday Edition

Bitcoin Crashes to a Six-Week Low After a $6.3 Billion Binance Hack, as Iran Claims New Mine Strikes in the Strait of Hormuz

Binance disclosed Thursday that it suffered a security breach affecting roughly $6.3 billion in crypto assets — the largest exchange hack on record, eclipsing last year's $1.5 billion Bybit breach. The exchange says the intrusion traced to a compromised employee laptop that enabled unauthorized withdrawals from hot wallets, and that it has reserved $6.34 billion in company funds to cover the loss while it works to recover roughly $3 billion still frozen. Bitcoin tumbled on the news, falling from roughly $80,600 Thursday morning to a six-week low near $77,400, with market-wide liquidations topping $1.5 billion in 24 hours.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard separately claimed several oil tankers caught fire Thursday after striking mines along what Tehran called an "unauthorized" transit route through the Strait of Hormuz — directly disputing a recent U.S. CENTCOM claim that American forces had cleared mines from the strait's primary shipping lanes. A UK maritime agency separately reported a small boat attaching explosive devices to a tanker near Khasab, Oman. Saudi Arabia still has not directly confirmed or denied Tuesday's Houthi claim of a strike on the Ras Tanura refinery, now going on four days without an official answer either way.

Markets had already turned lower Thursday before any of this broke, with the S&P 500 snapping a three-day win streak to close down 0.3% at 7,720.39, the Nasdaq falling 1.4% to 26,184.72, and the Dow slipping 0.25% to 52,342.56. The drag was almost entirely Alphabet, which plunged 12.6% — its worst day ever, erasing roughly $500 billion in market value — after reporting negative free cash flow and 2027 capital-spending guidance near $300 billion that spooked investors already nervous about AI-spending sustainability. Microsoft's results, by contrast, were strong, with Azure revenue up 48%. Brent crude eased to $114.88 Thursday on hopes tied to renewed diplomacy, before reportedly climbing back toward $117 in early Friday trading on the fresh Hormuz mine claims.

Amid all of it, Milwaukee is still basking in Wednesday night's World Series championship, and the Blackhawks host Montreal tonight at the United Center.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Contested Narratives Are a Feature of This Phase, Not a Bug

Armstrong Economics has argued that as a conflict matures, the information war becomes as central as the kinetic one — competing claims about mines, strikes, and diplomacy aren't noise obscuring the real story, they are increasingly the story. Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Prenatal Wildfire-Smoke Exposure Linked to Higher Autism Risk

Research published in JAMA Pediatrics, drawing on Medicaid records covering more than 893,000 mother-child pairs, found that each incremental increase in a mother's average wildfire smoke exposure during pregnancy was associated with roughly a 30% higher risk of an autism diagnosis in the child, with the strongest association in the second and third trimesters. The study found no significant link to ADHD and, as an observational analysis, cannot establish that the exposure directly causes autism — but researchers say it adds to growing evidence that wildfire smoke's health effects may extend well beyond the respiratory system.

Word of the Day

(adjective, from Latin custos "guardian") — relating to the safekeeping of assets on another's behalf.
