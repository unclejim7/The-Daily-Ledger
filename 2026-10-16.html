Markets · Iran · Friday Edition

Tech Stocks Sink on Renewed AI-Bubble Fears as Oil Closes at Its Highest Level Since May

A broad technology sell-off dragged the Nasdaq down 2.1% to 26,078.60 Friday, its worst session in weeks, as renewed worries about AI-related valuations hit megacap names across the board. The S&P 500 fell 1.1% to 7,612.47 and the Dow slipped 0.3% to 51,168.63, though the blue-chip index still managed a 0.7% gain for the week even as the broader market logged its worst week since late June. The selling compounds the pressure that began Wednesday with a hotter-than-expected inflation report and has only intensified since.

Oil closed at its highest level since May as the Hormuz crisis ground on without any single dramatic new incident but also without any sign of easing: maritime trackers counted roughly ten vessels attacked in and near the strait over the past week alone, a pace analysts describe as a wartime high. Gold, meanwhile, climbed to $4,181 an ounce as Treasury yields and the dollar both pulled back from earlier in the week, with the 10-year yield easing to around 5.19% as some of Wednesday's spike unwound.

Federal Reserve rate-hike odds for the October 27–28 meeting now sit close to a coin flip, up sharply from roughly one-in-four before Wednesday's CPI print — a remarkable shift in sentiment for a central bank that was widely expected to be cutting by now. Markets head into the weekend with genuinely two-sided uncertainty about the Fed's next move for the first time in months.

Bitcoin extended its slide to roughly $79,800 as the broader risk-off mood showed no sign of letting up. The 30-year mortgage rate holds at 7.46%, with housing economists increasingly describing the run-up as a serious drag on the fall selling season; Bright MLS chief economist Lisa Sturtevant called it “a significant headwind,” noting sellers are already cutting prices and offering more concessions to attract buyers.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Global New Dementia Cases Projected to More Than Double by 2050

A study published in JAMA Neurology, led by researchers at UNSW Sydney pooling data from 22 population-based cohorts across 18 countries, projects that new dementia cases diagnosed each year will rise from roughly 11.4 million in 2023 to 24.7 million annually by 2050. The researchers found incidence is higher among women than men and substantially higher in low- and middle-income countries, underscoring what they describe as a widening global gap in dementia risk and care access.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

Gold and Treasury yields usually move in opposite directions, since gold pays no interest of its own — when yields fall, as they did Friday, the opportunity cost of holding gold instead of interest-bearing bonds drops too, making gold relatively more attractive.

Word of the Day
incidence

(noun, from Latin incidere "to fall upon") — the rate of new cases of a condition diagnosed within a given period, as distinct from prevalence, the total number of existing cases at any one time. Today's dementia study measures incidence specifically, not the larger, more commonly cited prevalence figures.
