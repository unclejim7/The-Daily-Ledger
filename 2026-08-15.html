Geopolitics · Saturday Edition

Trump Says He'll Declare the Strait of Hormuz U.S. “Territory” — Iran: “Cannot Be Seized by Tweet”

Speaking Friday at the Nassau County Police Academy in New York, President Trump made his most sweeping Hormuz claim yet: "After we finish defeating Iran... pretty soon I'll be declaring the Hormuz Strait a territory of the United States." The remark builds on his earlier suggestion that the U.S. could act as "guardian" of the strait while collecting a 20% fee on transiting cargo — an idea legal experts have already said would violate international law's protections for free commercial passage, since Iran shares jurisdiction over the waterway with Oman rather than holding it alone.

Iran's response was immediate and pointed. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said "the Strait of Hormuz has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian," adding that it "cannot be seized by tweet, nor by aircraft carrier, nor by issuing an order, nor by an election speech" and that Iran is "neither afraid of threats nor intimidated by a show of force." NBC News reported Iran separately dismissed the territorial idea outright as "delusions." The rhetorical gap between the two governments has arguably never been wider.

The words arrived alongside more physical evidence the underlying conflict hasn't cooled: CNN reported another vessel was reportedly struck in the strait Friday, with Reuters noting no breakthroughs have emerged in efforts to revive June's interim peace framework. The cumulative toll from the broader 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis now stands at no fewer than 17 merchant ships damaged and 12 seafarers killed or missing since the war began in February — a running count that keeps climbing three days before the 60-day Oman-Iran memorandum of understanding expires Sunday, August 18.

Markets closed out the week Friday on a softer note (S&P 500 −0.2% to 7,785.76) after a preliminary University of Michigan reading showed consumer sentiment souring on persistent inflation worries, even as the index still notched its third straight weekly gain and the Russell 2000 hit a fresh record. Armstrong Economics is treating Trump's territorial rhetoric skeptically as messaging rather than strategy, noting that neither side's escalating word choices this week have been matched by any corresponding shift in the physical facts on the water — the strait remains functionally closed regardless of which flag either government insists belongs over it.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Large Study Links Estrogen-Only Hormone Therapy to Lower Dementia Risk

A study of more than 21,000 participants found that women who used estrogen-only hormone therapy later in life were less likely to develop dementia and showed fewer brain markers associated with Alzheimer's disease compared with non-users. Researchers cautioned the therapy isn't appropriate for everyone and carries its own risks that must be weighed individually, but the scale of the study adds meaningful weight to a body of research increasingly questioning older, more cautious guidance around hormone therapy in menopause and beyond.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

The Strait of Hormuz was never a single country's exclusive territory even before this crisis: it sits within the territorial waters of both Iran and Oman, with a narrow international shipping corridor running between them under UNCLOS rules on innocent passage — a legal structure that makes any unilateral "territory" claim by a third country like the U.S. exceptionally difficult to square with existing maritime law, whatever military presence backs it up.

Word of the Day
sovereignty

(noun, from Old French "soverain," supreme) — supreme authority over a territory, and the right to govern it without external interference. Trump's territorial claim and Iran's rebuttal are, at bottom, a fight over exactly this word — and it's telling that both sides are now willing to invoke it directly rather than continue trading euphemisms like "control" and "very close."
