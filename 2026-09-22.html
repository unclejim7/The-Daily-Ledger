Geopolitics · Diplomacy · Tuesday Edition

Trump Tells the UN Iran "Was the Bully of the Middle East, but No Longer" as Gulf Leaders Meeting Ends With Little Optimism

President Trump used his address to the UN General Assembly Tuesday to declare that Iran, which he called the "number one sponsor of terror," was once "the bully of the Middle East, but no longer," while stressing that the regime must never be allowed to obtain a nuclear weapon. The speech, previewed by U.N. Ambassador Mike Waltz as a showcase for Trump's broader peace-deal record and Gaza plan progress, doubled as his most direct public accounting yet of how he views the Iran war's trajectory seven months in.

Trump followed the address with his planned meeting with leaders from the six Gulf Cooperation Council states — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Oman — on the war's next phase and a possible postwar strategy. The session produced little visible optimism: Qatar's foreign ministry spokesman, serving as a mediator between Washington and Tehran, publicly cast doubt on the prospects for any near-term deal. Gulf officials are said to be caught between two competing fears — further military escalation that could draw retaliation onto their own territory, and a diminished U.S. security commitment if Washington's attention shifts elsewhere.

The economic pressure campaign continues on schedule regardless of the diplomatic mood: Treasury officials confirmed Wednesday's global shutdown of Iranian airlines remains on track, part of what the administration is calling its "Operation Economic Outcast" campaign. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian is set to deliver his own address to the Assembly Wednesday, giving Tehran a formal opportunity to respond to Trump's characterization before the same body, even as the two governments remain without any confirmed direct meeting.

Markets largely tuned out the UN theater. The Nasdaq notched another record close, rising 0.30% to 27,202.16 on continued AI-stock strength, while the Dow slipped 213.53 points (-0.41%) to 51,835.30 and the S&P 500 finished essentially flat near 7,761.59. Bitcoin extended its recent run past $86,000, even as gold slid further to around $4,308 amid persistent risk appetite. The 30-year mortgage rate held at 6.95%, with Freddie Mac's next reading due Thursday.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

GLP-1 Cardiovascular Benefits Can Fade Quickly After Treatment Stops

A large study examining GLP-1 medications such as Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound found that their associated cardiovascular benefits can disappear surprisingly quickly once patients discontinue treatment, raising questions about how these drugs should be prescribed for long-term heart-health management. Researchers say the findings suggest the cardiovascular protection observed in earlier trials may depend on continuous use rather than reflecting a lasting physiological change, with real implications for patients considering stopping the medications.
