Geopolitics · Markets · Wednesday Edition

Iran Fires Missiles at U.S. Forces, Shattering the Pause, as the Fed Holds Rates Steady for a Fifth Straight Meeting

The four-day lull that had briefly reshaped the mood around the Iran war ended abruptly overnight, when Iran launched a surprise ballistic missile attack on U.S. forces in the Middle East, piercing what had been the longest stretch without new hostilities since the campaign began in early July. Jordan's Petra news agency said the country's air defenses intercepted five missiles fired from Iran early Wednesday, and U.S. Central Command confirmed all ballistic missiles aimed at American troops were shot down. President Trump did not mince words in response, telling Fox News "we'll be hitting them hard. They're going to get a beating," and vowing a heavy retaliatory strike. Hours later, U.S. and Saudi forces struck what CENTCOM described as multiple Iran-backed militia logistics and weapons sites in eastern Iraq, killing more than 20 fighters from the Iran-aligned Popular Mobilization Forces and four members of Iran's Revolutionary Guards.

The timing could hardly have been more pointed: the attack landed less than 24 hours after Prime Minister Netanyahu's first face-to-face meeting with Trump since the war began, and just as Iran's own Foreign Ministry had spent Tuesday insisting no negotiations were underway and that Tehran would "never allow the United States to determine the timing of war and peace." Whatever the pause had represented over its four days—genuine de-escalation, tactical patience, or simply a lull awaiting the next provocation—Wednesday's missiles answered the question. Armstrong Economics's running caution through this stretch, that a pause is not a peace, held up.

Markets absorbed the shock alongside the day's other major event: the Federal Reserve's rate decision, delivered as scheduled at 2 p.m. ET. The committee held its benchmark rate steady at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting, Chair Kevin Warsh's first policy decision at the helm, with no Summary of Economic Projections released and reportedly some dissents from officials who wanted to at least debate a hike. Warsh's 2:30 p.m. press conference offered limited forward guidance, consistent with his stated preference for letting decisions speak for themselves rather than pre-committing the committee's hand.

Equities closed lower across the board: the Dow fell roughly 0.7% to around 52,242, the S&P 500 slipped about 0.2% to near 7,374, and the Nasdaq eased a similar 0.2% to about 24,544. Oil told the more volatile story of the day, spiking to an intraday high of $82.42 on the missile attack before reversing hard as traders who had built positions on renewed-conflict expectations rushed for the exits, ultimately settling near $79.40—below Tuesday's $82.61 close. Gold, more conventionally, caught a safe-haven bid to $4,043. Bitcoin, oddly, kept climbing through it all, touching roughly $64,350 by mid-morning in a reminder that crypto has spent this stretch of the war trading on its own logic rather than the headlines.

The Retaliation

U.S. and Saudi Forces Strike Iran-Backed Militia Sites in Eastern Iraq

CENTCOM said the strikes hit multiple logistics and weapons sites belonging to Iran-aligned Popular Mobilization Forces units, killing more than 20 fighters, with four members of Iran's Revolutionary Guards also reported killed—the U.S. response to Wednesday's missile attack, though Trump has separately promised a "heavy" strike still to come.

Oil's Wild Ride

WTI Spikes to $82.42, Then Reverses to ~$79.40 as Conflict Longs Bail

Crude's initial jump on the missile attack proved short-lived: traders who had built positions on an escalating-conflict narrative moved swiftly to the exits once it became clear the response was contained to Iraq, sending WTI from a day high of $82.42 to an intraday low of $78.29 before settling near $79.40, below Tuesday's close.

Fed Decision

A Fifth Straight Hold, But With Dissents in the Room

The Fed held its benchmark rate at 3.50–3.75% in Kevin Warsh's first meeting as chair, skipping a Summary of Economic Projections and offering little forward guidance at the 2:30 p.m. press conference—though reports of dissents from officials open to a hike suggest less committee unity than the headline decision implies.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

Decision Day

Warsh's First Meeting as Chair Delivers a Hold, Not Much Else

The Federal Open Market Committee held its benchmark rate steady at 3.50–3.75% Wednesday, the fifth consecutive meeting without a change and the first policy decision of Kevin Warsh's chairmanship. No Summary of Economic Projections accompanied the release, and Warsh's 2:30 p.m. press conference stuck closely to his promise of reduced forward guidance, giving markets little to trade on beyond the decision itself. Reports of dissents from officials who wanted to at least debate a hike suggest the committee's unity is thinner than the unanimous-seeming hold suggests, with several officials continuing to flag inflation running near 3.7%. The renewed Iran missile attack, landing hours before the announcement, complicated an already difficult call; markets are now watching whether a September meeting brings the more contentious debate this one avoided.

Word of the Day
