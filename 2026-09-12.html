Geopolitics · Energy · Saturday Edition

Houthis Seize Control of Yemen's Red Sea Coast as a Drone Strike Shuts Down Saudi Arabia's Key Oil Pipeline

The war's geography expanded dramatically this week as Iran-backed Houthi rebels completed a rapid seizure of Yemen's Red Sea coastline, capturing the historic port of Mocha earlier in the week before taking the strategic island of Perim on Friday, right at the mouth of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. Houthi forces are now advancing on the coastal town of Dhubab as well. The strait, which handles roughly 12% of global seaborne oil trade and serves as a critical gateway between the Suez Canal and the Indian Ocean, had already become significantly more important since Hormuz tanker traffic slowed to a trickle — and now sits substantially under Houthi control on both its African and Arabian sides.

The timing compounded an already serious blow to regional energy infrastructure. Saudi Arabia shut down its 745-mile East-West pipeline after the conduit came under drone attack; both Riyadh and Baghdad say the drone originated in Iraq, where Iranian-backed militias operate. That pipeline had been the kingdom's primary workaround for exporting oil without relying on the Hormuz chokepoint, meaning Saudi Arabia's two main routes to global oil markets — the strait to its east and the pipeline running west across the peninsula — are now both compromised simultaneously, for the first time since the war began.

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman is now urging President Trump to take direct military action against the Houthis, a request that would mark a significant widening of direct U.S. involvement beyond the Iran-focused Hormuz campaign that has defined the war so far. Brent crude, unsurprisingly, has pushed toward $108 a barrel, its highest level since July, as traders price in the prospect of both the region's major chokepoints being contested at once.

Markets nonetheless closed out the week on a stronger note: Friday's session saw the Dow gain 509.19 points (+0.98%) to 52,573.29, the S&P 500 rise 0.86% to 7,656.98, and the Nasdaq add 0.96% to 26,333.04, as oil eased back from Thursday's spike and Thursday's CPI report came in close enough to expectations to calm some of the week's inflation anxiety. Bitcoin held roughly steady near $77,050 and gold traded sideways around $4,350 an ounce. Whether Friday's relief rally survives contact with the Bab el-Mandeb escalation is likely to be an early test for markets reopening Monday.

No More Workaround

Saudi Arabia's Backup Plan Is Now Also Down

The East-West pipeline existed specifically to let Saudi Arabia route around a Hormuz disruption; losing it to a drone strike at the same moment the Red Sea route falls under Houthi control leaves the kingdom with meaningfully fewer options than it had even a week ago.

A Request With Weight

A direct Saudi request for U.S. military action against the Houthis, if acted on, would formally extend America's role in this conflict well beyond the Iran-Hormuz theater that has defined it for the better part of a year.

Attribution Without Confirmation

The Iraq-Origin Claim Deserves the Same Scrutiny as Other War Claims

Riyadh and Baghdad's joint attribution of the drone strike to Iraq-based, Iran-backed militias is a serious allegation, but like many claims in this war, it arrives without independent, third-party verification of the drone's actual origin.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: A War That Spreads Geographically Rarely Contracts Again Quickly

Armstrong Economics has repeatedly warned that once a regional conflict expands to draw in new territory and new state actors — as this one now has, with Yemen, Saudi Arabia, and Iraq all directly implicated within days of each other — the odds of a swift negotiated resolution drop sharply. The site's War Cycle analysis treats geographic spread, more than any single battlefield event, as the clearest marker of a conflict settling into a prolonged, multi-year phase.

Random Fact

Perim Island, now seized by Houthi forces, has been a strategically fought-over piece of land for centuries, having hosted a British garrison in the 19th century specifically to monitor traffic through Bab el-Mandeb — a reminder that this stretch of water has been a contested chokepoint long before oil made it an economic one.

Word of the Day
chokepoint

(noun, from choke + point) — a narrow geographic passage through which trade or military traffic must pass, creating vulnerability to blockade or control. With Hormuz and now Bab el-Mandeb both contested, the term has moved from an abstract strategic-studies concept to this week's literal, day-to-day market driver.
