Geopolitics · Markets · Wednesday Edition

U.S. Navy Fires on Cargo Ship Enforcing Iran Blockade as 60-Day MoU Deadline Nears — July CPI Cools to 3.4%, Lifting Futures

A U.S. Navy MH-60 helicopter fired two Hellfire missiles at the engine room of the Panama-flagged cargo ship Vela Nova on Tuesday, disabling its steering after the vessel allegedly ignored repeated orders to stop while attempting to breach the American naval blockade of Iran. All 17 crew members were accounted for, and CENTCOM said the ship is no longer transiting toward Iran. It's the latest and most forceful example yet of the blockade's active enforcement: CENTCOM says U.S. forces have redirected 55 commercial vessels, disabled three, and boarded two since the blockade was reinstated in mid-July, after an earlier version was lifted following June's interim ceasefire.

The strike lands as a hard deadline approaches. The 60-day memorandum of understanding governing the current Oman-Iran shipping arrangement is set to expire Sunday, August 18, and Pakistan has stepped into an active mediating role, with Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi traveling to Tehran to try to keep the broader peace process from unraveling before then. Iran, for its part, has established what it calls a "Persian Gulf Strait Authority" and maintains that no vessel may transit Hormuz without a permit from that body — a direct challenge to the "100% control" Trump asserted Monday, and the exact kind of claim Tuesday's missile strike was arguably meant to answer.

On the economic side, Wednesday's July CPI report came in almost exactly as expected: headline inflation eased to 3.4% annually from June's 3.5%, with core CPI holding at 2.5% and shelter costs contributing about two-thirds of the modest 0.1% monthly increase. Futures rose on the print — Dow futures up 0.2%, S&P 500 futures up 0.4%, Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.8% — extending a tech-led rally, even as Tuesday's cash session closed lower across the board (S&P 500 −0.32% to 7,728.20, Dow −184 points to 53,791.85, Nasdaq −0.60% to 26,445.45) on renewed Middle East jitters and rising oil.

Armstrong Economics argues a cooling CPI print doesn't cool the underlying War Cycle: rate-cut mechanics and geopolitical escalation are running on separate tracks this week, with Tuesday's missile strike, the Houthis' first Red Sea fatalities in more than a year, and Sunday's looming MoU deadline all pointing toward a more volatile stretch regardless of how favorably the Fed reads today's inflation number.

The Print Everyone Was Waiting For

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Full-Fat Dairy May Not Deserve Its Bad Reputation, New Research Suggests

A 12-week study found that adults who ate three servings of full-fat dairy a day showed no meaningful increases in weight, body fat, cholesterol, or insulin resistance, adding to a growing body of research pushing back against decades of blanket low-fat dairy advice. Researchers caution the findings apply to overall dietary patterns rather than giving carte blanche for unlimited consumption, but the study is another data point suggesting the fat content of dairy itself may matter less than total diet quality.

Random Fact

The AGM-114 Hellfire missile used against the Vela Nova was originally developed in the 1970s and 80s as an anti-tank weapon fired from helicopters — its name is a backronym for "Helicopter Launched Fire and Forget Missile" — and has since become one of the U.S. military's most widely used precision weapons against a huge range of targets, land and maritime alike, exactly because a helicopter-launched missile can disable a ship's engine room without necessarily sinking it or endangering its crew.
