Geopolitics · Iran · Wednesday Edition

Trump's National Security Team Held Secret Camp David Talks on Iran as Attacks Surge and Bitcoin Crashes Below $84,000

Senior members of President Trump's national security team met for hours at Camp David last week to discuss next steps in the Iran war and the Saudi-Houthi conflict in Yemen, Axios reported Monday, citing three U.S. officials. Vice President JD Vance chaired the previously unannounced meeting, which included Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, White House envoy Steve Witkoff, CIA Director John Ratcliffe, Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. One official told Axios that decisions were made or at least "deeply discussed" regarding the Middle East crisis, as Trump weighs renewed major combat operations against Iran while Saudi Arabia separately presses him to join Houthi airstrikes that could mobilize more than 100,000 Yemeni troops.

The meeting's urgency is underscored by a sharp rise in tanker attacks: nearly 20 commercial ships have been hit in the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf, or off Oman over the past month, with nine attacks already logged this month alone, roughly half September's total. The attacks have intensified even as overall oil shipments through Hormuz approach prewar levels, a paradox that's kept Brent crude hovering near $100 a barrel: more oil is moving, but the operational risk and cost of moving it keeps prices elevated regardless.

That volatility spilled directly into crypto markets overnight. Bitcoin, trading near $85,500 as recently as Tuesday morning, crashed below $84,000 as a rally in oil prices tied to the renewed tanker attacks triggered a broad risk-off move, with roughly $547 million in leveraged positions liquidated in the selloff.

Equities, by contrast, closed Tuesday at fresh records: the S&P 500 gained 0.58% to 7,818.93, the Dow rose 0.49% to 51,521.28, and the Nasdaq added 0.45% to 27,599.79, with nuclear-sector stocks surging on broader energy-security themes. The rally came even as the 10-year Treasury yield pushed toward a fresh 24-year high near 5.3% amid a continuing global bond selloff, and gold slid to roughly $4,121, a two-month low, as a stronger dollar and surging yields overwhelmed any safe-haven demand. The 30-year mortgage rate held at 7.28% ahead of Thursday's official Freddie Mac reading.

Secrecy as Its Own Signal

An Unannounced Meeting Says More Than Any Announced One Would Have

Choosing not to disclose a national security meeting of this seniority is itself informative; routine coordination doesn't usually require concealment, which suggests the administration anticipated the contents would be read as more escalatory than it wanted broadcast.

A Blockade's Side Effects

Rising Attacks Alongside Rising Exports Is the Blockade Working Imperfectly, Not Failing

Iran stepping up attacks even as overall regional shipments recover suggests Tehran is trying to reassert leverage precisely because the broader trend is moving against it, using force to offset a negotiating position it can no longer sustain through economics alone.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Secret Strategy Sessions Late in a War Usually Precede Escalation, Not Resolution

Armstrong Economics has observed that unannounced, senior-level strategy meetings late in a prolonged conflict more often precede an expansion of military action than a negotiated exit, since a genuine off-ramp rarely requires the level of secrecy this meeting received. The site's War Cycle model reads the Camp David session, paired with Saudi pressure on a second front, as evidence the administration is actively weighing wider action rather than winding the conflict down.

Viral Infections Linked to 2.3 Million New Cancer Cases Worldwide in 2024

An analysis of global cancer incidence data found that roughly 2.3 million new cancer cases diagnosed in 2024 were attributable to viral or bacterial infections, with H. pylori, human papillomavirus, hepatitis B virus, and Epstein-Barr virus accounting for the largest shares. Researchers say the findings underscore how much of the global cancer burden remains preventable through existing vaccines, screening programs, and infection treatment, particularly in regions where access to those interventions remains limited.
