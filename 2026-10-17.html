Markets · Weekend Wrap · Saturday Edition

Wall Street Closes Its Worst Week Since June as Bitcoin Ticks Higher Into the Weekend

Markets are closed for the weekend, so Friday's numbers stand: the S&P 500 finished at 7,612.47 and the Nasdaq at 26,078.60, both logging their worst week since late June, while the Dow closed at 51,168.63 and still managed a 0.7% gain for the week despite Friday's dip. The split outcome reflects a market where a renewed AI-valuation selloff and Wednesday's hot inflation report did real damage to the broader index even as a handful of blue-chip names held up the Dow. Fed rate-hike odds for the October 27–28 meeting remain parked near a coin flip, a level of genuine uncertainty markets haven't faced in months heading into next week's data.

Bitcoin, which trades through the weekend, ticked up slightly to roughly $80,200 as the week's heaviest selling pressure eased with equity markets closed and trading volumes thinner. It's a modest reprieve rather than a reversal — the asset remains well off its recent highs and still subject to whatever direction next week's Fed commentary and inflation data push sentiment.

The Hormuz situation holds at an elevated plateau heading into the weekend: no single new incident emerged today, but oil remains near its highest levels in months after a week that saw roughly ten vessels attacked in and around the strait. With Reuters' report of Iranian officials privately admitting to a midterm-timed strategy still fresh, markets head into next week bracing for the possibility that this grinding, lower-intensity phase could escalate again with little warning.

The 30-year mortgage rate holds at 7.46% over the weekend, with Freddie Mac's next weekly reading due Thursday. In Chicago, Friday's rain and wind have given way to a bright, mostly sunny Saturday — a welcome break before next week's cooler pattern settles back in.
