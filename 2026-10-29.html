Federal Reserve · Markets · Thursday Edition

Fed Holds Rates in a 10-2 Vote as Warsh Warns the Inflation Fight Is "Unfinished," While the Brewers Capture Their First World Series Title

The Federal Reserve held its target rate at 3.75%–4.00% Wednesday, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting in favor of a 25-basis-point hike. Chair Kevin Warsh struck a notably hawkish tone for a hold, calling growth "strong" and inflation "elevated," and insisting the job of containing it remains "unfinished." Asked directly whether politics had shaped the decision just days before the midterms, he rejected the suggestion outright. "We will deliver price stability, no ifs, ands or buts, and that continues to be the case," he told reporters. Warsh offered little explicit guidance on December, but futures markets continue to price a high probability of a hike at the Fed's Dec. 8–9 meeting regardless.

Oil extended its gains on reports that the United States may renew strikes on Iran once the midterm elections have passed, with Brent settling at $115.32 a barrel and WTI at $102.78. Saudi Arabia still has not directly confirmed or denied Tuesday's Houthi claim of a strike on the Ras Tanura refinery; Aramco's statement on a "fully contained" export-terminal fire remains the closest thing to an official acknowledgment nearly 48 hours later.

Markets took the Fed's hold in stride: the S&P 500 closed at a record 7,743.98, the Nasdaq rose to 26,549.85, and the Dow added 97 points to 52,475.10. Bitcoin, which had popped briefly above $81,500 on the decision, gave back most of that move and now holds near $80,600. The 30-year mortgage ticked down to 7.34%, its lowest weekly reading in some time.

And in Tampa Bay, the biggest story of the night had nothing to do with the Fed: the Milwaukee Brewers beat the Rays 4-1 in Game 5, closing out the series 4-1 and winning the first World Series championship in the franchise's 57-year history. William Contreras was named Series MVP.

A hold was always the likely outcome; what moves markets now is a Fed Chair who called the inflation fight "unfinished" and faced down two dissenting hawks without softening his language — a combination that keeps December genuinely live.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Microplastics May Form Reactive, Cell-Damaging Molecules in the Body

Researchers publishing in Nature found that microplastics can react with water to form reactive chemical molecules capable of damaging cells — offering one of the first concrete mechanisms for how microplastic exposure might harm human tissue, rather than simply documenting that the particles accumulate in the body. Some commentators have cautioned that early coverage overstated the finding's immediate health implications, noting the study is focused more on identifying a possible pathway than establishing new real-world danger; researchers themselves describe it as an early but important step toward understanding microplastics' biological effects.
