Sixth Night of Strikes Brings Sirens to Bahrain and Kuwait as Trump Weighs Seizing Iran’s Main Oil Terminal — UnitedHealth’s Blowout Offsets a Chip Wobble

The war ground into its sixth straight day of American strikes overnight, with U.S. Central Command reporting it struck Iranian command centers, air defense sites, missile and drone capabilities, and coastal surveillance facilities to further degrade Tehran’s ability to threaten shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian state media reported explosions on Qeshm Island, in Bandar Abbas, and in Chabahar, while air raid sirens sounded early Thursday in both Bahrain and Kuwait. Iran’s army said at least seven military personnel were killed in strikes on a base in the country’s southeast, and Iran’s military warned that “all infrastructure in the region will be crushed under steel blows” if the U.S. follows through on threats to hit civilian sites. Separately, U.S. forces disabled an Iranian tanker headed for Kharg Island — Iran’s primary oil export terminal — and CNN reported Trump is weighing options to expand the military operation, including the possible seizure of Kharg itself. Amid the escalation, Trump said Thursday that Iran released an American woman who had been detained since December 2024, calling it a “gesture of goodwill”; Iran’s Foreign Ministry maintains it has no plans for negotiations and remains “focused on defending the country.”

Wall Street enters Thursday digesting a heavy earnings slate against that backdrop. UnitedHealth Group jumped more than 7% premarket after posting adjusted earnings of $6.38 per share on $112.03 billion in revenue — well above the $4.90 and $110.85 billion analysts expected — and raising full-year guidance to $19.50–$20.00 per share from a prior floor of $18.25. GE Aerospace beat as well, with $2.02 adjusted EPS on $12.63 billion in revenue against estimates of $1.86 and $11.86 billion, and raised its own full-year outlook, though shares fell 4% anyway on valuation. J.B. Hunt jumped nearly 7% on a beat. The chip trade told a rougher story: Taiwan Semiconductor topped estimates but fell more than 4% after raising 2026 capital spending guidance to $60–64 billion from a prior $52–56 billion range, spooking investors already nursing losses from Wednesday’s Micron slide. The pain spread overnight to Asia, where SK Hynix sank 11% in Seoul, Samsung dropped more than 7%, and Japan’s chip-equipment names each fell 4–7%.

The net effect: S&P 500 futures were down about 0.2–0.4% and Nasdaq 100 futures off roughly 0.8–1% in early trading, while Dow futures inched up 0.02% on UnitedHealth’s strength. Wednesday’s cash session had closed higher across the board — S&P 500 up 0.38% to 7,572.40, Nasdaq up 0.62% to 26,269.23, Dow up 0.29% to 52,658.64 — as investors rotated out of chips and into Big Tech names including Apple, which hit a fresh all-time high, Alphabet, Amazon, and Microsoft, all up 3% or more. The VIX ticked up to 16.35 as oil-driven jitters offset the inflation relief from Tuesday’s CPI and Wednesday’s PPI reports. Overnight, the Bank of Korea raised its policy rate 25 basis points to 2.75% — its first hike since January 2023 — citing rising inflation, a reminder that the disinflation story playing out in the U.S. isn’t universal.

Oil remains the connective tissue between the war and the market’s mood. WTI traded near $79.70 and Brent near $84.60 Thursday morning, both close to one-month highs and up roughly a third of the way back toward their second-quarter peak after the ceasefire’s collapse. EIA data released Wednesday showed U.S. crude inventories fell 1.7 million barrels last week. With Fed Chair Kevin Warsh having just wrapped two days of testimony in which he called particular price shocks “imperfect measures” of underlying inflation but stopped well short of ruling out a hike, the oil market is once again doing double duty — pricing war risk and shaping the Fed’s next move at the same time.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

Warsh Wraps Testimony

Two Days, Few Missteps: Warsh Questions the Inflation Gauges Themselves as Credibility Test Looms

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh closed out back-to-back days of House and Senate testimony Wednesday having avoided major slip-ups, even as lawmakers in both parties pressed him on why prices remain elevated more than five years into the Fed’s fight against inflation. Warsh cast doubt on the reliability of traditional gauges themselves — “my view is these are all imperfect measures of the state of underlying inflation,” he said — and drew a distinction between the Iran war’s gas-price shock and inflation the Fed can actually influence: “particular price shocks happen to particular prices that we don’t have control over. But I don’t want to suggest we don’t have control over inflation in the medium term. That’s our job.” Pressed repeatedly on his independence from the White House, Warsh told Senate Banking Committee members “my commitment to you is to follow the law and follow the data. Follow our very best judgment,” and separately told Sen. Chris Van Hollen he has not discussed rate policy with Trump. Markets now overwhelmingly price a possible hike by year-end; the risk analysts flag is that if Warsh loses an internal debate over how much AI-driven data-center investment is inflationary, and rates rise anyway, or if he wins that argument and inflation reaccelerates regardless, his credibility takes the hit either way. The next FOMC decision lands July 28–29.

Tax & Accounting

IRS Policy Change

IRS Rolls Out Automatic Penalty Relief (AEP), Retiring the Decades-Old First-Time Abate Request Process

The IRS this month began phasing in a new Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP) program that will, for the first time, apply failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, and failure-to-deposit penalty relief automatically during return processing rather than requiring compliant taxpayers to request it. AEP replaces First Time Abate (FTA), the administrative waiver that has existed since 2001 but depended on taxpayers or their preparers knowing it existed and asking for it. Eligibility mirrors FTA’s: a clean three-year filing and payment history (or 12 consecutive quarters for quarterly filers), covering Forms 1040, 1065, 1120, and the core employment-tax series. IRS CEO Frank Bisignano said the change “reflects the IRS’ commitment to making the payment of taxes owed simpler and more consistent.” The rollout applies to 2025 annual returns and 2026 quarterly returns now, with AEP fully replacing FTA for returns due on or after January 1, 2027; during the transition, some qualifying taxpayers may still receive a penalty notice and need to request FTA manually. National Taxpayer Advocate Erin Collins, who first floated the change publicly at last year’s AICPA conference, called it “a long-awaited taxpayer win,” while urging the IRS to consider applying it retroactively to eligible 2026 penalties assessed before the system went live. Practitioners should flag AEP notices for clients now rather than assuming automatic relief has already kicked in for every eligible return.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Heat and Bad Air Don’t Just Add Up — They Compound Each Other, and Today Is a Textbook Case

Today’s Air Pollution Action Day lands on top of a fourth straight day near or above 90°, and research on the interaction between heat and ozone suggests that combination is worse than either factor alone. Rising temperatures accelerate the chemical reactions that form ground-level ozone, while heat itself independently strains the cardiovascular system — and several population studies have found the two effects compound rather than simply add: one large U.S. analysis of 95 metro areas found that a same-day temperature spike was associated with meaningfully higher cardiovascular mortality on high-ozone days than on cleaner ones. The people most at risk are the same groups the Illinois EPA and Chicago Park District are flagging today — older adults, children, and anyone with existing heart or lung conditions. The practical takeaway on a red-AQI day like this one isn’t just “stay hydrated” or “limit exertion” individually; it’s to treat both together, moving strenuous outdoor activity indoors or to the cooler morning hours rather than assuming a shaded, low-effort afternoon outside is safe simply because it doesn’t feel like a heat emergency.

Random Fact

Kharg Island, the terminal Trump is reportedly weighing whether to seize, handles roughly 90% of Iran’s crude oil exports despite covering barely nine square kilometers of the Persian Gulf. It has been a strategic target before: Iraqi forces struck the island repeatedly during the Iran-Iraq War of the 1980s, temporarily crippling Iran’s ability to export oil and contributing to the era’s so-called “Tanker War,” the last major precedent for the kind of shipping conflict now playing out again in the Strait of Hormuz four decades later.

Word of the Day
chokepoint

(noun, strategic geography) — a narrow geographic passage, such as a strait or canal, whose control confers outsized economic or military leverage because it cannot easily be bypassed. The Strait of Hormuz is the world’s best-known chokepoint; today’s reporting that Iran has asked the Houthis to stand ready to close the Bab el-Mandeb Strait as well raises the possibility of a second one opening at the same time, which is precisely what keeps oil traders up at night.
