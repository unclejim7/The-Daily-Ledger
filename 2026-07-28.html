Markets · Geopolitics · Tuesday Edition

Netanyahu and Trump Meet for the First Time Since the Iran War Began as a $250 Billion Nvidia-OpenAI Deal Triggers a Global Chip Rout

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu arrived at the White House Tuesday for his first face-to-face meeting with President Trump since the two launched their joint war on Iran in February, a sit-down both leaders' aides are framing as a chance to smooth over months of accumulated strain. Netanyahu said he intends to discuss "all the issues on the agenda, first and foremost, Iran," but the visit comes against an awkward backdrop: the easy alliance the two touted at the war's outset has given way to reported friction, with Trump privately venting frustration at his most vocal international backer even as Netanyahu, facing his own reelection fight at home, needs the relationship to look stronger than it currently does. Trump, for his part, is under real pressure to show progress toward ending a war that has grown unpopular and expensive ahead of November's midterms.

That pressure collided directly with Iran's own messaging Tuesday. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei flatly denied President Trump's Monday claim that Tehran had requested new talks, telling reporters Iran "will never allow the United States to determine the timing of war and peace" and giving no indication Iran is prepared to negotiate directly or offer new concessions. The denial doesn't reverse the underlying pause in strikes, now well into its fourth day, but it does undercut the more optimistic framing coming out of Washington—even as separate, lower-profile talks between Iran and Oman over reopening the Strait of Hormuz continue in parallel.

Markets, though, spent Tuesday reacting to a very different story. Nvidia shares fell roughly 5% after CNBC and the Wall Street Journal reported the company is in talks to backstop up to $250 billion in OpenAI data-center financing—letting OpenAI borrow against Nvidia's credit for a campus in Pike County, Ohio—on top of separate talks covering as much as $350 billion in future chip purchases. The scale of the arrangement revived concerns that the entire AI boom now rests on a small circle of companies financing each other's growth; investor Michael Burry summarized the reaction bluntly as "around and around we go." The selloff hit hardest in Asia, where Samsung Electronics closed down 13.4% and SK Hynix fell 14.7%, dragging South Korea's Kospi index down 10.8% in its worst single session since the early days of the Iran conflict in March, since the two chipmakers together account for nearly half the benchmark. Nvidia's slide let Apple reclaim the title of world's most valuable company at roughly $4.95 trillion, edging back ahead of Nvidia's roughly $4.8 trillion.

U.S. indexes closed more calmly by comparison: the Dow gained 0.77% to 52,609.61 on strength outside tech, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.33% to 7,388.38 and the Nasdaq dropped 1.36% to 24,593.43 as chip weakness spread stateside. All of it sets up a consequential 24 hours: the Fed's two-day meeting opened Tuesday, with Wednesday's 2 p.m. decision and Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference now the week's central event, CME FedWatch still pricing a 65.7% chance of a fifth straight hold. Gold slipped toward $4,030 and oil steadied near $84 after Monday's nearly 9% plunge, both markets still digesting whether the Iran pause and the AI financing jitters pull policy in the same direction or different ones.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Extending the Overnight Fast by Two Hours — Without Cutting Calories — May Help the Heart

New research finds that middle-aged and older adults at elevated risk for cardiometabolic disease saw measurable heart and metabolic benefits simply from extending their overnight fasting window by roughly two hours—timing the fast to better match their natural sleep-wake cycle rather than eating less overall. The findings add a timing-based angle to the broader intermittent-fasting research, arriving alongside a separate study showing that losing even 80 minutes of nightly sleep for six weeks was enough to cause weight gain and reduced activity—a useful pair of reminders for anyone whose sleep got disrupted by Monday night's storms.

Random Fact

The $250 billion Nvidia is reportedly backstopping for OpenAI's Ohio data-center buildout is larger than the entire annual GDP of New Zealand, and comparable to Finland's—a single corporate financing arrangement now measured on the same scale as a mid-sized country's economy, which is part of why Tuesday's news rattled markets as far away as Seoul.

Word of the Day
backstop

(noun/verb, from baseball, the catcher's role stopping balls that get past the plate) — in finance, a guarantee or standing commitment that absorbs risk a lender or investor would otherwise have to bear alone, allowing borrowing to proceed on the strength of the guarantor's credit rather than the borrower's own. Nvidia's reported willingness to backstop OpenAI's data-center debt is precisely this: a bet that the chipmaker's balance sheet, not OpenAI's own financials, is what ultimately gets tested if the buildout doesn't pay off.
