Geopolitics · Iran · Sunday Edition

Iran Weighs a Nuclear Policy Shift as Ghalibaf Insists Tehran Must "Fight and Negotiate" at Once, Two Days Before Trump's UN Address

Iran's parliament speaker, Mohammad-Bagher Ghalibaf, said Sunday that Tehran should reject the idea that war and negotiation are mutually exclusive, arguing the Islamic Republic must pursue both simultaneously rather than choosing one path. "Any rational action from a revolutionary position, any strong negotiation, or any effort to secure the rights of the people from the enemy must not be wrongly defined as compromise and passivity," he said, framing diplomacy as compatible with continued resistance rather than a concession to it.

More striking was a parallel discussion among Iranian lawmakers over whether the country's nuclear posture itself might need to change. Tehran representative Kamran Ghazanfari said that "if conditions change in such a way that we feel the existence of the Islamic Republic is seriously threatened, the leadership could take a position on nuclear weapons," raising the prospect of a shift away from decades of official doctrine. Iran's top security official, Mohsen Rezaei, pushed back only partially, insisting the late supreme leader's fatwa against nuclear weapons remains intact and undoctrine has not changed — while still adding, pointedly, "we do not know what will happen in the future."

The comments land just two days before a consequential stretch of diplomacy in New York. President Trump is set to address the UN General Assembly Tuesday, where officials say he'll stress that Iran must never be allowed a nuclear weapon, before meeting separately with six Gulf leaders on the war's next phase. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian is separately traveling to New York to address the Assembly Wednesday, part of a smaller, tightly controlled Iranian delegation the U.S. has agreed to admit despite the two countries remaining formally at war — a rare, narrow channel of engagement running alongside the broader hostilities.

U.S. equity markets are closed for the weekend, leaving Friday's mixed close to stand (S&P 500 at 7,650.50). Bitcoin, however, pushed back above $80,000 for the first time in over a week, extending its bounce off Wednesday's lows, while gold held near $4,377 after posting its first weekly gain in a month. The 30-year mortgage rate remains at 6.95%, with Freddie Mac's next weekly reading not due until Thursday.

A Careful Rhetorical Balance

Ghalibaf's Formula Lets Iran Negotiate Without Looking Weak

Framing talks as compatible with continued resistance, rather than a retreat from it, gives Iran's leadership political cover to pursue diplomacy at the Gulf summit and UN sidelines without appearing to capitulate to domestic hardliners.

A Deliberately Ambiguous Signal

Floating a Nuclear Shift Is a Message, Not Necessarily a Plan

Iranian officials raising and then only partially walking back the prospect of abandoning the anti-nuclear-weapons fatwa functions as a pressure tactic aimed at Tuesday's diplomacy as much as a genuine change in doctrine — ambiguity itself can be the point.

Diplomacy Alongside War

Two Presidents, Two UN Speeches, Zero Direct Contact Planned

Trump and Pezeshkian addressing the same body within a day of each other, with no confirmed meeting between them, captures this war's persistent pattern: parallel messaging to the same audience, without the two governments actually speaking to one another.

A Quiet Weekend Bounce

Bitcoin's Move Past $80K Comes as Equities Sit Idle

A Non-Trivial Threshold

Even Discussing NPT Withdrawal Changes the Conversation

Whether or not Iran ever acts on it, the mere public airing of nuclear-doctrine change by sitting lawmakers shifts the diplomatic baseline heading into Tuesday's summit, giving negotiators on all sides a new, harder-edged data point to plan around.

A Narrow, Controlled Channel

Granting Iran's Delegation Visas Is Itself Notable

Admitting a smaller, closely monitored Iranian delegation to the UN while the two countries remain formally at war signals that even minimal, tightly bounded diplomatic contact is still considered worth preserving by both sides.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Nuclear Rhetoric Escalation Often Precedes, Not Replaces, Negotiation

Armstrong Economics has long argued that public escalation in rhetoric — including floating previously taboo options like nuclear-doctrine shifts — frequently serves as a negotiating tactic meant to strengthen a party's position just before real talks, rather than a genuine step toward using such capabilities. The site's War Cycle model reads Iran's dual signals this weekend, hard-line nuclear talk paired with Ghalibaf's openness to negotiation, as consistent with a regime trying to enter Tuesday's diplomacy from a position of perceived strength rather than one preparing for further war.

Semaglutide Linked to Nearly 40% Fewer Asthma Attacks and 20% Fewer COPD Flare-Ups

A large real-world study found that semaglutide, the active ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy, was associated with nearly 40% fewer asthma attacks and roughly 20% fewer COPD exacerbations among patients using the medication, adding respiratory benefits to the drug's already expanding list of documented effects beyond diabetes and weight management. Researchers say the findings support growing interest in GLP-1 medications as a broader metabolic and inflammatory intervention rather than a single-purpose treatment.
