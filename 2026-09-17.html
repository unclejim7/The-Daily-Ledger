Geopolitics · Diplomacy · Thursday Edition

Trump Says a "Big Decision" Looms on Whether to "Annihilate" Iran, Ahead of a Tuesday Meeting With Six Gulf Leaders

President Trump escalated his public rhetoric on the war sharply Thursday, telling Axios he has a "big decision" coming on whether to "go in and annihilate" the Iranian regime. "Anything could happen with me," he added, a marked departure from his more measured recent predictions that the conflict would wind down around the midterms. The comments arrive just days before a planned Tuesday meeting with leaders from six Gulf nations — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Oman — on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York, a session that could determine whether the war's next phase leans toward renewed diplomacy or a return to major combat operations.

The stakes of that meeting were underscored Wednesday when UN Secretary-General António Guterres repeated his calls for de-escalation and the restoration of freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz. Some U.S. officials, speaking anonymously, describe the conflict as having settled into an unsustainable "neither-war-nor-peace" stalemate, with one outside expert warning the war could drag on "until late 2028, if not beyond" absent a genuine breakthrough. Trump's own repeated recent statements that the war will end soon sit uneasily next to both that assessment and his own newly sharpened threat.

The Houthis, for their part, show no sign of de-escalating on their own front: the group has vowed to target any Saudi-flagged oil tanker transiting the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and claims to have struck at least six such vessels since July 22, a running tally that predates but continues alongside this week's claimed strikes on Saudi Aramco's Yanbu facility and the Khamis Mushait air base.

Markets, meanwhile, are still digesting Wednesday's Fed decision. The Dow fell 631.21 points (-1.2%) to 51,461.90, the S&P 500 dropped 0.4% to 7,551.81, and the Nasdaq finished nearly flat at 25,978.42, with stocks turning decisively lower during Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference as he repeatedly stressed that inflation remains too high even as the economy strengthens. Gold, notably, surged 2.5% to $4,368.91 the same day rates rose — an unusual pairing that suggests investors are treating persistent inflation, not the rate path itself, as the dominant story. Bitcoin slipped below $76,000, and Freddie Mac's weekly survey showed the 30-year mortgage rate jumping to 6.95%, a fourth consecutive weekly increase and the highest level since January 2025, now within striking distance of 7%.

Tuesday's Gulf Summit Carries Real Weight

Six regional leaders in the same room as Trump, days after his most extreme rhetoric yet, sets up a genuinely consequential meeting — the kind that could either produce a diplomatic breakthrough or harden positions further, depending on what each side brings to the table.

A Sobering Outside Assessment

2028 Is a Long Way From "Immediately"

An expert's warning that the war could persist beyond 2028 stands in stark contrast to Trump's own repeated predictions of a near-term resolution — a gap this wide between insider rhetoric and outside analysis is itself a notable data point.

Inflation, Not Rates, Driving Gold

A Rate Hike That Didn't Hurt the Safe Haven

A Running Tally That Keeps Growing

Six Tankers Since July Is Its Own Kind of Escalation

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Summits Convened Under Threat Rarely Produce Clean Outcomes

Armstrong Economics has argued that diplomatic gatherings held in the shadow of an explicit military threat tend to produce ambiguous, face-saving outcomes rather than genuine breakthroughs, since no party wants to appear to have negotiated under duress. Tuesday's Gulf summit, convened days after Trump's "annihilate" comment, fits that pattern closely — the site's War Cycle analysis treats this kind of threat-shadowed diplomacy as more likely to buy time than to end a conflict outright.
