Election Day · Markets · Tuesday Edition

Polls Open Across the Country as Control of Congress Hangs in the Balance

Voters head to the polls today with control of both chambers of Congress, 36 governorships, and a wide slate of state races on the line. Early and mail voting has run well ahead of the last two midterm cycles, and pre-election polling has shown a notably wider enthusiasm gap in favor of Democratic voters than Republican ones — though several marquee Senate races, including Iowa's open seat, remain genuine toss-ups heading into tonight's returns. Results won't begin arriving in earnest until polls close this evening; this morning's edition goes to press with the outcome still entirely in voters' hands.

Markets drifted modestly higher on Election Day itself, a pattern consistent with how equities have typically behaved on the day of a vote regardless of the expected outcome, with any larger moves historically arriving only once results are known. The S&P 500 rose 0.2% to 7,625.09, the Dow added 0.2% to 52,081.60, and the Nasdaq gained 0.3% to 25,641.31.

Brent crude held near $117 a barrel, with Gulf war-risk insurance costs remaining well above pre-conflict norms even without a fresh overnight incident. Saudi Arabia has now gone eight days without confirming or denying the Houthi claim of a strike on the Ras Tanura refinery. Bitcoin extended its recent climb to roughly $78,200, and the 30-year mortgage holds at 7.34% ahead of Thursday's new reading.

On a lighter note, the Bears delivered the night's most decisive result of any kind, routing the Seahawks 34-17 on Monday Night Football to improve to 6-2.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Domestic Votes Rarely Move a War's Underlying Economics

Armstrong Economics has long argued that insurance markets, not political rhetoric, are the most honest real-time gauge of how seriously professional risk-takers assess an ongoing conflict. The site's War Cycle model notes that today's elevated Gulf insurance costs, holding steady through an election cycle that has largely ignored the conflict, suggest the market's own assessment of risk hasn't shifted regardless of what's dominating the news cycle at home.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Higher Ultra-Processed Food Intake Linked to Worse Survival Among Cancer Survivors

A study of more than 800 cancer survivors, published in Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention, found that higher ultra-processed food intake was associated with higher all-cause and cancer-specific mortality. Researchers say inflammation and resting heart rate may partly explain the association, though the study cannot establish that ultra-processed food directly causes worse outcomes; a separate large UK cohort study similarly linked higher ultra-processed food consumption to higher overall cancer incidence and death, with the strongest association found for ovarian cancer.

Random Fact

Congress fixed Election Day as "the Tuesday after the first Monday in November" in 1845, specifically to avoid conflicts with market days and the Sabbath in a largely agrarian, horse-and-buggy America — a scheduling quirk that has outlasted the world that created it by nearly two centuries.

Word of the Day
plurality

(noun, from Latin pluralitas "a greater number") — the largest number of votes received by a candidate in a race with more than two options, not necessarily a majority. In several of tonight's tightest races, the winner may take office with a plurality rather than an outright majority of the vote.
