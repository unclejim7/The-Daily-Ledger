Markets · Geopolitics · Friday Edition

US Diesel Hits an All-Time Record $5.85 a Gallon as a Blowout August Jobs Report Reverses the Week's Dovish Fed Turn

Six months of war finally produced the kind of number that shows up directly on American household and business bills: diesel hit an average of $5.85 a gallon nationally Friday, an all-time high that surpasses the previous record set in June 2022 during the early shock of the Ukraine war and Russia sanctions. Brent crude, the international benchmark, is trading above $95 a barrel, up from roughly $70 before the war began, as renewed fighting around the Strait of Hormuz keeps squeezing the same crude supply that refineries depend on for diesel and jet fuel. Because diesel underpins freight and delivery networks, the higher cost is already showing up as added fees on shipped goods and online orders — a second-order effect of this war that's proven considerably harder for most Americans to ignore than any single headline about tanker strikes.

Iran's own economy, meanwhile, is absorbing damage of a different, arguably more severe kind. Iranian officials say the country's exports and imports have fallen roughly 35% since the war began, inflation is approaching 70%, and oil exports have collapsed from around 1.7 million barrels a day before the conflict to roughly 260,000 barrels now — a nearly 85% reduction that dwarfs, in percentage terms, anything the U.S. side of this war has experienced economically. Both governments, in other words, are absorbing real economic pain from a conflict that shows no sign of a negotiated resolution, just in very different forms and at very different scales.

The Fed story, for its part, whipsawed again. Friday's official August jobs report showed the economy added 162,000 jobs, decisively beating the 55,000 consensus estimate, with unemployment holding steady at 4.1%. Just two days after Wednesday's weak ADP print flipped odds toward a rate cut, Friday's stronger official number swung the pendulum right back: fed funds futures traders are now pricing a 58% chance of a September hike, and the 2-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since January 2025. Stocks were mixed on the news — the Dow down 226 points, the S&P off 0.2%, the Nasdaq up slightly — a modest pullback after Thursday's best session in a month (S&P +1.06% to 7,747.71, Dow +624 points to 53,686.11, Nasdaq +1.4% to 26,584.06), when Waller's dovish comments had pulled yields sharply lower.

Bitcoin, notably, didn't wait around for Friday's data to make up its mind: BTC pushed above $81,000 overnight on what traders describe as massive spot ETF inflows, a fresh high for this month's rally regardless of which way the Fed's rate decision ultimately breaks. Gold held closer to $4,470 an ounce, largely unmoved either direction as the jobs report re-injected some uncertainty into a rate picture that had, for about 48 hours, looked settled.

An 85% collapse in oil exports and inflation nearing 70% represent an entirely different order of economic damage than a record but still-manageable diesel price — a reminder that however painful this war is for American consumers, it's an existential-level shock for Iran's own economy.

Random Fact

Diesel engines power roughly three-quarters of America's freight trucks despite representing a minority of vehicles on the road overall, meaning a spike in diesel prices specifically, rather than gasoline, translates almost directly into higher costs for nearly everything shipped by truck.

Word of the Day
whipsaw

(verb, from the two-handled saw used by two people pulling in opposite directions) — to subject to two opposing forces or pressures in quick succession. Markets being whipsawed from rate-cut to rate-hike expectations within 48 hours captures the term almost literally — pulled hard in one direction, then immediately the other.
