Markets · Geopolitics · Thursday Edition

Nvidia's Blowout Quarter Sends Stock Futures Soaring as Trump Insists Hormuz Mines Are Already Gone, Complicating Iran and Oman's New Corridor Plan

Nvidia capped Wednesday's close with a genuinely blockbuster second-quarter report: revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year over year, and earnings per share of $2.22, up more than 111%, while guiding to $108 billion in third-quarter revenue — growth of nearly 90%. The stock jumped as much as 7.63% in after-hours trading and was still up roughly 5% in Thursday's premarket, dragging Nasdaq-100 futures and European tech stocks (the Stoxx Europe Tech index +1.8%) higher along with it, easing weeks of quiet worry that the AI infrastructure buildout was finally running out of room to surprise.

The euphoria landed the same morning Trump moved to undercut the premise of this week's Iran-Oman diplomatic breakthrough. Where Tehran and Muscat had outlined a joint project to clear mines from the Strait of Hormuz as part of a proposed temporary shipping corridor, Trump insisted Wednesday that all mines in the strait have already been removed or detonated, and warned that any vessel attempting to lay new ones would be destroyed. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps spokesperson Hossein Mohebbi pushed back hard, saying the U.S. is "obstructing this process" and that the critical waterway will stay closed if Washington doesn't accept the corridor proposal's terms as negotiated.

The dispute leaves the corridor plan in a strange limbo: Iran and Oman have a framework, but implementing the mine-clearance half of it requires acknowledging a problem the U.S. now publicly denies exists. It's the same dynamic that's defined this war for months, where facts on the ground — or, in this case, facts about the ground beneath the water — remain contested even when the diplomatic language sounds like progress.

Markets closed Wednesday modestly higher ahead of Nvidia's report (S&P 500 +0.2% to 7,692.15, Dow +0.1% to 53,631.80, Nasdaq +0.3% to 26,231.50), setting up Thursday's earnings-driven rally on top of an already constructive week. Bitcoin held near $79,300, gold ticked up to about $4,700 an ounce, and oil continued sliding — Brent down roughly 1% to $86.93, WTI off 2.5% to $80.28 — as the corridor talks, contested as they are, still put some downward pressure on the war's crude-supply premium. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote is now just a day away.

Armstrong Economics has argued that markets, not official statements, ultimately determine a waterway's real status; a dispute over whether mines even exist adds a new wrinkle to that framework, since it's no longer just a question of enforcement or access, but of basic factual reality on the ground.
