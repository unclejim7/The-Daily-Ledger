Bitcoin · Thanksgiving · Thursday Edition

Bitcoin Bounces Off the Brink as Markets Close for Thanksgiving, Bears Head to Detroit

Bitcoin's slide reversed course Thanksgiving morning, with BTC climbing back to roughly $60,730 — a gain of better than 4% from Wednesday's close, which had come within $46 of this publication's all-time tracked low. It's the first meaningful bounce since the correction reasserted itself earlier this week, pulling BTC back above $60,000 and to roughly 26% off last Friday's peak near $82,450, an improvement from Wednesday's 29% drawdown. The record low of $58,278 remains close enough to stay the dominant technical reference point.

With U.S. stock, bond, and commodity markets closed entirely for Thanksgiving, Wednesday's closing levels stand across the board: the S&P 500 at 7,630.17, the Dow at 52,195.91, the Nasdaq at 25,772.43, and Brent crude holding near $118 through a nineteenth straight quiet week in the Gulf. Markets reopen Friday for a shortened session.

Chicago's Thanksgiving Day trip to Detroit is the day's other marquee event — kickoff at Ford Field is set for 1 p.m. ET (noon Chicago time) on CBS, with the Bears (8-2) looking to end a four-game losing streak in the rivalry and settle real NFC North seeding implications. Chicago itself turned colder and blustery for the holiday, with a high near 39°F.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Review: Fiber Shows the Most Consistent Link to Longevity Across Every Age Group Studied

A new literature review published this month pulled together existing diet-and-aging research and found that, across every age bracket examined, higher fiber intake showed the most consistent association with longer life relative to other individual dietary factors. The review's authors identify adequate sleep, regular physical activity, and sufficient hydration as complementary contributors, but argue fiber stands out as the single most reproducible signal across the literature they examined. As a review of existing studies rather than a new clinical trial, the findings describe an association seen across the literature rather than new proof that increasing fiber intake alone would extend any individual's life, and the authors caution that the underlying studies varied in quality and population.
