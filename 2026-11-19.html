FOMC Minutes Reveal a Genuinely Split Committee, Leaving December's Decision Wide Open

Minutes from the Fed's late-October meeting, released Wednesday afternoon, showed a committee genuinely divided over its next move — several participants continued to favor raising rates to address persistently high inflation, while others argued the data didn't yet justify further tightening. Rather than clarifying December's path, as this publication's Wednesday edition noted it might, the minutes instead confirmed just how unresolved the debate inside Chair Kevin Warsh's Fed remains.

Markets took the uncertainty poorly, with the S&P 500 falling 0.5% to 7,646.11 and the Nasdaq leading losses on renewed rate anxiety layered atop the ongoing AI-stock selloff. Bitcoin eased further to roughly $63,150, extending its choppy, directionless stretch since the weekend's crash. Thursday's new Freddie Mac reading brought a genuine bright spot, with the 30-year mortgage ticking down to 7.24%, continuing a gradual multi-week decline.

Brent crude held steady near $118 through a twelfth straight quiet week in the Gulf. The Maple Leafs topped the Blackhawks 5-2 at the United Center, and Chicago saw its first measurable snowfall of the season overnight — right around the city's climatological average date — before clearing to a cold, sunny Thursday.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Four-Minute Daily Strength Routine Improves Mobility in Older Adults Within 12 Weeks

Penn State researchers tested a four-minute daily resistance routine — alternating 30 seconds of push-ups, chair stands, two-arm rows, and stair stepping with 30-second rest periods — in 97 sedentary adults aged 65 and older, with an average age of 74. Published in PLOS One, the twelve-week trial found participants improved by an average of 4.2 repetitions on a 30-second chair stand test, gained 3.6 seconds in one-legged standing balance, and got 2.3 seconds faster on a sit-to-stand speed test, with an 81% adherence rate to the brief daily routine. The researchers note the study did not directly measure whether the improvements translate into fewer falls, and the sample size was relatively small, but say the results suggest meaningful functional gains are possible with a minimal time commitment.
