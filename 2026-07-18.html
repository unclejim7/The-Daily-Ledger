Geopolitics · Markets · Weekend Edition

Iran War Fallout Deepens Into the Weekend as 10,000 Lose Water and Bahrain Intercepts Fresh Strikes — Wall Street Closes Its Worst Week in Months

The human toll from Friday night's seventh consecutive round of American strikes on Iran came into sharper focus Saturday morning. Iran's national water company said roughly 10,000 people across 20 villages in the country's south are without water after the overnight attacks, which U.S. Central Command said were aimed at "continuing to degrade Iranian military capabilities" and which struck six bridges near the port city of Bandar Abbas, a railway junction, and an airport further inland. Iran's Health Ministry says U.S. strikes since July 6 have now killed at least 50 people and wounded more than 500. Tehran's retaliation kept spreading geographically: Iran's army said it struck U.S.-linked assets in Bahrain, Jordan and Kuwait, where authorities reported a fire at a power and water desalination plant following the attacks. Bahrain's defense force said Saturday it intercepted and destroyed a fresh wave of Iranian aerial attacks, underscoring that the exchange of fire has not slowed heading into a second weekend of open conflict.

Wall Street, meanwhile, closed out its roughest week since the spring on a note that had comparatively little to do with the war. The S&P 500 fell 1.01% Friday to 7,457.69, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.40% to 25,520.24, and the Dow shed 406.55 points (-0.77%) to 52,146.42, capping a week in which the S&P lost roughly 1.6%, the Nasdaq slid 2.9%, and the Dow fell 0.9%. The real damage was concentrated in chips: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closed more than 20% below its June 22 record high, officially entering a bear market, as Chinese startup Moonshot AI's Friday release of its Kimi K3 model — which the company says rivals top U.S. offerings — reignited fears that years of assumed AI infrastructure spending may not be as durable as priced. Netflix fell for a second straight session after Thursday's weak Q3 revenue guide, and TSMC dropped 3.6% Friday despite posting a record profit quarter a day earlier, as its raised capex guidance continued to spook investors. The VIX jumped more than 12% to close near 18.8, its highest level in several weeks, even as a University of Michigan survey showed consumer sentiment improving to 54.4 in July — its best reading since the Iran war began in February.

Layered on top of the market and war headlines is a fresh political flashpoint that could complicate the fragile U.S.-China trade truce. In a Thursday night White House address, President Trump alleged that Beijing interfered in the 2020 election, citing declassified documents he said show China obtained 220 million American voter files in what he called the largest compromise of election data in history. The claim revives a dispute U.S. intelligence agencies had previously resolved differently: a 2021 assessment concluded China considered but ultimately did not deploy interference efforts intended to change the 2020 outcome, and specifically found no evidence Beijing tampered with vote-counting infrastructure. China's Foreign Ministry called the allegations "entirely fabricated" and said Beijing has "no interest" in interfering in U.S. elections. The dispute lands just months ahead of the November midterms and adds a new variable for markets already nervous about AI capex and Gulf escalation risk.

One pocket of relief: Bitcoin, which slid to $62,810 in Friday's risk-off selling, has clawed back above $64,000 over the weekend, changing hands near $64,100 Saturday morning. With no equity trading until Monday, the next real test for markets is whether the chip selloff finds a floor or whether the SOX's newly confirmed bear market becomes the dominant story of the week ahead — one that would compete for attention with an Iran war showing no sign of a genuine de-escalation.

Chip Sector

SOX Confirms a Bear Market, Down 20.2% From Its June 22 Record — Moonshot's Kimi K3 Adds a New Anxiety

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closed Friday more than 20% below its late-June closing high, the technical threshold for a bear market, after a week in which the index fell nearly 10% on its own. Chinese startup Moonshot AI's Friday release of Kimi K3, which the company describes as the world's largest open AI model and says rivals leading U.S. offerings, reignited concerns that cheaper, competitive models abroad could dent the case for the scale of U.S. AI infrastructure spending. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF posted its third weekly decline in four weeks. Chip names remain up sharply for the year even after the pullback, but the speed of the reversal has traders debating whether this is a healthy reset or the start of something longer.

Streaming & Chips

Netflix Slides a Second Day, TSMC Falls Despite Record Profit as Capex Guidance Overshadows Results

Netflix shares fell again Friday, extending Thursday's after-hours slide that followed a Q3 revenue guide of $12.86 billion against Street estimates near $13 billion. Taiwan Semiconductor, which posted a fifth consecutive quarter of record profit and topped Q3 guidance a day earlier, dropped 3.6% Friday as investors continued digesting a roughly 15% increase to 2026 capex guidance and a fresh $100 billion U.S. investment pledge that management says will dilute gross margins during the ramp-up period. The pattern across both names was the same: strong or in-line current results overshadowed by forward guidance that raised more questions than it answered.

Consumer

University of Michigan Sentiment Hits 54.4, Its Best Reading Since the Iran War Began in February

The University of Michigan's consumer sentiment gauge rose nearly 10% in July to 54.4, well above the Dow Jones consensus of 50.5 and its strongest level since the U.S.-Israel operation against Iran began in late February, though still down nearly 12% from a year earlier. Falling gasoline prices and improving inflation expectations drove the gain: the current-conditions measure rose 15.1% and the expectations index climbed 6.5%. The improvement is a rare bright spot in a week otherwise dominated by chip-sector selling and war headlines, and a reminder that the disinflationary pull from this month's oil-price pullback is starting to show up in household-level data even as Friday's Gulf-driven crude rally threatens to reverse it.

Berkshire

Buffett Warns of “Gambling” Markets as Berkshire Shares Rise on Safe-Haven Rotation

Warren Buffett used a CNBC interview this week to criticize a stock market he says is increasingly driven by speculative trading rather than long-term investing, telling Becky Quick it's "tough to find values when everybody is preferring gambling." The comments landed the same week investors rotated into Berkshire Hathaway's diversified mix of insurance, energy, railroad, and industrial businesses as a relative safe haven from the semiconductor selloff, with the stock among Friday's gainers even as the broader tape fell.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

The Week Ahead

Cooling Sentiment Data Meets a Renewed Oil Spike — the Fed's Two Competing Signals Head Into a Quiet Data Week

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh closed out back-to-back days of congressional testimony last week without tipping his hand on the July 28–29 FOMC meeting, and the data since has sent mixed signals in both directions. On one hand, June CPI and PPI both cooled and consumer sentiment jumped to its best level since February, evidence that the disinflationary pull from easing Gulf tensions earlier this month was working through the economy. On the other, Friday's Gulf escalation pushed WTI up more than 13% over five sessions and Brent above $87, reviving exactly the kind of energy-driven inflation impulse that overrode softer data in May and June. With no major economic releases scheduled until later in the week, the market's read on the July meeting will likely hinge on whether the latest round of strikes proves to be another contained flare-up or a genuine widening of the conflict.

Real Estate & Mortgage Rates

Weekend Rate Check

Freddie Mac's 30-Year Holds at 6.55% While Daily Trackers Diverge on Direction Into the Weekend

Freddie Mac's weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey, released Thursday, put the 30-year fixed-rate mortgage at 6.55% for the week ending July 16 — the third consecutive weekly increase and the highest reading since August 2025. Daily trackers are telling a more mixed story heading into the weekend: Bankrate's lender survey shows the average 30-year rate at 6.61% Saturday, while Zillow's marketplace data shows a 30-year rate of 6.48%, down four basis points from Friday as some of Friday's Treasury-market calm filtered through. The divergence reflects each source's methodology — Freddie Mac's weekly average captures applications submitted Monday through Wednesday, while Bankrate and Zillow update daily from different lender pools. The Mortgage Bankers Association still expects the 30-year to hold between 6.4% and 6.5% through year-end, with Fannie Mae's forecast at 6.4%, even as this week's Gulf-driven oil spike adds a fresh source of upward pressure on Treasury yields to watch.

Tax & Accounting

IRS & Security Summit

"Protect Your Clients; Protect Yourself" Campaign Kicks Off a Five-Week Push on Tax Pro Data Security

The IRS and its Security Summit partners launched their summer "Protect Your Clients; Protect Yourself" campaign this month, a five-week series aimed at giving tax professionals practical steps to guard client data against evolving identity-theft schemes ahead of the extension-filing crunch this fall. The campaign follows earlier July guidance that continues to phase in, including the IRS's new Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP) process for taxpayers with a strong history of on-time filing and payment, and final regulations identifying certain arrangements purporting to be Charitable Remainder Annuity Trusts as listed transactions. Practitioners handling extended returns over the coming weeks should treat this as a reminder to review multi-factor authentication, data-encryption practices, and staff training before the October 15 extension deadline arrives.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

A "Cooler" 87° Day Still Carries Real Heat Risk — Especially With Haze in the Air

After a stretch of mid-90s days earlier in the week, Saturday's forecast high near 87° can feel like a reprieve, but public health researchers caution that heat-related illness risk tracks humidity and air quality as much as raw temperature. Riana Pryor, who directs a hydration and thermoregulation lab, notes that the entire population is at some risk during a prolonged heat stretch, not just those with existing conditions. With smoke and haze possibly reducing air quality on top of continued humidity, the standard guidance applies: hydrate before you feel thirsty, take breaks in air conditioning rather than shade alone, avoid strenuous outdoor exertion during peak afternoon hours, and watch for dark urine as an early hydration warning sign. Anyone heading to Wrigley or watching the White Sox game outdoors this afternoon should plan around the highest heat-index window between roughly 2 and 6 PM, ahead of tonight's possible storm relief.

Random Fact

The Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint at the center of this week's fresh escalation, is only about 21 miles wide at its narrowest point — yet the U.S. Energy Information Administration estimates roughly a fifth of the world's total petroleum liquids consumption passes through it by tanker. That makes it, by a wide margin, the single most important oil chokepoint on Earth, which is a large part of why even contained strikes near Bandar Abbas move crude prices thousands of miles away within hours.

Word of the Day
capitulation

(noun, finance, from the Latin capitulare, "to draw up under headings," later "to surrender on terms") — the point in a selloff at which investors abandon their remaining positions en masse, often marking a selling climax rather than the start of a deeper decline. Traders will be watching next week to see whether the semiconductor sector's newly confirmed bear market produces genuine capitulation-style volume or continues grinding lower without one.
