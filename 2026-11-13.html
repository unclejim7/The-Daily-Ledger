Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Blood Test for p-tau217 Protein Can Flag Alzheimer's Risk Up to Ten Years Before Symptoms

A study published in JAMA pooled data from roughly 2,700 cognitively healthy older adults, 478 of whom went on to develop cognitive impairment during follow-up. Researchers found that people with very high blood levels of the p-tau217 protein had an estimated 38% risk of cognitive impairment within five years, rising to 78% over ten years based on a smaller subset of long-term data. Notably, the blood test added predictive value beyond brain imaging and genetic testing alone. The underlying test has recently received federal clearance, but researchers caution it remains too soon to recommend it broadly for people without symptoms, and say more diverse, longer-term studies are still needed before any widespread screening use.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

Tau proteins, the family p-tau217 belongs to, normally help stabilize the internal skeleton of brain cells; in Alzheimer's disease, misfolded tau accumulates into tangles that are one of the condition's two defining pathological hallmarks, alongside amyloid plaques.

Word of the Day
biomarker

(noun, from Greek bios "life" + "marker") — a measurable biological indicator of a disease process or condition, often detectable before symptoms appear. The p-tau217 blood test is exactly this kind of early-warning biomarker.
