Geopolitics · Markets · Monday Edition

Trump Warns Iran Will "Fail Economically" or See Its Leadership "Wiped Out" as Bessent Says Iranian Airlines Face a Global Shutdown Wednesday

President Trump escalated his economic and rhetorical pressure on Tehran Sunday into Monday, warning that Iran would fail economically or see its leadership wiped out if it didn't reach a deal, even as he said he'd be open to meeting Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian this week in New York. Iran's military pushed back hard, saying it had information that a new attack was being prepared against the country and vowing a harsh retaliatory response, while IRGC deputy commander Mostafa Izadi said Tehran continues strengthening its missile, drone, cyber, electronic-warfare, and air-defense capabilities.

The economic squeeze tightened further Monday when Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced that all Iranian airlines will be "shut down" worldwide starting Wednesday, September 23. The sanctions reach beyond the carriers themselves to the airports, fuel suppliers, and ticketing companies they depend on abroad, with Bessent warning that any company providing services to Iranian airlines risks being "knocked out of the dollar system" entirely. Mahan Air, one of Iran's largest carriers, had already begun suspending flights to Oman and Turkey ahead of the announcement.

On the ground, the Saudi-led coalition confirmed it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh, with no casualties or damage reported. Diplomatically, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi arrived in New York for the UN General Assembly after a stop in Qatar, ahead of Tuesday's dual spectacle: Trump's address to the Assembly and his planned meeting with six Gulf leaders, followed by Pezeshkian's own address Wednesday.

Markets, meanwhile, shrugged off the war headlines entirely. Monday delivered a broad rally led by AI-related names: the S&P 500 jumped 1.49% to 7,764.70, the Nasdaq surged 2.26% to a record close of 27,122.09, and the Dow added 366.19 points (+0.71%) to 52,048.83, as Intel popped 12% and AMD gained roughly 10% while oil prices and Treasury yields slipped. Bitcoin rode the same risk-on wave to an eight-month high above $85,000, even as gold eased to around $4,350. The 30-year mortgage rate held at 6.95%, with Freddie Mac's next weekly reading due Thursday.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Economic Warfare Escalation Often Signals Diplomatic Leverage-Building, Not War Preparation

Armstrong Economics has argued that sweeping economic measures like a global airline shutdown typically function as leverage ahead of negotiations rather than genuine preparation for further military escalation, since they impose costs without requiring additional military commitment. U.S. Alcohol Use Falls for the First Time Since the COVID-19 Pandemic

A new Keck Medicine of USC study published in Annals of Internal Medicine found that alcohol use among U.S. adults has declined for the first time since the pandemic began, though consumption levels remain higher than they were before COVID-19. Researchers describe the reversal as a modest but genuine shift after years of elevated drinking, while cautioning that rates still have room to fall before returning to pre-pandemic baselines.
