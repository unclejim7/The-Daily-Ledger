Health, Wellness & Fitness

Research: Straight-Line Head Impacts, Not Rotational Force, May Best Predict Concussion Risk

A Stanford-led study published in Annals of Biomedical Engineering found that straight-line, or linear, head acceleration may predict concussion risk more reliably than the rotational, twisting motion that helmet design has focused on reducing for years. Using motion-sensing mouthguards worn by athletes across seven sports, researchers found that an impact producing roughly 100g of linear acceleration carried about a 50% concussion risk, compared with an average football hit of only about 25g. The researchers caution the finding does not mean rotational impacts are safe or unrelated to concussion risk; rather, it suggests current helmet technology, largely optimized to reduce rotational force, may be solving only part of the underlying problem, a finding that could eventually reshape helmet design.

Word of the Day
linear acceleration

(noun, physics term, from Latin linea "line") — the rate of change of velocity along a straight path, as opposed to rotational acceleration, which involves spinning or twisting motion. Today's concussion research distinguishes sharply between the two kinds of head impact force.
