Federal Reserve · Geopolitics · Wednesday Edition

Fed Hikes Rates for the First Time Since 2023 as Houthis Claim a Strike on the Saudi Pipeline's Western Terminus

The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points Wednesday to a target range of 3.75%-4.00%, its first increase since 2023, in a unanimous 12-0 vote. The post-meeting statement was blunt about the reasoning: "inflation remains elevated." The committee's updated projections showed a strong majority expects the tightening to continue — 16 of 18 officials see at least one more hike before year's end, and four of those see two. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference did little to soften the message; markets, already lower into the announcement, extended their losses as Warsh's remarks were read as more hawkish than the statement alone suggested.

The decision landed the same day the Houthis claimed their most direct hit yet on Saudi Arabia's remaining oil infrastructure. The group said it struck the Saudi Aramco facility at Yanbu with dozens of ballistic missiles and attack drones, causing what it described as a large fire and extensive damage, though no independent evidence was released. Yanbu's significance is specific and severe if the claim holds: it's the western terminus of the pipeline that lets Saudi Arabia export crude across the peninsula without routing through the Strait of Hormuz — precisely the workaround the kingdom has leaned on since Hormuz traffic collapsed. The Houthis separately claimed a missile strike on King Khalid Air Base in Khamis Mushait. Neither claim has been independently verified.

Markets absorbed both stories at once. Wednesday afternoon, the Dow was down roughly 751 points, or about 1.4%, from Tuesday's close of 52,093.11, with the S&P 500 off around 0.5% and the Nasdaq down about 0.4% as the session played out; all three indexes had actually been higher earlier in the day before the Fed's announcement and Warsh's remarks turned sentiment. Tuesday itself had already been a down session (Dow -328.09 points/-0.63% to 52,093.11, S&P -0.45% to 7,585.73, Nasdaq -0.78% to 25,981.57) as Treasury yields hit multi-year highs ahead of the decision.

Crypto had its own rough 24 hours entirely apart from the Fed. The Senate's Clarity Act, a long-negotiated crypto market-structure bill, failed a cloture vote 49-50 Tuesday, falling short of the 60 needed to advance and effectively ending market-structure legislation in the Senate for the year. Bitcoin sold off sharply overnight on the news and now trades near $76,000, down from Tuesday's open near $78,189. Oil, meanwhile, eased modestly on an unexpected inventory build even as both benchmarks remain up more than 5% on the week amid the ongoing Middle East disruptions — a reminder that oil is currently trading on supply headlines while crypto and gold trade on the Fed, two forces that, as of Wednesday, are still pulling in different directions.

The First in Three Years

A Hike, Not a Cut, Marks a Real Regime Change

The Fed's last move before this one was a hike back in 2023 followed by a long hold and eventual cut cycle; resuming hikes now, rather than simply pausing longer, signals the committee sees this inflation episode as distinct from anything it's managed in the interim.

Unanimous Means Something

A 12-0 Vote Leaves No Dissent to Read Into

A split vote would have given markets a range of opinion to parse for hints about future policy; a unanimous decision instead signals the committee's internal debate is largely settled, at least for this meeting.

A Second Front for Saudi Oil

Yanbu Is a Different Kind of Target Than Hormuz

Striking the pipeline's western terminus, rather than tankers transiting a strait, targets fixed infrastructure that's much harder to reroute around — if verified, this would be a materially more damaging category of attack than most of what's preceded it.

A Quieter Crypto Story

The Clarity Act's Failure May Outlast the Fed Story

A stalled market-structure bill won't make headlines the way a rate hike does, but it removes a key source of institutional confidence crypto markets had been counting on heading into next year, with effects likely to linger well past this week's volatility.

Geopolitics

Unverified but Consequential

Even an Unconfirmed Claim Moves Markets

Oil traders don't wait for independent verification before pricing in risk, meaning the Yanbu claim's market impact is already real regardless of whether outside confirmation ever arrives — a dynamic that's defined much of this war's economic reporting.

A Widening List of Targets

Air Bases Now Join Pipelines and Tankers

Striking a military air base alongside civilian oil infrastructure in the same claimed operation suggests the Houthis are pursuing a broader campaign against Saudi capability generally, not narrowly focused on economic disruption alone.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Rate Hikes During Active Wars Are Historically Rare and Telling

Armstrong Economics has noted that central banks typically prefer easing, not tightening, policy during active regional conflicts, since war-driven supply shocks are usually treated as transitory. A Fed choosing to hike anyway, explicitly citing elevated inflation during a live two-front energy crisis, reads on the site as confirmation that policymakers now view this inflation as structural rather than a passing wartime spike — exactly the kind of regime shift the Economic Confidence Model flags as significant.

Random Fact

The Federal Reserve's "dot plot," officially the Summary of Economic Projections, anonymously charts each of the 19 FOMC participants' individual rate forecasts as a single dot — meaning the widely quoted "16 of 18" figures analysts cite are literally counted by hand from a scatter chart, not drawn from any named individual's public statement.

Word of the Day
cloture

(noun, from the French clôture, "closing") — a Senate procedure to end debate on a bill and force a vote, requiring 60 of 100 votes to invoke. The Clarity Act's failure to reach cloture at 49-50 means the bill remains technically alive but functionally stalled, unable to even reach a final up-or-down vote.
