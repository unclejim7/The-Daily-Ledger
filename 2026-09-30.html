Geopolitics · Iran · Wednesday Edition

Trump Says Iran Is "Doing Very Poorly" and Will "Give Up," Even as Araghchi Awaits a Formal Response to Its Seven-Point Hormuz Plan

President Trump shifted his public tone Tuesday from denial to prediction, telling reporters he doesn't know if Iran is "going to give up yet" but insisting flatly that "they'll give up," because the country is "doing very poorly" in the war. He amplified the point on Truth Social, posting "Iran Has Lost Control of the Strait" while sharing an analysis from Swiss energy analyst and investor Alexander Stahel — letting an independent voice make the substantive claim while Trump himself supplied only the triumphant framing.

Iran's own account of where talks stand is notably more procedural than defeated. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran is still awaiting a formal U.S. response to the seven-point proposal it transmitted through Qatari mediators, insisting Iran has made no concession on its nuclear program: "Our position is clear, and the only issue under discussion is the Strait of Hormuz," he said, adding that reopening the strait "is contingent upon the fulfillment of a set of conditions we have conveyed to the other side." Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf's warning from a day earlier — that no regional infrastructure would be safe if Iran's security isn't guaranteed — went unrepeated Tuesday, but it didn't need restating; it's still doing its work as leverage in the background. Iran's rial, meanwhile, kept sliding past its fresh record low near 2.5 million to the dollar.

Markets logged a third consecutive down session Tuesday, though a comparatively mild one: the Dow fell 131.59 points (-0.26%) to 51,349.92, the S&P 500 slipped 0.16% to 7,670.84, and the Nasdaq eased 0.09% to 26,797.54, as the 10-year Treasury yield held near 5.26%, still its highest since 2007. Boeing, Monday's biggest single-stock casualty on its 737 MAX 10 certification delay, bounced back somewhat in Tuesday's session as investors reassessed the software-fix timeline.

Bitcoin held steady near $83,800 and gold pushed toward $4,200 Wednesday morning, both relatively calm after Monday's whipsaw. The bigger surprise came in housing: the 30-year mortgage rate, which had held at 7.03% for weeks, jumped to roughly 7.36% on the latest daily readings — its highest level in more than a year — as elevated long-term yields finally worked their way into mortgage pricing ahead of Thursday's official weekly survey.

A New Rhetorical Register

Trump's Framing Shifts From Denial to Predicted Collapse

Moving from "I offered them nothing" to "they'll give up" is a shift from defending a specific claim to making a broader forecast — a framing that's harder to fact-check in the moment and easier to declare vindicated later, whichever way talks actually go.

Still Waiting

A Formal Response Iran Says Hasn't Arrived Yet

Araghchi's insistence that Tehran is still awaiting a formal reply to its seven-point plan reframes the standoff as a matter of process rather than principle — a framing that keeps Iran's own position intact while quietly pressuring Washington to move next.

Borrowed Credibility

Citing an Independent Analyst Lets Trump Claim Victory at a Distance

Amplifying someone else's technical assessment that Iran has "lost control of the strait" gives the president a headline-ready claim without requiring the administration to stand behind the underlying data itself.

A Slide With No Floor in Sight

The Rial Keeps Falling Even When It Isn't the Lead Story

That Iran's currency extended its record-low slide on a day when it barely made the ticker underscores how thoroughly economic deterioration has become the war's background condition rather than a discrete news event.

Diplomacy by Deadline Avoidance

Waiting for a "Formal Response" Is Itself a Negotiating Tactic

Framing the holdup as Washington's silence rather than Tehran's terms lets Iran avoid the appearance of stalling while keeping the pressure of an unanswered proposal squarely on the U.S. side of the table.

The Threat That Doesn't Need Repeating

Ghalibaf's Warning Looms Over Talks Without Being Restated

A threat doesn't have to be repeated daily to keep functioning as leverage; once stated clearly, it can simply sit in the background, shaping how every subsequent Iranian statement gets read by the other side.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Rhetoric That Predicts an Opponent's Collapse Often Signals a Negotiator's Own Uncertainty

Armstrong Economics has long argued that leaders lean hardest into predictions of an adversary's imminent capitulation precisely when their own side lacks a concrete, verifiable win to point to — using confident forecasting as a substitute for the specific concession they can't yet claim. The site's War Cycle model reads Trump's pivot from "I offered them nothing" to "they'll give up" as a sign the administration may be further from a finished deal than the rhetoric implies, not closer to one.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Insomnia Linked to a 26% Higher Risk of Stroke, Multi-Country Review Finds

A review published in Sleep Medicine Reviews, drawing on roughly 20 prior studies conducted across Austria, Germany, Italy, and Switzerland involving millions of participants combined, found that people with insomnia face a 26% higher risk of stroke than those without it. Researchers say the findings add to growing evidence that chronic sleep disruption isn't just a quality-of-life issue but a measurable cardiovascular risk factor deserving the same clinical attention as blood pressure or cholesterol.
