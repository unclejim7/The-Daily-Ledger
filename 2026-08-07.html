Markets · Economy · Friday Edition

July Payrolls Unexpectedly Shrink by 23,000, Fueling Rate-Cut Bets — as Iran Weighs Barring U.S. and Israeli Ships From a Reopened Hormuz

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday that nonfarm payrolls fell by a seasonally adjusted 23,000 in July — the first monthly decline in months and a sharp miss against the Dow Jones consensus forecast for an 83,000 gain. June's already-soft reading was revised down further, to just 20,000 jobs added. The unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1%, but the improvement came with a catch: the labor force participation rate slipped to 61.4%, its lowest level in more than five years, meaning the drop owes as much to Americans leaving the workforce as to hiring strength.

Futures rose Friday morning on the print, with contracts on the Dow up 0.1%, the S&P 500 up 0.3%, and the Nasdaq-100 up 0.6%, as traders bet the soft data raises the odds that Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's committee cuts rates at its September meeting — a notable shift from the "well positioned" language New York Fed President John Williams had used just days earlier. The reaction follows a Thursday session that pulled back from Wednesday's record: the Dow fell 464.02 points, or 0.85%, to 53,885, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.18% to 7,709.96 and the Nasdaq eased 0.06% to 26,348.35, as rising oil prices and a batch of disappointing tech earnings weighed on sentiment.

Meanwhile the Strait of Hormuz talks remain snagged on the same wording dispute that stalled progress Thursday. Iranian lawmakers are reportedly debating draft language for the Oman-brokered deal that would bar U.S. and Israeli-flagged vessels from the strait outright and require "hostile" nations to pay compensation before their ships could transit — a provision that cuts directly against Washington's demand for toll-free passage with no approval requirements. Trump told reporters late Thursday the talks are "moving along" but offered no further detail when pressed, leaving the same gap between "close" and "finished" that has defined this negotiation for weeks.

The cross-currents show up clearly across markets: gold is rallying toward its best week since January, near $4,380 an ounce, even as oil holds near $75 a barrel, down roughly 10% on the week on hopes the strait genuinely reopens. The 10-year Treasury yield eased toward 4.60% as the weak jobs data reinforced the case for a September cut, a case that had looked far shakier just a few days ago when Iran-driven oil risk was pushing hike odds higher.

Payrolls Post a Rare Monthly Decline

Rate-Cut Bets

September Odds Jump on the Weak Print

Futures traders moved quickly to price in a higher probability of a September cut from Chair Kevin Warsh's Fed, a sharp reversal from the "well positioned" framing New York Fed President John Williams offered just days before the report landed.

Federal Reserve & Inflation

Warsh's First Test

A Weak Jobs Report Puts the Fed's "Well Positioned" Line to the Test

The FOMC held its target range at 3.50–3.75% at the July 28–29 meeting, with three officials dissenting in what was described as one of the most unpredictable meetings in years under new Chair Kevin Warsh, who has been notably reticent about forward guidance in his first weeks on the job. New York Fed President John Williams said days ago that rates were "well positioned" with inflation expected to ease through the second half of the year; Friday's surprise payrolls decline complicates that framing considerably, and traders are now betting the September meeting brings a cut rather than another hold.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Major Review Links Restricting Specific Amino Acids to Healthier Aging

Researchers from the University of Wisconsin-Madison pulled together 350 studies on dietary protein restriction in a review published in Cell Press Blue, finding that reducing intake of specific amino acids — particularly isoleucine and methionine — rather than simply cutting total protein, was linked to improved mitochondrial function, lower cellular oxidative damage, and longer lifespans in animal studies. Independent scientists cautioned that most of the underlying research comes from rodent and insect studies with limited human data so far, and that protein needs should stay personalized to age and activity level, since very low protein intake carries its own risks to immune function and bone density.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

The monthly U.S. jobs report is actually two separate surveys stitched together: the establishment survey polls roughly 631,000 worksites for the payroll count, while a household survey of about 60,000 families produces the unemployment rate — different methodologies that can and do send conflicting signals, exactly as they did Friday, when payrolls fell even as the jobless rate ticked down.
