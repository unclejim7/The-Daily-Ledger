Inflation · Markets · Tuesday Edition

Markets Brace for October's Inflation Verdict as Hassett Warns a Hot Print Could Force the Fed's Hand

The Bureau of Labor Statistics releases October's CPI report at 8:30 a.m. ET today, with forecasters broadly expecting inflation to cool from September's hot 3.7% annual pace. TD Securities projects headline inflation rose just 0.30% month-over-month, down from September's 0.65%, with core inflation slowing to 0.21% from 0.33%. White House National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett has already framed the stakes bluntly: "If October comes out really high, then I think the Fed would probably appropriately act." This morning's edition goes to press roughly ninety minutes before the number itself is known.

Markets drifted modestly lower ahead of the report, with the S&P 500 slipping 0.1% to 7,671.20, the Dow roughly flat, and the Nasdaq down 0.1% — a classic pre-data holding pattern rather than any real conviction in either direction. Brent crude held near $118 a barrel; notably, Goldman Sachs has lifted its own December Brent forecast citing continued Gulf supply risk, a rare instance of outside analysts validating the kind of elevated pricing this publication has tracked for weeks.

Bitcoin climbed to roughly $81,500, a tenth consecutive day of gains, and the 30-year mortgage holds at 7.31% ahead of Thursday's new reading. Chicago enjoyed an unseasonably mild, sunny Tuesday, a welcome break before cooler air is expected to return later in the week.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Markets Pricing Risk Quietly Is Itself a War Cycle Signal

Armstrong Economics has long argued that institutional risk models, like Goldman's own forecast revisions, often reflect the market's genuine read on a conflict's trajectory more honestly than day-to-day headlines do. The site's War Cycle model treats a major bank's forecast increase as evidence that professional risk assessment still sees this conflict as a live, unresolved threat to supply, regardless of how quiet the news cycle has been lately.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: High Ultra-Processed Food Intake Linked to a 58% Higher Dementia Risk in Older Adults

A Harvard analysis of nearly 5,400 older U.S. adults followed for close to nine years, published in the American Journal of Public Health, found that the highest consumers of ultra-processed food had a 58% higher risk of developing dementia than the lowest consumers. Researchers acknowledge the possibility of reverse causation, where early, undiagnosed cognitive decline could itself drive dietary changes. A separate Australian study found higher ultra-processed food intake linked to risk factors like hypertension and obesity and to poorer attention scores, though it found no direct link to memory loss specifically, underscoring how much remains unsettled about the exact mechanism at work.

Random Fact

The Bureau of Labor Statistics collects roughly 94,000 individual price quotes from about 23,000 retail and service businesses nationwide every month to build a single Consumer Price Index figure.

Word of the Day
disinflation

(noun, from Latin dis- "reduction" + inflare "to blow into") — a slowing in the rate of inflation, distinct from deflation, where prices actually fall. Forecasters are betting on disinflation in today's report — prices still rising, just more slowly than in September.
