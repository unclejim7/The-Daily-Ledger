Retail Sales · Fed · Wednesday Edition

October Retail Sales Land in Line With Forecasts as Markets Await the Fed's Minutes

October retail sales rose a modest 0.3% month-over-month, roughly in line with economist expectations and a clear step down from September's surprise 0.8% beat. The unremarkable print gave markets one less thing to worry about heading into this afternoon's release of minutes from the Fed's late-October meeting, the first detailed look inside Chair Kevin Warsh's deliberations since a string of hawkish regional Fed commentary revived talk of a possible December rate hike.

Equities edged higher on the in-line reading, with the S&P 500 gaining 0.2% to 7,684.53. Bitcoin's tentative stabilization proved short-lived, with BTC slipping back to roughly $63,850, giving up part of Tuesday's modest gain — a reminder that one green day rarely resolves a correction this sharp on its own. Markets will be parsing this afternoon's FOMC minutes closely for signs of how united Warsh's more hawkish colleagues remain after several meetings featuring open dissent toward further tightening.

Brent crude held steady near $118 through an eleventh straight quiet week in the Gulf. The Maple Leafs visit the Blackhawks tonight at the United Center, and Chicago turned sharply colder and breezier, with a chance of the season's first flurries by evening.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: High-Dose Flu Vaccine Linked to a Stronger Reduction in Alzheimer's Risk Than the Standard Dose

Researchers at UTHealth Houston, publishing in the journal Neurology, analyzed nearly 200,000 adults age 65 and older and found that those who received a high-dose flu vaccine had roughly 55% lower odds of developing Alzheimer's disease compared with unvaccinated peers, versus about 40% lower odds for recipients of the standard-dose vaccine. The protective association also appeared more consistent and longer-lasting in women than in men. The study builds on earlier research from the same group showing any flu vaccination was linked to a roughly 40% lower Alzheimer's risk, but notes this is an observational study comparing vaccinated groups to each other rather than a controlled trial, so it cannot prove the vaccine directly causes the reduced risk.
