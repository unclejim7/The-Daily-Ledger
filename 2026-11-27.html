Chicago Sports · Markets · Friday Edition

Bears End Four-Game Lions Skid on Thanksgiving, Improve to 9-2, as Markets Reopen for Black Friday

The Bears snapped a four-game losing streak in the rivalry Thursday, holding off Detroit 27-23 at Ford Field to improve to 9-2 and settle real NFC North seeding implications in Chicago's favor heading into December. It's the kind of Thanksgiving result this franchise hasn't had in years, and it caps a five-game winning streak that has reshaped the division race.

Markets reopened Friday for a shortened Black Friday session, with equities trading higher in thin holiday volume ahead of today's 1 p.m. ET close. Bitcoin extended its bounce to roughly $61,280, a gain of better than 0.9% from Thursday's level and its second straight higher session off the brush with this publication's all-time tracked low. Brent crude held steady near $118 through a twentieth straight quiet week in the Gulf.

A delayed Freddie Mac reading, pushed back by the Thanksgiving closure, finally arrived Friday and ticked the 30-year mortgage rate down to 7.21%, a small bright spot for the holiday shopping weekend. Chicago itself turned colder and mostly sunny behind Thursday's system, with a high near 35°F.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Night-to-Night Sleep Schedule Swings Linked to 20-23% Higher Cardiovascular Risk

Research presented earlier this month at the American Heart Association's Scientific Sessions, drawing on more than a million nights of objective sleep data from the NIH's All of Us program, found that shifting bedtimes from night to night over roughly six months was associated with a 20 to 23% higher risk of heart attack, stroke, and heart failure combined. Each additional 29 minutes of night-to-night variation in sleep duration was tied to a 24% higher risk of that same combined outcome. The findings are preliminary, have not yet been peer-reviewed, and come from an observational study that cannot establish that irregular sleep itself causes the added risk, researchers caution, though the scale of the dataset gives the association unusual statistical weight.

Random Fact

"Black Friday" as a retail term dates to 1950s Philadelphia, where police used it to describe the chaos of shoppers and tourists flooding the city the day after Thanksgiving, well before it became associated with store profits.

Word of the Day
chronotype

(noun) — an individual's natural inclination toward sleeping and waking at particular times, shaped by internal circadian rhythm. Researchers studying sleep regularity note that a mismatch between a person's chronotype and their schedule can itself drive the night-to-night variability examined in today's featured study.
