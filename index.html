Markets · Federal Reserve · Tuesday Edition

Oil Tops $111 a Barrel as Chip Stocks Resume Their Selloff, Clouding the Picture Before the Fed's Pivotal Meeting

Brent crude pushed above $111 a barrel Tuesday, a fresh high for the war, as persistent tension around the Strait of Hormuz kept energy markets on edge even without a single new headline-grabbing incident. At the same time, a renewed selloff in chip stocks dragged the Nasdaq lower, reviving the AI-valuation worries that triggered last week's rout in the first place and suggesting Monday's rebound was more relief than resolution.

The S&P 500 fell 0.4% to 7,627.49 and the Nasdaq dropped 0.9% to 26,102.95, while the Dow managed a marginal 0.1% gain to 51,373.54. The moves land just a week before Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first major decision: the central bank already raised rates a quarter point in September, its first hike since 2023, and now faces a genuinely contested call on whether to hike again October 27–28. The most recent reconcilable odds put the probability of another hike near 37%, down from over 50% just weeks earlier — a reminder of just how quickly sentiment has swung in both directions this month.

Bitcoin slipped to roughly $81,900 as the renewed risk aversion in both chips and oil spread into crypto, trimming some of Monday's rebound. The 30-year mortgage rate holds at 7.46% ahead of Thursday's next weekly reading.

There was better news out of Tampa: the Rays clinched the American League pennant Monday night, beating the Guardians 7-3 in Game 6 to take the series 4-2 with no Game 7 needed. Tampa Bay now advances to face the Milwaukee Brewers — who haven't reached the World Series since 1982 — with Game 1 set for Friday at American Family Field.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Medicare's New Peer-Support Billing Codes Face Early Implementation Hurdles

A report examining Medicare's new billing codes for peer support specialists — trained individuals with lived experience who help patients manage mental health and substance use conditions — finds the rollout facing early friction, including low provider uptake, confusion over documentation requirements, and reimbursement delays. Advocates say the codes represent an important recognition of peer support as a legitimate part of care, but warn the implementation hurdles could blunt the policy's intended impact if left unaddressed.
