Inflation · Markets · Veterans Day Edition

October CPI Comes In Right on Target, Clearing the Way for a Relief Rally as Bitcoin Finally Breaks $82,000

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' October CPI report landed essentially where forecasters expected Tuesday morning — a meaningfully cooler read than September's hot 3.7% annual pace, consistent with the disinflation case TD Securities and others had been making ahead of the release. The in-line print gave markets exactly what Kevin Hassett's binary framing from Tuesday's edition implied they needed: with no hot surprise to force the White House's hand, the data removed one of the last major uncertainties hanging over trading desks heading into December.

Stocks rallied on the news, with the S&P 500 gaining 0.5% to close at 7,708.83, the Dow adding roughly the same to 52,663.30, and the Nasdaq outperforming with a 0.6% gain to 26,053.90. Brent crude held steady near $118 a barrel, with the Gulf supply-risk premium this publication has tracked for weeks still fully priced in regardless of the domestic inflation outcome.

The day's bigger milestone belonged to Bitcoin, which finally broke through the $82,000 level this publication has been watching for days, trading near $82,350 on an eleventh consecutive day of gains. The 30-year mortgage held at 7.31% ahead of Thursday's new Freddie Mac reading. The nation marked Veterans Day Wednesday; bond markets observed the holiday closure while equities traded a full regular session.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Implantable Device Lowers Blood Sugar Without Insulin by Converting Excess Glucose Into Gluconate

ETH Zurich researchers have developed an implantable device, nicknamed ECHO, that pairs with a commercial continuous glucose monitor and lowers blood sugar without delivering insulin or any drug. When blood sugar runs high, a smartphone signal activates an electrocatalytic unit inside the implant that converts excess glucose directly into gluconate, which the body then clears through urine. In diabetic mice, blood sugar returned to a normal range within about an hour of activation, and after six weeks of use, treated mice had lost up to roughly 10% of their body weight with lower triglycerides and no detected hypoglycemia. The work, published in the journal Science, remains at the animal-testing stage; human trials have not yet begun.

Random Fact

Veterans Day traces its origin to Armistice Day, marking the November 11, 1918 armistice that ended fighting in World War I at the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month — it was renamed Veterans Day in 1954 to honor veterans of all wars.

Word of the Day
gluconeogenesis

(noun, from Greek glykys "sweet" + neos "new" + genesis "creation") — the metabolic process by which the body generates glucose from non-carbohydrate sources. Today's featured health research runs the process in reverse of sorts, converting excess glucose into a compound the body simply excretes.
