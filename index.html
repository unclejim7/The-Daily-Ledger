Jobs Report · Markets · Friday Edition

October Payrolls Come In Soft as Bitcoin Crosses $80,000 for the First Time in Weeks

October's nonfarm payrolls report showed the U.S. economy added a modest 85,000 jobs, with the unemployment rate ticking up to 4.3%, continuing a gradual softening trend in the labor market that has persisted for much of the year. Markets read the report as dovish for the Fed's December meeting, rising on renewed hope that a cooling labor market gives Chair Kevin Warsh more room to cut rather than hold again. President Trump, who has previously argued that "with a great jobs report, like just announced, stocks should go up, not down," has made no secret of his preference for lower rates regardless of the data.

The S&P 500 rose 0.2% to 7,670.96, the Dow added a similar amount, and the Nasdaq gained 0.3%. Bitcoin crossed $80,000 for the first time in weeks, extending its win streak to seven straight sessions and finally clearing the level this publication has tracked as resistance since the Binance hack. Brent crude held near $118 a barrel, with no new incident reported overnight in the Gulf for the first time in several days.

The 30-year mortgage holds at 7.31%, with no new Freddie Mac reading until next Thursday. Tonight's calm extends to the sports pages too: the Blackhawks beat Florida 4-2 Thursday, with captain Connor Bedard notching two points in his return from shoulder surgery, and the Bears, at 6-2, prepare to host Tampa Bay Sunday night at Soldier Field. Milwaukee's long-awaited World Series victory parade finally arrives tomorrow.

CDC: U.S. Measles Cases Top 4,000 for the Year Amid Declining Vaccination Rates

The CDC's measles count for 2026 has climbed past 4,000 confirmed cases across more than 30 states, a figure that significantly outpaces recent years, while independent tracking from Johns Hopkins shows a similar trajectory. The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

The monthly U.S. jobs report is compiled from two separate surveys — a survey of roughly 121,000 businesses and government agencies, and a separate household survey of about 60,000 homes — which is why the payrolls and unemployment-rate figures occasionally seem to tell slightly different stories about the same month.

Word of the Day
countervailing

(adjective, from Latin contra "against" + valere "to be strong") — having equal or opposing force, offsetting another factor. A cooling labor market and above-target inflation are genuinely countervailing pressures on the Fed right now, pulling its December decision in opposite directions at once.
