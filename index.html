Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Weekly Park and Playground Visits in Childhood Linked to Better Mental Health Outcomes

Researchers analyzing more than 4,200 children from the long-running "Children of the 90s" cohort study in the UK, published in the Journal of Epidemiology & Community Health, found that children taken to a park or playground at least weekly from birth through age eleven scored roughly 16.5% better on mental health assessments than children who visited less often. The study's authors frame parks and playgrounds as more than simple recreational space, calling them "an important resource for children's healthy development." They caution that the findings are observational — showing an association rather than proving the visits directly caused the better outcomes — and say further research is needed to confirm whether the pattern holds across more diverse and urban communities.

Random Fact

The "Children of the 90s" study (formally ALSPAC) has followed more than 14,000 families in the Bristol, England area since the early 1990s, making it one of the longest-running birth cohort studies in the world.

Word of the Day
cohort study

(noun, from Latin cohors "company, group") — a research design that follows the same group of people over time to observe how their outcomes develop. Today's park-visit research, and several other items featured in this publication's health section, rely on exactly this method.
