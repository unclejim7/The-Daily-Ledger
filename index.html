Chicago Sports · Markets · Sunday Edition

Milwaukee's Parade Draws Huge Crowds as the Bears Prepare to Host Tampa Bay Tonight

Milwaukee's long-delayed World Series victory parade finally rolled through downtown Saturday, drawing enormous crowds nearly two weeks after the Brewers clinched the franchise's first championship in 57 years. The celebration capped a stretch that began with Game 5's clincher and ended, fittingly, on the kind of crisp, clear November Saturday that makes for good parade weather.

Closer to home, the Bears close out their weekend hosting Tampa Bay tonight at Soldier Field, with kickoff at 8:20 p.m. ET on NBC's Sunday Night Football. Chicago enters at 6-2 against a 3-5 Buccaneers squad built around a ball-control running game, with the Bears favored by roughly a touchdown.

Markets are closed for the weekend after Friday's gains left the S&P 500 at 7,670.96. Brent crude holds near $118 a barrel through a second consecutive quiet night in the Gulf, and Bitcoin edged higher to roughly $80,600, an eighth straight day of gains. The 30-year mortgage holds at 7.31%, with no new Freddie Mac reading until Thursday. October's CPI report arrives Tuesday, the next real data point ahead of the Fed's December decision.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Daily Avocado and Mango Intake Linked to Improved Vascular Health in Prediabetic Adults

A study from the Illinois Institute of Technology, published in the Journal of the American Heart Association, found that adults with prediabetes who ate one avocado and one cup of mango daily for eight weeks saw a meaningful improvement in flow-mediated dilation, a marker of blood vessel function, compared with a control group that saw the same marker decline. Diastolic blood pressure also improved in the intervention group, though the small, short-term trial found no significant differences in cholesterol, blood sugar, or inflammation markers. The study was supported in part by industry grants from mango and avocado trade groups, which researchers note played no role in the study's design or findings.
