Geopolitics · Markets · Friday Edition

Houthi Attacks on Saudi Arabia Push Oil Toward $114 as World Series Game 1 Arrives Tonight

Houthi forces launched a fresh wave of attacks on Saudi Arabia Friday, adding further pressure to oil markets already rattled by Thursday's reported mass tanker attack in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude, which surged 4% to $113.17 a barrel Thursday — its highest level since May — continued climbing toward $114 in Friday trading. Separately, an IRGC official claimed that “traffic of violating vessels in the Strait of Hormuz has been completely stopped,” an assertion that, like so many Iranian claims this week, arrived without independent verification.

Markets extended Thursday's sharp reversal into Friday, with the S&P 500, Dow, and Nasdaq all finishing lower again as the oil-driven inflation worry that ended this week's relief rally showed no sign of easing. Bitcoin slipped further to roughly $81,600, extending Thursday's slide back below the $84,200 level it had only briefly reclaimed earlier in the week, amid continued ETF outflows and a broader risk-off mood tied to the Hormuz escalation.

Fed rate-hike odds for the October 27–28 meeting held steady near 16%, little changed despite two straight days of oil-driven market turbulence — a sign markets still read this week's shock as a geopolitical risk premium rather than a lasting inflation driver. The 30-year mortgage rate holds at 7.38% following Thursday's decline, the first in weeks.

Tonight brings a welcome distraction: the World Series opens at Milwaukee, with the Brewers' Jacob Misiorowski facing the Rays' Nick Martinez and Milwaukee favored at home in its first Series appearance since 1982. Locally, the Blackhawks host the Montreal Canadiens at the United Center.

A Second Front Opens

Houthi Strikes on Saudi Arabia Broaden the Conflict's Geography

Attacks landing outside the strait itself, on the Arabian Peninsula's mainland, complicate any narrative that frames this purely as a Hormuz shipping dispute — and give oil markets a second, independent reason to stay elevated even if Hormuz traffic stabilizes.

A Claim Worth Doubting

"Completely Stopped" Is a Strong Word for an Unverifiable Claim

An IRGC official's assertion that violating traffic has fully ceased fits a familiar pattern of maximalist Iranian claims that rarely survive contact with independent shipping data, which has repeatedly shown continued, if reduced, transit activity.

Two Days, Not One

A Reversal That Holds Into a Second Session Is a Different Story Than a Single Bad Day

Markets shrugging off a one-day shock is normal; extending the selloff into Friday suggests traders are genuinely repricing risk rather than just reacting to a single headline.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Just Four Minutes of Daily Strength Training Improved Mobility in Older Adults

A Penn State study found that older adults who performed as little as four minutes of strength training per day saw measurable improvements in mobility and functional movement after twelve weeks, a notably small time investment for a meaningful result. Researchers say the findings suggest that consistency matters more than volume for older adults building or maintaining strength, and that even brief daily sessions can meaningfully offset age-related mobility decline.
