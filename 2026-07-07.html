Geopolitics · Markets · Tuesday Edition

Oil Tanker Struck Near Hormuz as Khamenei's Funeral Moves to Qom — Wall Street's Record Run Meets an Overnight Chip Wobble

A tanker was reportedly struck by an unknown projectile late Monday while sailing south through the Gulf of Oman near the Strait of Hormuz, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations authority, which said the vessel caught fire roughly eight nautical miles east of Limah, Oman, after being hit on its port side. No casualties or environmental impact have been reported, and UKMTO is advising vessels to transit the area with caution. Axios reported that Iran had resumed attacks in the strait, an unwelcome signal just as the broader diplomatic track had appeared to be gaining ground — a reminder that the stand-down reached after last week's exchange remains fragile even as both governments observe a funeral-week pause in formal talks.

That pause coincides with the fifth day of Ali Khamenei's six-day state funeral, which moves to the clerical hub of Qom today for prayers at the Jamkaran Mosque, one of Shia Islam's most visited pilgrimage sites. The procession's largest single day came Monday in Tehran, with crowds state media described as exceeding the estimated one million who turned out for Qassem Soleimani's 2020 funeral. Wednesday brings the journey's most symbolically loaded leg — Khamenei's body travels to Iraq's holy cities of Najaf and Karbala — before burial Thursday at the Imam Reza shrine in his native Mashhad. Qatari and Pakistani mediators say last week's indirect U.S.-Iran talks in Doha made "positive progress" on the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding before both sides agreed to pause until the mourning period ends; unresolved issues include Iran's proposed toll on Hormuz shipping and the unfreezing of Iranian assets abroad.

Wall Street enters Tuesday fresh off a record session: the Dow Jones Industrial Average closed above 53,000 for the first time Monday, up 0.29% to 53,055.91, while the Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.12% and the S&P 500 gained 0.72% to 7,537.43 as chip stocks shook off their late-June slump and Big Tech names including Alphabet, Apple, Meta, and Tesla rallied. The mood turned more cautious overnight: Nasdaq 100 futures fell roughly 0.9% to 1.1% and S&P futures slipped about 0.3% as renewed selling hit Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix in Asia, with the Kospi tumbling nearly 5% and briefly triggering a circuit breaker. Samsung reports quarterly results Tuesday, expected to show an 18-fold year-on-year profit surge, while SK Hynix proceeds with a roughly $28 billion Nasdaq depositary-receipt listing — one of the largest AI-era share offerings ever. SpaceX, meanwhile, officially joins the Nasdaq-100 today under newly created "fast-track entry" rules for mega-IPOs, less than a month after its record-setting debut. Separately, Microsoft confirmed it is cutting 4,800 jobs, about 2.1% of its workforce, with the Xbox division absorbing roughly a fifth of the losses as the company continues restructuring around AI.

Bitcoin is holding firm near $63,100 Tuesday morning, extending a July rally that has taken it more than 6% higher for the month and roughly 10% off the 21-month low near $58,278 hit just one week ago. Locally, both the Cubs and White Sox are back in action tonight after Monday's rest day — Chicago opens a three-game set at Baltimore while the White Sox host Boston at Rate Field — against a backdrop of markets trying to decide whether Monday's record close or Tuesday's chip-stock jitters better capture where the AI trade goes from here.

Tax & Accounting

Three Days Left: COVID-Era Penalty Refund Claims Under the Kwong Decision Are Due July 10

The window to claim refunds or abatements of penalties and interest the IRS assessed during the roughly 3.5-year COVID-19 disaster period narrows to three days. Individuals, small businesses, estates, and trusts must file Form 843, Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement, by July 10 to preserve their rights following Kwong v. United States, which calls into question the IRS's authority to assess those charges. Practitioners are advising clients with any uncertainty about eligibility to file protective claims now rather than risk missing the deadline.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Scientists Identify a Molecular “Switch” That Explains Why Exercise Keeps Aging Muscles Healthy

New research published this week identifies a specific gene, DEAF1, whose activity drops in response to physical exercise — a shift that appears to unlock older muscles' ability to clear out cellular damage, repair tissue, and maintain function over time. Researchers say the finding helps explain a pattern long observed but poorly understood: why regular movement so reliably preserves muscle quality in aging bodies, even at modest intensity. The discovery points toward a specific molecular pathway rather than a vague "use it or lose it" effect, and researchers say it could eventually inform targeted therapies for people who are unable to exercise, though the practical takeaway for now remains what it has always been — consistent movement matters at a cellular level.

Random Fact

Today's prayers take place at the Jamkaran Mosque outside Qom, one of the most-visited sites in Shia Islam despite being a relatively modern structure by Iranian standards. Its significance traces to an account from 984 CE, in which a local man reported an encounter near the site with the Twelfth Imam, a figure many Shia Muslims believe remains in occultation and will one day return. The mosque has since become a focal point for devotional practice tied to that belief — a fitting stop on a funeral route already saturated with symbolism linking Khamenei's death to Shia Islam's foundational narratives of martyrdom and awaited justice.

Word of the Day
flashpoint

(noun, mid-20th century, from chemistry via military/political usage) — originally the temperature at which a substance's vapor will ignite; now used to describe a place or situation where conflict or violence is especially likely to erupt. The Strait of Hormuz has functioned as exactly that kind of flashpoint throughout 2026 — a narrow waterway where a single incident, like Monday's tanker strike, can instantly reprice oil, unsettle markets, and threaten a diplomatic process that took months to build.
