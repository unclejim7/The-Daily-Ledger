Geopolitics · Markets · Monday Edition

Trump Calls Off “Massive” Strike on Iran, Announces New Talks Monday — Oil Plunges, Wall Street Rallies to Start August

The weekend's carefully staged buildup toward striking Iran's energy infrastructure ended not with strikes but with a stand-down. President Trump told reporters aboard Air Force One Sunday that he had called off what would have been a massive attack, saying bluntly, "it would have been the biggest attack since World War II." Trump said the decision came partly in response to pleas from regional allies, including Saudi Arabia, who had lobbied against a strike campaign they worried would destabilize the broader region and, not incidentally, their own energy infrastructure and economies. In its place, Trump announced new talks with Iran would begin Monday afternoon, covering both the Strait of Hormuz and, ultimately, Iran's denuclearization.

Iran's response arrived within hours and followed a pattern that has repeated throughout this war: flat denial. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei told reporters "we currently do not have negotiations with America," adding that Iran had no plans to host foreign delegations or send negotiators abroad in the coming days. It's nearly the same dynamic that played out the week before last, when Trump described being "in talks" with Iran only for Tehran to deny it within hours—a pattern reliable enough now that markets seem to be discounting the denials rather than the announcements, at least for now.

Wall Street, in any case, treated Sunday's stand-down as real news. The S&P 500 climbed 0.64%, the Dow surged 1.2%, and the Nasdaq added 0.49% to open the week, while oil did the most dramatic repricing of all: WTI crude plunged 4.7% to $80.72 as the attack risk premium that had been building since Friday deflated almost as quickly as it formed. Gold, which had dipped alongside the "no war" news, climbed back above $4,050 as investors recalibrated rather than abandoned their hedges.

Underneath the Iran-driven relief rally, though, sits a genuinely hawkish shift in Fed expectations: CME FedWatch now prices a 64.5% chance of a rate hike at September's meeting, a sharp change from the hold-dominated odds that prevailed through most of July. With no FOMC meeting until then, this week's nonfarm payrolls report and ISM manufacturing and services readings will carry outsized weight in confirming or undercutting that repricing—on top of whatever Monday's Iran talks (or lack thereof) do to oil prices and, by extension, inflation expectations.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

Trump's comparison point—"the biggest attack since World War II"—is a striking one given that the most destructive single bombing raids in history, including the March 1945 firebombing of Tokyo, killed more people in one night than most modern conflicts do in months; whether the phrase was meant literally or as rhetorical shorthand for scale, it set a bar that any actual 21st-century strike campaign would struggle to clear in raw destructive terms, even with modern precision-guided weapons.

Word of the Day
stand-down

(noun, military in origin, from the verb phrase "to stand down") — an order or decision to cease preparation for imminent action, typically after forces or plans have already been mobilized. A stand-down is distinct from a cancellation in one important way: it implies the capability and intent remain in place, ready to resume, rather than having been abandoned—exactly the ambiguity markets are pricing into Monday's relief rally.
