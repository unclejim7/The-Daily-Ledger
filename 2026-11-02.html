Midterms · Markets · Monday Edition

On the Eve of the Midterms, Markets Attempt to Stabilize as Ras Tanura's Silence Enters a Second Week

More than 35 million ballots have already been cast ahead of tomorrow's midterm elections, with control of Congress for the remainder of the term resting on a handful of genuine toss-up races, including a tight Iowa Senate contest. Closing-weekend campaigning turned sharp: at a Cleveland rally for Senate candidate Sherrod Brown, former President Barack Obama told the crowd, "After two years of chaos and cruelty and corruption and incompetence, the likes of which we have not seen in the modern era, you get a chance to say, 'Enough is enough.'" President Trump fired back from the campaign trail in Ohio.

Markets opened the week attempting to stabilize after Friday's rout, with the S&P 500 rising 0.4% to 7,610.87, the Dow adding 0.4% to 51,977.64, and the Nasdaq gaining 0.5% to 25,564.62. Historically, major indexes have tended to rally in the twelve months following a midterm election regardless of the outcome, and traders appear to be leaning on that pattern for now rather than pricing in fresh election-specific risk.

Brent crude held near $117 a barrel, with no new incident reported overnight in the Strait of Hormuz. Saudi Arabia has now gone seven days without confirming or denying the Houthi claim of a strike on the Ras Tanura refinery, a silence that has now outlasted the entire stretch between the original claim and tomorrow's election.

Bitcoin continued its slow climb to roughly $77,800, and the 30-year mortgage holds at 7.34% ahead of Thursday's new reading. In sports, the Bears close out their bye week tonight at Seattle, and Milwaukee has finally set a date for its long-awaited World Series victory parade: this Saturday.

Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Cutting Ultra-Processed Food Intake Linked to Improved Depression Symptoms

A small pilot trial published in JAMA Psychiatry found that adults with depression who cut their ultra-processed food intake by more than 85% showed reduced depression and anxiety symptoms. Researchers floated gut health as a possible mechanism but stressed the study’s small sample size means the findings are preliminary; a larger UK Biobank cohort study found only certain categories of ultra-processed food, not all of them, were associated with higher rates of depression, suggesting the relationship is more nuanced than a single blanket warning about processed food.
