Markets · Federal Reserve · Friday Edition

Hot CPI Cements Rate-Hike Odds at 64% the Same Week Iran Launches Its Largest Wave of Hormuz Shipping Attacks Yet

Friday's August CPI report gave the Federal Reserve exactly the kind of data it didn't want to see three business days before its policy decision. Consumer prices rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4% for the month, putting the 12-month increase at 3.4%; core CPI, which strips out food and energy, posted a 0.3% monthly gain — a tenth of a point above forecast — with the annual core rate holding at 2.4%, in line with estimates but still well above the Fed's 2% target. As one analyst put it, Fed Chair Warsh and others had signaled rates could stay on hold only if disinflation continued, and Friday's report simply didn't deliver that. Rate-hike odds for next Wednesday's FOMC decision have climbed to 64%, with Nationwide among the forecasters now expecting a quarter-point increase.

The inflation data landed the same week Iran carried out what Reuters described as the largest declared wave of tit-for-tat shipping attacks by either side since the war began. Iran said it struck 10 vessels near the Strait of Hormuz — two U.S. ships and eight oil tankers — while also firing ballistic missiles at a U.S.-linked base in Jordan. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency confirmed several merchant vessels in the northern Gulf and Gulf of Oman were hit by disabling fire overnight, though it could not confirm casualties or environmental impact; CENTCOM, for its part, said no American vessel was struck and that every IRGC attempt against U.S. forces failed.

Oil markets registered the escalation unambiguously. Brent crude surged to roughly $106.83 a barrel Thursday, up 5.6% on the day and its highest level since May 19, while WTI pushed past $100 as well. The combined shock of hot inflation data and a genuine energy-price spike drove long-dated Treasury yields to multi-year highs and pulled equities lower for a fourth consecutive session: the Dow fell 316.56 points (-0.6%) to 52,064.10, the S&P 500 dropped 0.58% to 7,591.70, and the Nasdaq eased 0.65% to 26,081.72. Freddie Mac's weekly survey, released the same day, showed the 30-year mortgage rate jumping to 6.76%, its highest level in more than 14 months.

Bitcoin slid to around $76,900 and gold fell to roughly $4,326 an ounce as the week's combined pressures — rising yields, surging oil, and now confirmed hot inflation — left few assets untouched. With CPI now in hand and the Fed's decision just three trading days away, markets head into the weekend having absorbed nearly every major piece of information they'll get before Wednesday's rate call, save for whatever the war itself produces between now and then.

A Tenth of a Point That Mattered

Core CPI's Small Beat Carries Outsized Weight

A 0.3% core monthly print, one-tenth above forecast, wouldn't ordinarily reshape a rate decision on its own — but arriving alongside a genuine oil-price shock and a jobs report that already ran hot, it was enough to tip the odds meaningfully toward a hike.

Escalation by Volume

Ten Vessels Is a Scale Shift, Not Just Another Incident

Reuters explicitly framing this as the largest wave of shipping attacks yet suggests both sides have moved past isolated tanker incidents into something closer to a sustained, high-volume campaign against Hormuz traffic.

Confirmed vs. Contested

UKMTO's Independent Confirmation Matters Here

Unlike many of this war's disputed naval claims, UKMTO's confirmation that several vessels were hit by disabling fire comes from a neutral maritime safety body rather than either combatant — giving this particular incident more credibility than most.

A Genuine Squeeze

Borrowers Are Feeling This Week Directly

A 14-month-high mortgage rate isn't an abstract market statistic — it's a real, immediate cost increase for anyone shopping for a home loan this week, layered directly on top of already-record fuel prices.

Geopolitics

A Widening Definition of "Target"

Two US Ships Now Explicitly Named Among Iran's Claimed Strikes

Iran's own accounting of the attack specifically separates "two U.S. ships" from "eight oil tankers," a level of claimed specificity about targeting American vessels directly that marks a rhetorical escalation even before CENTCOM's denial is weighed.

The Denial That Matters

CENTCOM's "Every Attempt Failed" Is a Strong Claim Too

A flat denial that any American vessel was struck, paired with UKMTO's independent confirmation that other ships were hit, suggests this incident is real and significant even if the exact American-vessel dimension of Iran's claim doesn't hold up.

Armstrong Economics

Armstrong: Inflation Data and War Escalation Rarely Arrive on Convenient Schedules

Armstrong Economics has long argued that policymakers rarely get the luxury of separating monetary decisions from geopolitical shocks, and this week is close to a textbook case: a Fed board now walking into a hike decision with an active, escalating war actively feeding the inflation numbers it's trying to interpret. The site's Panic Cycle framework treats this kind of convergence — policy and conflict pressures peaking simultaneously — as exactly the condition under which markets tend to overreact in both directions.

The Daily Triptych

Random Fact

The Consumer Price Index tracks a fixed "basket" of hundreds of specific goods and services, reweighted periodically to reflect actual spending patterns — meaning a single hot monthly report, like Friday's, reflects thousands of individual price changes compressed into one closely watched number.

Word of the Day
disinflation

(noun, from "dis-" + inflation) — a slowing in the rate of price increases, distinct from deflation (falling prices outright). The Fed wasn't hoping prices would fall this month, only that they'd rise more slowly than before — and Friday's report shows they didn't even manage that.
