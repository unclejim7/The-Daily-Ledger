Geopolitics · Markets · Tuesday Edition

Tankers Struck in the Exact Hormuz Corridor Iran and Oman Were Negotiating, as Iran's Six Public Conditions Remain Unmet and Treasury Yields Hit a 20-Month High

Overnight Monday, unknown projectiles — widely attributed to Iranian anti-ship cruise missiles — struck two oil tankers, one Saudi-flagged and one South Korean-owned, within minutes of each other roughly 17 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman. The location isn't incidental: the strikes landed directly along the U.S. Navy's so-called "southern corridor" out of the Gulf, the exact route Iran and Oman spent last week negotiating as the centerpiece of their proposed temporary shipping corridor. UKMTO confirmed the incident, the fourth such tanker attack in recent days, with no casualties or environmental damage reported as an investigation continues.

The attacks land alongside a reminder of just how far apart Washington and Tehran remain on paper, whatever the state of any given week's diplomacy. Iran's Supreme National Security Council, under Mohammad Baqer Zolghadr, published six explicit conditions for reopening the strait back in August: an end to threats against Iran, a permanent end to hostilities against Iran and its regional allies, a lifted naval blockade and full U.S. troop withdrawal from the area, full compensation for "two wars of aggression," and the lifting of sanctions alongside the release of frozen Iranian assets. None has been met. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, speaking Tuesday at the Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit in Bishkek, said Iran would "immediately reciprocate" if Washington returns to its commitments under the Islamabad memorandum of understanding — a claim that, like so much else in this war, has no obvious path to verification from the outside.

Markets, meanwhile, are increasingly pricing a genuinely different problem: the bond market. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.78% Tuesday, its highest intraday level since January 2025, extending the rise that began with Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks and has now been reinforced by a full week of renewed Hormuz violence adding its own geopolitical risk premium. Monday's cash session closed lower across the board (S&P 500 −0.33% to 7,686.14, Dow −0.7% to 53,185.90, Nasdaq −0.12% to 26,370.89), even as all three indexes still closed out August with a monthly gain. Tuesday's futures pointed to a rougher open still: Dow futures down roughly 300 points, Nasdaq futures off more than 1%, with tech names including Nvidia, AMD, and Micron all slipping more than 1% in premarket trading.

Oil is the one asset behaving exactly as the headlines would suggest: Brent climbed above $92 a barrel and WTI traded up toward $88, both extending gains on the renewed strait violence. Bitcoin and gold, by contrast, continue sliding together on the same rising-rate story that's pressured them since Warsh's speech — Bitcoin down to around $77,950, and gold falling below $4,450 an ounce, extending Monday's sharp drop.

A clinical trial involving people with obesity, prediabetes, and fatty liver disease found that a ketogenic diet outperformed both Mediterranean and low-fat diets across several measures of metabolic health.
