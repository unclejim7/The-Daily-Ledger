Health, Wellness & Fitness

Study: Plant-Forward Diet Pattern Linked to 15-17% Lower Type 2 Diabetes Risk in Large UK Cohort

Researchers analyzing more than 155,000 UK Biobank participants without type 2 diabetes at the study's start found that those most closely following an EAT-Lancet-style "planetary health" diet — emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and plant-based proteins over red and processed meats — had a 15 to 17% lower relative risk of developing type 2 diabetes over an average follow-up of more than eleven years, compared with those adhering least closely to the pattern. Of the more than 155,000 participants tracked, 7,626 went on to develop the condition. The study also found that higher "biological age" relative to a person's actual age was linked to greater diabetes risk. Researchers caution the study is observational, cannot prove the diet itself caused the lower risk, and drew primarily from a largely white, UK-based population, so the findings may not generalize to all groups.

Random Fact

The EAT-Lancet Commission's 2019 "planetary health diet" was designed jointly to address human nutrition and environmental sustainability, aiming to roughly double consumption of foods like fruits, vegetables, and legumes worldwide while halving red meat and added sugar intake.

Word of the Day
biological age

(noun, distinguished from "chronological age") — a measure of how old a person's cells, tissues, and organ systems appear to be functioning, based on biomarkers, as opposed to the number of years since birth. Today's featured diabetes study found this measure linked to disease risk independent of chronological age alone.
