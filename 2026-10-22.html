Geopolitics · Iran · Thursday Edition

Iran Claims Seven Tankers Struck in a Single Day as Oil Surges Past $113, Erasing This Week's Market Relief

Iranian state television reported that seven oil tankers were struck or hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz overnight — by Tehran's own account, the most vessels damaged in a single day since the war began in February. The Associated Press could not independently confirm the claim, continuing the familiar pattern of unverifiable Iranian incident reports. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi offered a conditional path forward: “If they comply with our conditions, the Strait of Hormuz will reopen on the sixth day, and negotiations will begin on the seventh day.”

Brent crude surged 4% to $113.17 a barrel, its highest level since May, as the claim rattled energy markets already on edge after weeks of sustained disruption. The relief rally that carried markets through much of this week reversed sharply: the Nasdaq posted its worst session since June, and the S&P 500 its worst since July, as the oil spike revived every inflation worry the market had just started to set aside.

Bitcoin fell back below the $84,200 level it had only just reclaimed Wednesday, sliding to roughly $81,800 amid the broader risk-off mood — a reminder, as this edition noted just yesterday, that one day above a key level isn't the same as holding it. Fed rate-hike odds for the October 27–28 meeting held steady near 16% despite the oil shock, suggesting markets still don't expect a single day of geopolitical escalation to change the Fed's calculus.

Freddie Mac's new weekly mortgage reading, meanwhile, ticked down to 7.38% — its first decline in weeks. The World Series opens tomorrow night at Milwaukee, with the Brewers' Jacob Misiorowski facing the Rays' Nick Martinez and Milwaukee favored at home.

Personalized mRNA Melanoma Vaccine Plus Keytruda Cuts Recurrence Risk by 42%

New data presented at a major European oncology conference found that a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine, developed by Moderna and Merck and combined with the immunotherapy drug Keytruda, cut the risk of melanoma recurrence by 42% compared with standard treatment alone. The vaccine is tailored to each patient's specific tumor mutations, and researchers say the results support its potential as a meaningful addition to post-surgical melanoma treatment, though broader approval and longer-term outcome data are still pending.
